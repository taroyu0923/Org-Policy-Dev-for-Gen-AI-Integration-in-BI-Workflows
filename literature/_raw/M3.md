### 1. WHY — Research Problem & Motivation
* **Motivation & Context:** The study was prompted by a South Australian policy mandating nurses to complete a signed self-declaration of competence for annual registration, where official guidelines recommended using performance feedback to inform self-assessment despite a lack of empirical research supporting its utility [1, 2].
* **Methodological Problem:** Within qualitative interpretive research, investigators face the challenge of establishing scientific rigor, credibility, and auditability without compromising subjective participant perspectives [3-5].
* **Objective:** The authors sought to demonstrate how a step-by-step hybrid thematic analysis provides a transparent evidence trail showing how raw focus group and document data are systematically transformed into overarching interpretive themes [5-7].
* **Location:** Section "Introduction" (pp. 1–2) & "Demonstrating rigor within the framework of social phenomenology" (pp. 2–3) [1, 2, 4, 5, 7].

### 2. HOW — Method / Design
* **Study Type:** Qualitative, interpretive exploratory study grounded philosophically and methodologically in Schutz’s social phenomenology [6-8].
* **Data Sources:** Semi-structured focus group transcripts and organizational policy/procedure documents [7, 9, 10].
* **Sample / Corpus Size:** 10 focus groups (comprising nursing clinicians and clinical managers) and 16 organizational policy/procedure documents [9].
* **Databases Searched:** None (the study analyzed primary qualitative empirical data and organizational documents collected directly from healthcare organizations; QSR NVivo software was utilized for qualitative data management) [7, 9, 11].
* **Time Span:** Data collection context draws on policy requirements introduced by the Nurses Board of South Australia in 2000; empirical collection occurred prior to doctoral analysis [1, 2, 9].
* **Location:** Section "Introduction" (p. 2), "Demonstrating rigor through a process of thematic analysis" (p. 4), and "Stage 4: Applying template of codes and additional coding" (p. 8) [7, 9, 11].

### 3. WHAT — Key Findings, Frameworks, & Typologies Proposed
* **Philosophical Methodology:** Adopted Alfred Schutz's social phenomenology framework (first- and second-order *verstehen* constructs) governed by three postulates of rigor: *logical consistency*, *subjective interpretation*, and *adequacy* [3, 12-14]. Location: "The philosophical framework of social phenomenology" & "Demonstrating rigor within the framework of social phenomenology" (pp. 2–3) [3, 13].
* **Six-Stage Hybrid Thematic Analysis Framework:** Formulated a structured 6-stage qualitative analysis protocol integrating Crabtree & Miller's (1999) deductive template coding with Boyatzis's (1998) inductive data-driven coding:
  1. *Stage 1: Developing the code manual* (establishing *a priori* theoretical codes) [15, 16];
  2. *Stage 2: Testing the reliability of the code* (inter-coder testing on sample data) [17, 18];
  3. *Stage 3: Summarizing data and identifying initial themes* (paraphrasing raw transcripts under trigger questions) [19, 20];
  4. *Stage 4: Applying template of codes and additional coding* (deductive coding in NVivo + inductive coding of emergent data) [11, 21, 22];
  5. *Stage 5: Connecting the codes and identifying themes* (discovering patterns across data sets) [23];
  6. *Stage 6: Corroborating and legitimating coded themes* (scrutinizing iterations to finalize second-order core themes) [24-26].
  *Location:* "Demonstrating rigor through a process of thematic analysis" & "Stages of data coding" (pp. 4–11, Figure 1) [9, 15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 24, 27].
* **Empirical Core Typology on Performance Feedback:** Identified three core themes governing feedback credibility, centered on *Familiarity* (relational closeness, trust, and mutual respect between source and recipient), while noting that excessive familiarity can degrade feedback integrity [26, 28, 29]. Location: "Stage 6: Corroborating and legitimating coded themes" (pp. 11–12, Table 7) [26, 28, 29].
