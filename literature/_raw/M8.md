### **1. WHY — Research Problem & Motivation**
* **Research Problem:** Qualitative health research lacks standardized tools like quantitative power calculations to evaluate sample size [1]. Researchers frequently cite the concept of **"saturation"** without defining how it was assessed or acknowledging that saturation originated specifically as a tool for Grounded Theory (GT) analysis, leading to low transparency and uncritical application in non-GT studies [2, 3].
* **Motivation:** General sampling principles (e.g., that sample size should be sufficiently large to elucidate aims) offer little practical guidance during study planning [3]. The authors motivate the need for **"information power"** as a pragmatic, shared methodological tool to guide initial sample size planning and continuous appraisal during data collection without relying on rigid formulas or GT-bound redundancy [1, 4, 5].
* **Location:** Section: **Background** (pp. 1753–1754) [1-5].

### **3. WHAT — Key Findings & Proposed Framework**
The authors propose the **Information Power Model**, which identifies five core methodological items operating along continuums to determine sample size adequacy [11, 12]:

1. **Study Aim (Narrow vs. Broad):** A narrow aim requires a smaller sample size (\\(N\\)), whereas a broad aim requires a larger \\(N\\) [12, 13].
2. **Sample Specificity (Dense vs. Sparse):** Participants holding characteristics highly specific to the study aim require a smaller \\(N\\) than a sample with sparse specificity [12, 14].
3. **Established Theory (Applied vs. None):** A study supported by established theoretical frameworks requires a smaller \\(N\\) than an atheoretical study starting from scratch [12, 15].
4. **Quality of Dialogue (Strong vs. Weak):** Strong, clear communication and skilled interviewing yield higher information power, requiring a smaller \\(N\\) [12, 16].
5. **Analysis Strategy (Case vs. Cross-Case):** In-depth analysis of specific narratives/cases requires fewer participants than broad exploratory thematic cross-case analysis [12, 17].

* **Core Principle:** Sample size should be evaluated dynamically and stepwise during planning and data collection based on the information the sample holds relative to the study aim, rather than relying on pre-determined mathematical formulas or redundancy claims [4, 10, 18].
* **Location:** Section: **Items Having an Impact on Information Power** (pp. 1754–1756), Section: **Information Power in Qualitative Interview Studies—The Model** (pp. 1756–1757), & Section: **Implications for Research Practice** (p. 1760) [10-18].
