### 1. WHY — Research Problem and Motivation
Graduate students and novice researchers frequently misunderstand qualitative case study methodology, often misperceiving it as limited to individual clinical studies, historical event reviews, or simple teaching tools [1]. To address this knowledge gap, the authors provide a practical, step-by-step guide designed to help novice researchers rigorously design, bound, and execute qualitative case study projects using established methodological frameworks [1-3].
* **Location:** "Abstract" (p. 544) and "Introduction" (p. 545) [1, 2].

### 3. WHAT — Key Frameworks and Typologies Proposed
* **Paradigm Foundations:** Grounds case study methodology in constructivism, comparing Yin's structured, post-positivist approach with Stake's subjective, interpretive approach ("Philosophical Underpinnings", p. 545) [4, 12].
* **Case Study Typology:** Categorizes designs by purpose into explanatory, exploratory, and descriptive (Yin) versus intrinsic, instrumental, and collective (Stake) ("Determining the Type of Case Study", pp. 547–549) [13-19].
* **Structural Design Typology:** Differentiates between single holistic cases, single cases with embedded sub-units, and multiple/collective cases ("Single or Multiple Case Study Designs", pp. 549–550) [19, 20].
* **Case Bounding Mechanisms:** Outlines three primary strategies to bind a case: by time/place, time/activity, or definition/context ("Binding the Case", pp. 546–547) [21, 22].
* **Guidance via Propositions/Issues:** Highlights using theoretical/empirical propositions (Yin) or contextual issues (Stake) to narrow data collection and anchor analysis ("Propositions", p. 551; "Issues", p. 552) [23-25].
* **Conceptual Framework Integration:** Recommends developing an evolving conceptual framework to categorize constructs and anchor data interpretation ("Conceptual Framework", p. 553) [26, 27].
* **Data Convergence Principle:** Stresses converging multiple data sources during analysis to illuminate the overall case rather than reporting sources independently ("Data Sources", p. 554; "Analysis", p. 555) [5, 28].
* **Trustworthiness Framework:** Synthesizes rigor strategies including source triangulation, prolonged engagement, member checking, double coding, and audit trails ("Strategies for Achieving Trustworthiness in Case Study Research", pp. 555–556) [29, 30].
