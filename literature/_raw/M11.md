### 1. WHY — Research Problem and Motivation

* **Purpose:** The paper highlights the unique opportunities of case-based inductive research, addresses common methodological challenges, and provides actionable antidotes for authors [2].

---

### 3. WHAT — Key Proposals / Framework
* **Justifying Inductive Research:** Demonstrate that existing theory is inadequate and show that the question requires "how" or "why" inductive logic rather than quantitative frequency testing (Section *Justifying Theory Building*, pp. 25–26) [5, 6].
* **Theoretical Sampling:** Sample cases for theoretical suitability (e.g., polar types, unusual revelation) using replication logic rather than random/representative selection (Section *Theoretical Sampling of Cases*, pp. 26–27) [7-9].
* **Mitigating Informant Bias:** Mitigate retrospective sensemaking and impression management by interviewing diverse, highly knowledgeable informants across hierarchical levels and combining real-time with retrospective cases (Section *Dealing with Interview Data*, p. 27) [10-12].
* **Presenting Empirical Evidence:** Combine selective narrative descriptions with structured "construct tables" and visual summary aids to demonstrate deep empirical grounding within spatial limits (Section *Presenting Empirical Evidence*, pp. 27–28) [13-15].
* **Writing Emergent Theory:** Frame theory through clear propositions backed by explicit logical arguments, pattern-matching across cases, and an overarching theoretical frame (Section *Writing the Emergent Theory*, p. 28) [16].
* **Navigating Terminology & Parsimony:** Avoid ambiguous labels (e.g., "qualitative research", "grounded theory"), and leverage multiple cases to construct parsimonious, robust, and generalizable theory (Sections *Dealing with Interview Data*, p. 27; *Writing the Emergent Theory*, pp. 29–30) [17-20].
