### 1. WHY — Research Problem & Motivation
The paper addresses important gaps in organizational theory caused by neglecting how micro-level human interaction, talk, and meaning-making generate macro-level organizational order [1, 2]. Its motivation is to take stock of the sensemaking concept and restate it to be more action-oriented, future-oriented, macro-level, and tightly integrated with organizing and identity [1, 3]. The authors argue that coordination and order emerge continuously from how individuals notice cues, talk, and act under ongoing ambiguity [1, 4, 5].  
*(Location: Introductory text, pp. 409–410 [1-5])*

### 3. WHAT — Key Findings, Frameworks, & Typologies Proposed
* **8 Descriptive Properties of Sensemaking:** Sensemaking unfolds as a continuous sequence that organizes flux, begins with noticing and bracketing, depends on labeling, is retrospective, is guided by presumption, is social/systemic, is action-oriented, and is realized through communication and articulation *(Location: "The Nature of Organized Sensemaking: Viewed Descriptively", pp. 410–413 [12-19])*.
* **Intraorganizational Evolutionary Model (ESR Framework):** Maps sensemaking onto Donald Campbell’s evolutionary sequence (Ecological Change \\(\rightarrow\\) Enactment \\(\rightarrow\\) Selection \\(\rightarrow\\) Retention), where retained plausible interpretations feed back into future enactments *(Location: "Sensemaking as Intraorganizational Evolution", pp. 413–414, Figure 1, p. 414 [32–34])*.
* **Instigations to Sensemaking Typology:** Identifies that sensemaking is triggered when expectations of continuity are breached by discrepancies, breakdowns, disconfirmations, or surprises under conditions of ambiguity or novelty *(Location: "Instigations to Sensemaking", pp. 414–415 [20])*.
* **Plausibility over Accuracy Principle:** Demonstrates that sensemaking is driven by plausible, pragmatic, and actionable stories rather than absolute perceptual accuracy *(Location: "Plausibility and Sensemaking", pp. 415–416 [21-23])*.
* **Identity Construction & Sensegiving Dynamic:** Shows that organizational identity ("who we think we are") filters how actors interpret cues, while sensegiving actively shapes internal and external understandings *(Location: "Identity and Sensemaking", pp. 416–417 [24-26])*.
* **Prospective Research Agenda:** Proposes multi-level linkages connecting micro-sensemaking to institutional theory, distributed cognition/systems, power dynamics, and emotional arousal *(Location: "The Nature of Organized Sensemaking: Viewed Prospectively", pp. 417–420 [11, 27-30])*.

#### (a) Verbatim Definitions of Sensemaking

* *"In this context sensemaking means basically 'inventing a new meaning (interpretation) for something that has already occurred during the organizing process, but does not yet have a name... has never been recognized as a separate autonomous process, object, event' (Magala 1997, p. 324)."* (Section: **Sensemaking Starts with Noticing and Bracketing**, p. 411) [10]

#### (b) Main Claims and Propositions
* **Sensemaking and Action Interplay (Quote):** *"Sensemaking is about the interplay of action and interpretation rather than the influence of evaluation on choice. When action is the central focus, interpretation, not choice, is the core phenomenon"* (Section: **Opening text**, p. 410) [7, 40].
* **Mutual Constitution of Organizing and Sensemaking (Quote):** *"[S]ensemaking and organization constitute one another: 'Organization is an attempt to order the intrinsic flux of human action, to channel it toward certain ends, to give it a particular shape, through generalizing and institutionalizing particular meanings and rules' (Tsoukas and Chia 2002, p. 570)... The operative image of organization is one in which organization emerges through sensemaking, not one in which organization precedes sensemaking or one in which sensemaking is produced by organization."* (Section: **The Nature of Organized Sensemaking: Viewed Descriptively**, pp. 410–411) [34].
* **Order via Equivocal Inputs (Quote):** *"[P]eople organize to make sense of equivocal inputs and enact this sense back into the world to make that world more orderly."* (Section: **The Nature of Organized Sensemaking: Viewed Descriptively**, p. 411; **Sensemaking as Intraorganizational Evolution**, p. 414) [24, 34].

#### (c) Mechanisms, Conditions, and Antecedents
* **Antecedents / Conditions:**

* **Mechanisms:**
  * **Noticing and Bracketing:** Interruption prompts actors to carve out specific cues from the aboriginal flux of experience using mental models [15–17]. (Section: **Sensemaking Starts with Noticing and Bracketing**, p. 411) [10, 41]
  * **Labeling and Categorizing:** Functional deployment of socially defined cognitive categories to stabilize experience and establish recurring behavioral common ground [11, 35]. (Section: **Sensemaking Is About Labeling**, p. 411) [11, 35]
  * **Retrospection:** Constructing meaning after the fact by looking backward across past observations ("how can I know what I'm seeing until I see what it was") [42, 43]. (Section: **Sensemaking Is Retrospective**, pp. 411–412) [42, 43]
  * **Presumption & Action:** Acting "as if" a hunch is true to generate new data and test interpretations through progressive approximations [15-18]. (Section: **Sensemaking Is About Presumption**, p. 412; **Sensemaking Is About Action**, pp. 412–413) [15-18]
  * **Communication & Articulation:** Social interactive talk that lifts tacit understanding into explicit, public, and actionable form [20, 44]. (Section: **Sensemaking Is About Organizing Through Communication**, p. 413) [20, 44]
  * **Identity Filtering:** Self-conceptions and external image perceptions filter what cues are extracted and how meanings are constructed [25-27]. (Section: **Identity and Sensemaking**, pp. 416–417) [25-27]
  * **Power Structuring:** Powerful actors influence reality construction by controlling cues, permitted actions, and standards of plausibility [28, 29]. (Section: **Sensemaking and Power**, p. 418) [28, 29]
