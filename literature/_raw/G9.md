### **ENGLISH VERSION**

#### **1. WHY — Research Problem and Motivation**
As organizations transition from integrated hierarchies to modular, decentralized forms, reduced contact between senior executives and middle managers creates cognitive disorder, raising the question of how middle managers resolve this ambiguity to operationalize top-down designs [1]. Prior change research predominantly focuses on senior executive leadership, leaving a significant gap in understanding how change recipients interpret structural initiatives and reorient their cognitive schemata in the absence of direct executive oversight [2-4]. This study addresses this gap by investigating how middle manager sensemaking and horizontal peer interactions shape organizational schema development and final transition outcomes [2, 5, 6].

---

#### **2. HOW — Method / Design**
* **Type of Study:** Longitudinal, qualitative, interpretive case study [7].
* **Research Setting & Time Span:** A privatized UK utility company undergoing strategic restructuring across three new divisions (Core, Engineering, Services) tracked during implementation from August 1993 to July 1994 (with initial data collection from April 1993) [7-10].
* **Sample Size:** 26 middle managers (diarists) out of a pool of ~90 middle managers across the three divisions [11].
* **Data Corpus (1,485 total transcribed pages):**
  * **Solicited Diaries:** 242 pages of verbatim diary logs [11, 12].
  * **1-on-1 In-Depth Interviews:** Round 1 (Oct 1993, 353 pages) and Round 2 (Apr–May 1994, 538 pages) [12, 13].
  * **Focus Groups:** 5 post-interview focus groups (Apr–Jul 1994, 170 pages) [12, 14].
  * **Observational Notes & Documents:** 182 pages of meeting/observation notes plus senior management change briefings [12, 14].
* **Analysis Tool/Database:** NUD.IST qualitative text analysis software for tree-based coding and grounded theme identification [15].

---

#### **3. WHAT — Key Findings and Frameworks Proposed**
* **Replacement Sequence Model of Schema Change:** Imposed restructuring forces a sudden replacement of old hierarchical schemata ("organization as hierarchy — common purpose") with new multidivisional schemata, evolving from "clustered sensemaking" to "shared yet differentiated sensemaking" [5, 16-20].
* **Primacy of Lateral Peer Sensemaking:** In the absence of daily executive direction ("senior managers as ghosts"), middle managers rely primarily on horizontal peer conversations, rumors, stories, and personal experiences to negotiate operational meaning [21-23].
* **Multi-Mechanism Schema Dynamics:** Demonstrates that schema evolution combines conversion (sudden structural replacement), bookkeeping (incremental fine-tuning), and conflict (dialectical peer negotiation) models [20, 24-26].
* **Residual Conformity ("Failed Relocation"):** While structural relationships were replaced, ingrained cultural control-blame schemata persisted because executive actions continually reinforced old management styles [27-31].
* **Active Construction of Transition Reality:** Change recipients actively construct change outcomes through local social interaction, frequently leading to operational results (e.g., interdivisional friction and delayed service response) unintended by executive designers [21, 22, 32].
