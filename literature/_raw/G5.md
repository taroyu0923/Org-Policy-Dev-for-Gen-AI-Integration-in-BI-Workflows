  > *"Sensemaking is triggered by cues—such as issues, events, or situations—for which the meaning is ambiguous and/or outcomes uncertain. Prospective or future-oriented sensemaking constructs interpretations and projections of future states [5, 56, 57]. Scholars debate whether prospective sensemaking is distinct or operates via "future perfect" retrospective thinking (envisioning a future state and acting as if it has already transpired to interpret it retrospectively) [56].

#### (c) Mechanisms, conditions, or antecedents
* **Conditions Making Sensemaking MORE Likely**:
  * Discrepancies, surprises, or violations of expectations that interrupt ongoing activity [6, 11].
  * Threats to individual or organizational identity and associated intense negative emotions or anxiety [12, 15, 60, 62].
  * Environmental jolts, crises, or planned change initiatives that disrupt taken-for-granted routines [7, 63-65].
  * Contexts with high ambiguity, slack time, autonomy, and psychological safety [52, 66].
  * *Location*: Section: **"How do events become triggers..."** (pp. 69–76) [6, 7, 12, 64, 65]; Section: **"Learning"** (pp. 90–91) [52, 66].
* **Conditions Making Sensemaking LESS Likely (Deterrents/Buffers)**:
  * Strong positive individual or organizational identities that buffer members from external cues or lead them to discount contradictory data [67-69].
  * Normalization of deviance or cultural practices ("positive asymmetry") that accommodate, normalize, or explain away anomalous cues [6, 70, 71].
  * Routines, systems, or cultures that reduce mindfulness or prioritize production targets over attending to weak cues [6].
  * "Sticky prior accounts" or residual sensegiving from previous change initiatives that prime members against new interpretations [72, 73].
  * *Location*: Section: **"How do events become triggers..."** (pp. 69–76) [6, 67-71, 73].
