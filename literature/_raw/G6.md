### **2. WHY — Research Problem and Motivation**
**Organizational change had received extensive attention, but the distinctive nature of *strategic change* and the CEO's role during its critical initiation phase remained significantly under-studied** [4]. Traditional literature portrayed CEOs strictly through top-down strategy formulation and implementation, overlooking the rich symbolic, interpretive, and social construction processes needed to launch strategic change [3, 5]. The study addresses this gap by proposing a grounded, interpretive model of how strategic change is instigated [6].
* **Location**: Introduction (pp. 433–434) [4-6].

---

### **3. HOW — Method and Research Design**
* **Type of Study**: Single-site qualitative ethnographic study using participant-observation paired with a dual "insider/outsider" grounded research design [7-9].
* **Sample / Context**: A large, public, multi-campus university undergoing strategic change under a newly appointed President [7, 10].
* **Time Span**: 2.5 years of total on-site data collection, tracking 12 months (1 year) of strategic change initiation across four distinct phases [11, 12].
* **Databases / Data Sources**: Primary data comprised tape-recorded ethnographic interview transcripts with key leaders (President/CEO, Executive VP/COO, Deans), ethnographer diaries, personal observations, and validation by a "hold-out" informant; secondary data included internal memos, confidential reports, position papers, and feasibility studies (no commercial databases were used) [13-15].
* **Location**: Ground Assumptions and Methodology (pp. 435–437) [8, 10, 13-15].

---

### **4. WHAT — Key Findings and Proposed Frameworks**
* **Four-Phase Temporal Framework**: Strategic change initiation unfolds sequentially across four temporal phases: Envisioning, Signaling, Re-Visioning, and Energizing [FIRST ORDER FINDINGS – Strategic change initiation phases, pp. 438–441] [16].
* **Sensemaking and Sensegiving Process Cycle**: Strategic change initiation operates through an iterative, reciprocal cycle where leaders construct meaning for themselves (sensemaking) and attempt to influence stakeholders (sensegiving), who in turn interpret the vision and feed back their own sensemaking/sensegiving [SECOND-ORDER FINDINGS, pp. 442–444] [17-19].
* **Tactical Ambiguity-by-Design**: Executives intentionally introduce ambiguity to disrupt entrenched status-quo interpretive schemes and foster fluidity without requiring an organizational crisis [FIRST ORDER FINDINGS – The Signaling Phase, p. 439; DISCUSSION AND IMPLICATIONS – Implications for a wider domain, p. 446] [20-22].
* **Instrumental Role of Symbolic Leadership**: CEO leadership in strategic change relies on evocative symbolic imagery (e.g., "Top 10 public university") and symbolic actions to mobilize collective effort and reframe organizational identity [DISCUSSION AND IMPLICATIONS – Implications for academic organizations, p. 445] [23-25].
* **Negotiated Social Construction**: Strategic change is a negotiated reality shaped by stakeholder resistance, consultation, and mutual influence rather than top-down executive decree [INSTIGATING STRATEGIC CHANGE: A GROUNDED OVERVIEW, p. 434; DISCUSSION AND IMPLICATIONS – Implications for a wider domain, pp. 446–447] [26-28].

---

### **5. DEFINITION — Core Constructs**
* **Strategic Change**:
  > *"strategic change involves an attempt to change current modes of cognition and action to enable the organization to take advantage of important opportunities or to cope with consequential environmental threats."* [Introduction, p. 433] [4]
* **Sensemaking**:
  > *"'sensemaking' has to do with meaning construction and reconstruction by the involved parties as they attempted to develop a meaningful framework for understanding the nature of the intended strategic change."* [SECOND-ORDER FINDINGS – Sensemaking, p. 442] [17]
* **Sensegiving**:
  > *"'Sensegiving' is concerned with the process of attempting to influence the sensemaking and meaning construction of others toward a preferred redefinition of organizational reality."* [SECOND-ORDER FINDINGS – Sensegiving, p. 442] [17]

* **Negotiated Reality**: Because non-crisis change cannot be executed by simple decree, leadership must expend political goodwill and engage in reciprocal negotiation, accepting a slower pace or policy adaptations to secure commitment [10, 43, 47, 48].
