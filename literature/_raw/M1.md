### 1. WHY — Research Problem and Motivation
* **Research Problem:** Despite being widely utilized across qualitative research, thematic analysis historically suffered from being **"poorly demarcated and rarely acknowledged"** [1]. Researchers frequently failed to clarify their theoretical assumptions or specific analytic procedures, often describing themes as passively "emerging" from data without explaining how coding was conducted [1-3].
* **Motivation:** The authors aimed to fill an essential gap by providing an **accessible, rigorous, and systematic guide** to the theory, application, and evaluation of thematic analysis [1, 4]. They position thematic analysis as a **foundational method in its own right**—rather than a mere tool embedded in other traditions—to equip students and researchers with clear methodological vocabulary and standards [4-6].
* **Location:** Section "Introduction", pp. 77–80 [1-6].

### 2. HOW — Method / Design
* **Type of Study:** Theoretical paper and practical methodological guide/tutorial [1, 4, 7].
* **Data / Sample / Corpus Size:** As a methodological framework paper, it does not collect or analyze an empirical dataset of its own [1, 4]. Instead, it illustrates its guide using published qualitative literature and the authors' own prior studies—such as Frith and Gleeson's (2004) qualitative questionnaire study of **75 men** [8], Clarke and Kitzinger's (2004) analysis of **26 talk shows** [9], and Braun and Wilkinson's (2003) study on women's talk [10, 11].
* **Databases & Time Span:** Not applicable (N/A); the source is a conceptual and methodological guide rather than a systematic review or empirical investigation bound by bibliographic databases or timeframes [1, 4].
* **Location:** Section "Introduction", pp. 77–78 [1, 4]; Section "A number of decisions", p. 82 [9]; Section "Doing thematic analysis: a step-by-step guide", p. 86 [7]; Section "Pinning down what interpretative analysis actually entails", p. 97 [8].

### 3. WHAT — Key Frameworks and Typologies Proposed
* **Six-Phase Analytic Framework:** A structured, recursive 6-phase guide comprising: (1) *Familiarizing yourself with your data*, (2) *Generating initial codes*, (3) *Searching for themes*, (4) *Reviewing themes*, (5) *Defining and naming themes*, and (6) *Producing the report* (Section "Doing thematic analysis: a step-by-step guide" / Table 1, pp. 86–87) [7, 12].
* **Theoretical & Epistemological Flexibility:** Thematic analysis is independent of a single pre-existing theory and can be applied across essentialist/realist, contextualist (e.g., critical realist), or social constructionist paradigms (Section "Epistemology: essentialist/realist versus constructionist thematic analysis", pp. 81, 85) [13-15].
* **Inductive vs. Theoretical Identification:** Themes can be generated inductively ("bottom-up", data-driven) or theoretically ("top-down", analyst-driven based on prior research questions) (Section "Inductive versus theoretical thematic analysis", pp. 83–84) [16, 17].
* **Semantic vs. Latent Levels:** Analysis can examine explicit, surface meanings (semantic level) or unpick underlying assumptions, conceptualizations, and ideologies (latent level) (Section "Semantic or latent themes", pp. 84–85) [18, 19].
* **Analytical Scope:** Researchers must decide whether to provide a broad, rich thematic description of the entire dataset or a detailed, in-depth account of one specific aspect/topic (Section "A rich description of the data set, or a detailed account of one particular aspect", p. 83) [20].
* **15-Point Quality Evaluation Checklist:** A criteria checklist covering transcription, coding, analysis, overall process, and written reporting to ensure qualitative rigor (Section "What makes good thematic analysis?" / Table 2, pp. 95–96) [21-23].

#### (c) Mechanisms, Conditions, or Antecedents
* **Conditions/Antecedents for Analytical Rigor:**
  1. *Immersive Data Familiarization:* Deep reading, re-reading, and verbatim orthographic transcription build necessary interpretative bedrock [Section: "Phase 1: familiarizing yourself with your data", pp. 87–88].

  4. *Dual Criteria Theme Verification:* Verifying internal homogeneity (data extracts cohere meaningfully) and external heterogeneity (distinctions between themes) across Level 1 (extracts) and Level 2 (full dataset) [Section: "Phase 4: reviewing themes", pp. 91–92].
  5. *Interpretative Narrative Embedding:* Embedding extracts in an analytical narrative that addresses *why* patterns exist relative to literature [Section: "Phase 6: producing the report", p. 93].

  1. *Failure to analyze:* Simply paraphrasing extracts or stringing quotes together without narrative [Section: "Potential pitfalls", p. 95].
  2. *Using interview schedule questions as themes:* Failing to perform cross-dataset pattern analysis [Section: "Potential pitfalls", p. 95].
  3. *Weak/overlapping themes & Anecdotalism:* Reifying 1–2 isolated instances into an overarching theme [Section: "Potential pitfalls", pp. 95–96].
  4. *Mismatch between data extracts and claims:* Generating unsupported or contradicted claims [Section: "Potential pitfalls", p. 96].
