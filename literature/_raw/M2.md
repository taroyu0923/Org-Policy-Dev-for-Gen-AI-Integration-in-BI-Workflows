### **1. WHY — Research Problem and Motivation**
Developing universal quality standards for thematic analysis (TA) is severely complicated by the existence of multiple, paradigmatically divergent iterations of the method [1]. Editors, reviewers, and authors frequently ignore this plurality, improperly imposing positivist "coding reliability" demands (such as inter-rater agreement) onto qualitative reflexive TA studies [1-3]. The authors' motivation is to dismantle ten widespread conceptual and procedural missteps in published TA literature and establish a 20-question evaluation tool to promote conceptual coherence and reflexive quality practice [1, 2, 4]. *(Locations: ABSTRACT, p. 328; "Quality in thematic analysis: what matters?", pp. 328–329; "Introducing our evaluation tool for reviewers and editors", p. 345)* [1-4].

### **2. HOW — Method / Design**
* **Type of Study:** Conceptual methodological commentary, critical reflection, and evaluation framework development [1, 2].
* **Data / Sample / Corpus Size / Databases / Time Span:** This paper is not an empirical review or database meta-analysis [1, 2]. Instead, it synthesizes published literature citing Braun & Clarke (2006) between 2006 and 2020, drawing on editorial and peer-review observations to analyze ten recurrent methodological misapplications without using a structured database search query or quantitative sample corpus [2, 5]. *(Locations: "Quality in thematic analysis: what matters?", pp. 328–329; "Ten common problems in published TA research", p. 333; "Problem two: citing without reading!", p. 336)* [2, 5, 6].

### **3. WHAT — Key Findings, Frameworks, or Typologies Proposed**
* **Typology of TA Approaches:** Categorizes TA into three distinct clusters—**coding reliability**, **codebook**, and **reflexive** TA—that differ fundamentally in philosophy and procedure *(Location: "Problem one: assuming TA is one approach", pp. 333–334)* [7, 8].
* **Critique of Ten Common Misconceptions:** Unpacks 10 prevalent errors in published TA, including assuming TA is homogeneous, treating it as atheoretical or purely descriptive, conflating codes with themes, treating topics as themes, and claiming themes "emerged" *(Location: "Ten common problems in published TA research", pp. 333–345)* [16–44].
* **Twenty-Question Evaluation Tool:** Introduces a 20-item critical appraisal tool (Table 1) for reviewers, editors, and authors to assess methodological fit, theoretical coherence, and analytical execution *(Locations: "Introducing our evaluation tool for reviewers and editors", p. 345; "Table 1", pp. 348–350, 352)* [47–55, 58].

### **4. DEFINITION — Core Construct & Distinction**
*(Locations: "Problem one: assuming TA is one approach", pp. 333–334)* [7-9]:

* **Reflexive TA (Core Construct):**
  > **Quote:** *"'Reflexive' TA captures approaches that fully embrace qualitative research values and the subjective skills the researcher brings to the process – a research team is not required or even desirable for quality. Analysis, which can be more inductive or more theoretical/deductive, is a situated interpretative reflexive process."* [8]
* **Distinction from Coding Reliability TA:**

* **Distinction from Codebook TA:**
  > **Quote:** *"'Codebook' TA captures a cluster of methods that broadly sit within a qualitative paradigm... They use some kind of structured coding framework for developing and documenting the analysis, but consensus between coders and inter-rater reliability are not usually measures of quality."* [8]
