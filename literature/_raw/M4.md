### English Analysis

#### 1. WHY — Research Problem and Motivation
Although the Critical Incident Technique (CIT) has been widely used in service research for three decades, no published research synthesis had systematically evaluated its application [1]. The primary purpose of this study is to systematically review CIT usage in service research to help current and future scholars rigorously examine their methodological choices and establish standardized guidelines for conducting and reporting CIT procedures [1].
* **Location:** Abstract & "Introduction", p. 65 [1].

---

#### 2. HOW — Method / Design
* **Type of Study:** Systematic literature review / research synthesis ("RESEARCH SYNTHESIS METHOD", p. 69) [1, 2].
* **Corpus / Sample Size:** 141 published CIT studies across 27 publications (106 journal articles, 27 conference proceedings papers, and 8 book chapters) ("Sample", p. 69) [2].
* **Time Span:** Studies published from 1975 through 2003 ("Sample", p. 69) [2].
* **Databases / Search Sources:** Initial search of targeted marketing and services journals, followed by online database queries via ABI/Inform, Uncover, and Business Source Premier, plus a snowball sampling procedure ("Sample", p. 69) [2].
* **Data Coding:** Content analysis evaluating 51 identified variables coded across all 141 studies ("Coding and Analysis of Studies", p. 71) [3].

---

#### 3. WHAT — Key Findings and Proposed Framework
* **Typology of CIT Studies:** Categorizes CIT applications into three distinct types: (a) combined with another empirical method (13%), (b) purely interpretive analysis (5%), and (c) content analytic CIT approaches (82%) ("Classification of CIT Studies", p. 70) [4].
* **Widespread Reporting Deficiencies:** Reveals that a majority of CIT studies fail to report essential methodological procedures, including operational definitions of incidents (63% omit criteria), judge independence (56% non-independent or unstated), and reliability metrics (38% report no reliability statistics) ("Discussion - Research Synthesis Summary", p. 77; "Systematization", p. 76) [5-7].
* **Proposed Process Checklist:** Introduces a comprehensive 5-phase research process and reporting checklist (Problem Definition, Study Design, Data Collection, Data Analysis/Interpretation, and Results Report) to standardize procedural rigor and reporting in future CIT studies ("Table 4 / Procedural Recommendations", p. 81–82) [8-10].

---

#### 4. DEFINITION — Core Constructs

* **Critical Incident Technique (CIT):**
  > "...a qualitative interview procedure which facilitates the investigation of significant occurrences (events, incidents, processes, or issues) identified by the respondent, the way they are managed, and the outcomes in terms of perceived effects." [11]
  * **Location:** Section "OVERVIEW OF THE CRITICAL INCIDENT TECHNIQUE", p. 66 (quoting Chell 1998) [11].

* **Critical Incident:**
  > "...an observable human activity that is complete enough in itself to permit inferences and predictions to be made about the person performing the act. A critical incident is described as one that makes a significant contribution, either positively or negatively, to an activity or phenomenon..." [11]
  * **Location:** Section "OVERVIEW OF THE CRITICAL INCIDENT TECHNIQUE", p. 66 (quoting Bitner, Booms, and Tetreault 1990) [11].
  * *Additional foundational definition:* "...defined a critical incident as 'extreme behavior, either outstandingly effective or ineffective with respect to attaining the general aims of the activity' (p. 338)." (Section "Systematization", p. 76, quoting Flanagan 1954) [6].

### 7. INTERVIEW VALUE

Gremler (2004) does **not** reproduce full verbatim interview scripts or specific survey question items within its text [1]. However, it details data collection instrument requirements, story-triggering procedural guidelines, and methodological constructs [1-3]. These items map to the three phases of an interview study on how organizational generative-AI (GenAI) policy is encountered and enacted:

#### Phase 1: Policy Encounter & Interpretation
* **Story-Triggering Instructions & Open-Ended Recall:**
  * **Construct/Guideline:** CIT interviews rely on asking respondents to recall and describe specific, concrete events in their own language rather than forcing them into predefined framework categories [2].
  * **Verbatim Quote:** Respondents are *"simply asked to recall specific events; they can use their own terms and language... The CIT method produces unequivocal and very concrete information as respondents have the opportunity to give a detailed account of their own experiences"* [2].
  * **Instrument Requirement:** Researchers must *"Develop data collection instrument (clear instructions, appropriate story-triggering questions)"* [3].
  * **Location:** Section: **Strengths and Advantages of the CIT Method**, p. 66; Section: **Table 4: Research Process and Reporting Checklist**, p. 81.
* **Contextual Definition & Inclusion Criteria:**
  * **Construct/Guideline:** Before conducting interviews, the researcher must establish clear operational boundaries for what qualifies as an incident versus a routine occurrence [4].
  * **Verbatim Quote:** *"In addition to defining what a critical incident is for a given context, it is important to determine and report the criteria for whether an incident should be included in a study"* [4].
  * **Location:** Section: **Systematization**, p. 76.

#### Phase 2: Implementation & Change Management
* **Dual Incident Valence (Positive and Negative Encounters):**
  * **Construct/Guideline:** Capturing both satisfying/effective and dissatisfying/ineffective policy encounters to understand the full spectrum of change management [5].
  * **Verbatim Quote:** Asking respondents for *"a mix of both positive and negative critical incidents"* [5].
  * **Location:** Section: **Sampling**, p. 73.
* **Extreme vs. Routine Events:**
  * **Construct/Guideline:** While traditional CIT emphasizes extreme or memorable occurrences, examining both exceptional and ordinary incidents can reveal day-to-day policy enactment [6].
  * **Verbatim Quote:** *"Many CIT studies specifically instruct respondents to think of situations that are in some fashion 'critical' or are exceptional... However, the collection of such events can actually be an asset... depending on the research questions being considered"* [6].
  * **Location:** Section: **Past Criticisms of Use of CIT Method in Service Research**, p. 78.
* **Internal Encounter Perspective:**
  * **Construct/Guideline:** Applying CIT to study internal organizational/employee encounters rather than external end-customer interactions [7, 8].
  * **Verbatim Quote:** Studies examine service interactions *"from the perspective of the firm—specifically, the customer-contact employee"* or focus on *"internal services"* [7, 8].
  * **Location:** Section: **The Role of the CIT Method in Service Research**, p. 68; Section: **Study Contexts**, p. 70.

#### Phase 3: Organizational Adaptation Dynamics
* **Dyadic / Multi-Perspective Data Collection:**
  * **Construct/Guideline:** Collecting incident descriptions from both sides of an internal boundary (e.g., policy enforcers/governance teams vs. BI/analytics practitioners) [6, 9].
  * **Verbatim Quote:** *"capturing data from both sides of the service encounter... Perhaps using the CIT method to capture both the customer's and the employee's view of the same incident would provide additional insights into other service interaction issues"* [6, 9].
  * **Location:** Section: **Contextual Recommendations**, pp. 78–79.
* **Longitudinal & Relational Adaptation Variations:**
  * **Construct/Guideline:** Adapting CIT to assess how incident criticality changes over time during ongoing policy adaptation [9].
  * **Verbatim Quote:** Methodological variations such as *"the Sequential Critical Incident Technique (SIT)... Critical Incident in a Relational Context (CIRC)... Criticality Critical Incident Technique (CCIT)..."* address long-term adaptation [9]. Furthermore, *"the criticality of critical incidents may differ over time and between customers. Thus, future CIT research might try to determine which events are truly critical to the long-term health of the customer-firm relationship"* [9].
  * **Location:** Section: **Application Recommendations**, pp. 79–80; Section: **Contextual Recommendations**, p. 79.
