1. WHY: High-level AI ethics principles frameworks offer abstract, technology-neutral direction but "without further best practice guidance, practitioners are left with nothing much beyond truisms" (Sec.1 Introduction, p.173:1-173:2). Existing research concentrates on algorithm-level solutions for a narrow subset of mathematics-amenable principles (fairness, privacy), whereas ethical issues can arise at any step of the development lifecycle, crosscutting AI, non-AI, and data components (Sec.1, p.173:1-173:2). A pattern-oriented approach is needed to bridge this principle-algorithm gap with reusable, system-level solutions across governance, process, and technical architecture (Sec.1, p.173:2-173:3).
2. HOW: Systematic Multivocal Literature Review (MLR) combining academic peer-reviewed literature and industry grey literature (Sec.1, p.173:3; Sec.2.1, p.173:3-173:4). Academic review followed Kitchenham & Charters' SLR guideline; grey-literature review followed Garousi et al.'s guideline (Sec.2.2, p.173:4). Databases: ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Science Direct, Springer Link, Google Scholar (academic); Google Search Engine (grey lit) (Sec.2.2, p.173:4). Search terms: "AI," "Responsible" (mapped via Australia's 8 AI ethics principles + synonyms), "Solution" (Sec.2.2 & Table 1, p.173:4). Initial search: 4,470 academic + 2,595 grey items (7,065 total); screening yielded 308 academic + 95 grey; after quality assessment + snowballing, final corpus = 205 academic items + 69 grey items (274 total) (Sec.2.1-2.2, Fig.2, p.173:4-173:5). Time span: literature published up to 31 July 2022 (Sec.2.2, p.173:4).
3. WHAT: Overall catalogue classifies RAI operationalization patterns into three groups — multi-level governance patterns, trustworthy process patterns, RAI-by-design product patterns (Sec.1, p.173:3; Sec.2.3, p.173:5). Typology 1 — Multi-Level Governance Patterns: Industry level (RAI regulation, regulatory sandboxes, building codes, RAI standards), Organization level (leadership commitment, ethics committees, code of ethics, software bill of materials), Team level (customized agile processes, diverse teams, continuous documentation) (Sec.3, p.173:5-173:15). Typology 2 — Trustworthy Process Patterns: Requirement Engineering (verifiable ethical requirements), Design (multi-level co-architecting), Implementation (RAI governance of/via APIs), Testing (ethical acceptance testing), Operation (continuous deployment, multi-level co-versioning) (Sec.4, p.173:15-173:21). Typology 3 — RAI-by-Design Product Patterns: Supply Chain layer (bill of materials registry, verifiable ethical credentials), Deployed System layer (AI mode switcher, multi-model decision maker), Operation Infrastructure layer (continuous ethical validator, ethical sandbox, ethical black box) (Sec.5, p.173:21-173:28).
4. DEFINITION: "The governance for RAI systems can be defined as the structures and processes that are employed to ensure that the development and use of AI systems meet AI ethics principles." (Sec.3 Governance Patterns, p.173:5). Related: "Responsible Artificial Intelligence (RAI) refers to the ethical development of AI systems to benefit the humans, society, and environment." (Sec.1, p.173:1-173:2). "In software engineering, a pattern is a reusable solution to a problem that occurs commonly within a given context in software development." (Sec.1, p.173:2)
