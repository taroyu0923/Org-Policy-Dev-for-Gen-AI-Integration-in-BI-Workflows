## 1. WHY
Sensemaking research had grown (the paper notes over 4,000 journal articles in ProQuest, p.108) but was fragmented and carried unarticulated ontological differences; the review takes stock and sets a research agenda. *Location: introduction, pp.57–58; ontological differences p.94.*

## 2. HOW
Qualitative literature review of organisational sensemaking research, with a definitional synthesis (Table 1, Selected Definitions, pp.63–65). The paper restricts itself to organisational studies and collective sensemaking (scope statements p.59–60, see §9). *Location: pp.58–60.*

## 3. WHAT
- An integrated definition (p.67).
- Tables of specific forms (Table 2, p.68) and related constructs (Table 3, p.69), including sensegiving and sensebreaking.
- Triggers of sensemaking: cues with ambiguous meaning or uncertain outcomes (p.70).
- A four-way typology of who shapes sensemaking in organisations, **guided, fragmented, restricted, minimal** (p.79). The paper attributes it to Maitlis (2005), so cite Maitlis (2005) for the typology itself.
- Ontological and temporal debates (individual cognitive versus social construction; retrospective versus prospective), p.94.
- Future agenda: "four broad domains", p.97 (power, emotion, institutions, distributed sensemaking per the p.97 sentence).

## 4. DEFINITION
Sensemaking is "a process, prompted by violated expectations, that involves attending to and bracketing cues in the environment, creating intersubjective meaning through cycles of interpretation and action, and thereby enacting a more ordered environment from which further cues can be drawn." *(p.67)*
Opening definition: "Sensemaking—the process through which individuals work to understand novel, unexpected, or confusing events—has become a critically important topic in the study of organizations." *(p.58)*
Sensegiving (quoting Gioia & Chittipeddi 1991): "the process of attempting to influence the sensemaking and meaning construction of others toward a preferred redefinition of organizational reality" *(p.67)*. Sensebreaking (quoting Pratt 2000): "the destruction or breaking down of meaning" *(p.69)*.

## 5. CITABLE
- "even in more top-down processes, when organizational leaders engage in sensegiving, organizational members are not simply passive recipients of meaning but instead engage in their own sensemaking and adopt, alter, resist, or reject the sense they have been given" *(p.78)*
- "People who act in organizations often produce structures, constraints, and opportunities that were not there before they took action" *(p.85, quoting Weick 1988)*
- "organizational stakeholders' ongoing reinterpretations of their institutions and the institutional practices in which they engage produce shifts in the very institutions they reproduce" *(p.105)*
- "Sensemaking is triggered by cues—such as issues, events, or situations—for which the meaning is ambiguous and/or outcomes uncertain" *(p.70)*
