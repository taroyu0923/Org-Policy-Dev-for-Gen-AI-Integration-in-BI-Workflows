## 1. WHY
Voice and silence research has grown sharply since the author's 2014 review; the paper asks how far understanding has deepened or narrowed, what progress has been made on earlier gaps, and where research should go. *Location: Abstract p.79; Introduction p.80.*

## 2. HOW
Systematic literature review. Search "using several online databases (PsychInfo, PsychNet, ABI-INFORM)" for peer-reviewed articles 2014–2021 with voice, silence, speaking up, whistleblowing, issue selling or dissent in the title; forward citation search from Morrison (2014); restricted to the 2021 Financial Times Journal List or a five-year impact factor of at least 2.5; plus papers accepted in 2021. "This resulted in a set of 158 papers." Primarily empirical work. *Location: p.81.*

## 3. WHAT
- **Voice concept:** organisational-behaviour research has converged on voice as discretionary, constructive, change-oriented, with a promotive versus prohibitive distinction. *(pp.80–84)*
- **Antecedents:** opportunity, dispositions, supportive leadership acting through psychological safety, self-efficacy and felt responsibility. *(pp.85–90)*
- **Managerial endorsement:** depends on voicer status, message quality and supervisors' state and responses. *(pp.88–91)*
- **Silence** treated as its own construct, tied to psychological safety. *(pp.92–95)*
- **Agenda:** temporal dynamics, ethical voice and whistleblowing, non-pro-organisational voice, and bridging organisational behaviour with employment relations. *(pp.95–100)*
- The literature is "increasingly fragmented" *(p.84)*; largest sample origin China (34% of studies) then the US (21%). *(p.83)*

## 4. DEFINITION
Voice: "informal and discretionary communication of ideas, suggestions, concerns, problems, or opinions about work-related issues, with the intent to bring about improvement or change." *(p.80)*
Silence: "Silence, in contrast to voice, is when employees withhold ideas, information about problems, or opinions on work-related issues." *(p.81)*
Scope: "I am restricting my focus to internal and upward voice behavior—speaking up to, or in the presence of, someone who holds a higher position within the organization." *(p.80)*

## 5. CITABLE

- **Main claims:** voice and silence are driven by perceived safety and perceived efficacy; supervisors' openness and explanations shape later voice; prohibitive voice threatens managers more than promotive voice; formal voice mechanisms can produce selective silence (§5).
