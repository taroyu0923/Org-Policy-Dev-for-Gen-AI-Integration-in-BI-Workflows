## 1. WHY
GenAI is rapidly diffusing into the workplace and widely seen as productivity-enhancing, but simultaneously introduces new forms of **technostress** that pre-GenAI theoretical models can no longer fully capture (pp. 1–2). Young professionals are a critical demographic — highly adaptable early adopters, but with limited job tenure, making them highly sensitive to psychological uncertainty, displacement anxiety, and adaptation pressure (p. 3). The study investigates GenAI's effect on well-being/stress across four industries: R&D, IT, finance, marketing (pp. 2, 4).

## 2. HOW
Qualitative study, semi-structured expert interviews + structured qualitative content analysis (pp. 5–6). **15 interviews** (35–48 min), guide combining deductive technostress-dimension core with open-ended emergent-experience questions (p. 5). Sample: 15 young professionals (26–35, ≥bachelor's degree, ≤5 yrs experience): IT (n=4), R&D (n=4), finance (n=2), marketing (n=5); thematic saturation observed after 11 interviews (p. 6). Recruited via professional networks/referrals at organizations piloting/using GenAI. Submitted Oct 22, 2025; published Dec 12, 2025.

## 3. WHAT
- **Transformational (not substitutive) anxiety**: professionals largely reject full-replacement fears but perceive strong workflow/role transformation (pp. 6–7).
- Sector-dependent adaptation pressure: R&D/IT feel intense pressure to track bi-weekly model releases; marketing/finance feel comparatively little pressure (p. 7).
- Eradication of "mental breaks": automation of simple tasks leaves continuous complex/conceptual work (p. 7).
- Forced private-time learning: high workloads push IT/R&D/finance professionals to learn GenAI on personal time (p. 8).
- Dual-path view: same technology triggers "techno-eustress" (positive, motivating) and "techno-distress" concurrently (p. 11).
- **Five novel GenAI-specific stressor clusters** (Table 2, pp. 8–11): Compliance (GDPR, EU AI Act, copyright, ethical uncertainty), Cognitive Effects (dependency, skill degradation, creativity decline), Reliability (verification/hallucination-checking load), Role Shift (toward conceptual/monitoring roles), Positive Stress (efficiency, motivation, idea generation).

## 4. DEFINITION
Uses Brod's (1984) classic technostress definition ("a modern disease of adaptation caused by an inability to cope with the new computer technologies in a healthy manner"). Core governance construct proposed for mitigation: "...to establish a structured guideline or governance to provide employees with clear information on the scope and purpose of GenAI use. Such a framework should clearly define the scope and purpose of GenAI use." (Section 5.2 Practical Implications, p. 13)

## 5. CITABLE
> "The issue of compliance is not new to technostress, but it is being amplified by GenAI due to new regulations, copyright laws, and emerging data protection risks. Consequently, GenAI implementation introduces additional compliance challenges that extend beyond conventional IT governance, thereby adding both cognitive and administrative burdens on employees and organizations." (Section 5.1, p. 11)

> "In the context of the AI Act, this is no longer just a formal recommendation. Instead, as of February 2, 2025, it has become mandatory for companies to ensure that their workforce possesses a sufficient level of AI literacy (European Union, 2024)." (Section 5.2, p. 12)

> "...while managers often expect employees to work faster with AI (Monahan and Burlacu, 2024), they may underestimate the amount of review and verification work required to obtain reliable results. Therefore, organizations should explicitly plan their verification efforts and allocate time for employees to review AI-generated outputs." (Section 5.2, p. 12)

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
Full interview protocol lives in online Supplementary Material (not fully reproduced in-text), but 5 illustrative questions are quoted directly.

**Policy Framework Development**
- Trust-verification question: "How do you typically deal with the outputs GenAI provides? What's your process for deciding whether to trust or adjust them?" (Section 3, p. 5) — also probes "Shadow AI" risk (unauthorized private-account use).

**Implementation & Change Management**
- Workflow-disruption baseline: "Thinking about your everyday work: What has changed for you since you started using GenAI tools like ChatGPT or similar applications?" (Section 3, p. 5)
- Techno-complexity/usability: "When using GenAI tools at work, how easy or difficult do you find it to understand and use them effectively?" (Section 3, p. 5)

**Organizational Adaptation Dynamics**
- Techno-insecurity: "Do you feel that GenAI poses a threat to your role or job security?" (Section 3, p. 5)
- Skill-development trajectory: "In what ways, if any, has GenAI influenced how you develop your skills or think about your own expertise?" (Section 3, p. 5)

## 9. LIMITATIONS
- Limited generalizability: "the sample size was sufficient for qualitative research, [but] it limits the generalizability of the findings to broader populations." Sample skews toward GenAI-affected industries/young professionals. (Section 5.3, p. 14)
- Unbalanced sectoral distribution (IT n=4, R&D n=4, Marketing n=5, Finance n=2); "patterns should be considered as exploratory." (p. 14)
- Suggestive wording/response bias: "Although a pre-test was conducted, some open questions appear suggestively worded, so response bias cannot be entirely ruled out." (p. 14)
- Residual interviewer bias despite mitigation efforts (p. 14).

## 10. BI LINK
Literal terms "business intelligence"/"data warehousing"/"decision-support" absent from findings, but the authors' department is "Business Informatics" (institutional mention, p. 1), and the study directly touches decision-making and analytical data handling:
- "This heightened dependence may introduce new strains on individuals, particularly when GenAI tools become indispensable to day-to-day tasks and decision-making processes." (Section 5.1, p. 11)
- Illustrative quote on uncritical AI-driven statistical analysis: "...advertising effectiveness is sometimes evaluated with ChatGPT... we throw an Excel table into ChatGPT and then say please tell me if you apply this test? We have people who did not study this, who do not know what they are interpreting, who throw a table in somewhere a number comes out at the bottom and then they say okay, I have now found my truth." (Section 4.6, p. 9) — directly relevant to BI data-literacy/governance risk.
