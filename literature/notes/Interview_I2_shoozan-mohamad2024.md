## 1. WHY
Designing a reliable interview protocol is complex and requires methodological skill to ensure data credibility (Section 1 Introduction, p. 2). The study needed to systematically develop and refine a semi-structured interview protocol to analyze English-speaking needs of front-office operators in the Maldives tourism industry, to inform a mobile-learning module design (Section 1, p. 2; Section 2/2.1, p. 3).

## 2. HOW
Empirical case study applying Castillo-Montoya's (2016) IPR Framework (Section 1, p. 2; Section 2, p. 3). 
- **Phase 1**: matrix aligning 8 interview questions to the main research question + 4 sub-questions (Section 2.1, p. 3; 3.1.1, pp. 4–5).
- **Phase 3**: 4 expert reviewers (Maldivian higher-ed lecturers/managers, 2–6 yrs research experience, 18–30 yrs English-teaching experience) evaluated the protocol against a 15-item close-reading checklist over ~2 weeks (Section 3.1.3, p. 7; Table 2, p. 7).
- **Phase 4**: 3 pilot interviewees matching the target population (1 guesthouse receptionist, 2 resort front-office agents, 1–2 yrs experience), 40–50 min simulated interviews (Section 3.1.4, p. 8; Table 3, p. 8).
- No external literature database used for the corpus itself (primary empirical study).

## 3. WHAT
- Phase 1 matrix successfully closed information gaps between protocol and research questions (3.1.1, p. 5).
- Phase 2: removing academic jargon (e.g. "readiness for mobile learning" → plain language) and structuring introductory/transition/key/closing questions produced an inquiry-based conversation (3.1.2, pp. 5–6).
- Phase 3: expert feedback narrowed an overbroad "daily life or work" question to strictly "work" (3.1.3, pp. 7–8).
- Phase 4: pilot revealed limited responses to bare open-ended questions, underscoring the need for pre-planned clarifying/elaborating probes (3.1.4, pp. 8–9).

## 4. DEFINITION
- **IPR Framework**: "a framework proposed by Castillo-Montoya in 2016 to systematically develop and refine interview protocols... helps qualitative researchers strengthen the reliability of interview protocols" (Section 1, p. 2).
- **Interview protocols**: "consist of main questions aligned to the research questions, potential follow-up questions and probes, or an outline of the topics to be addressed" (Section 1, p. 2).

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
A **worked, second-hand application of the IPR framework** — useful as a template for how the thesis's own protocol matrix/pilot process should look.

**Policy Framework Development**
- Interview Protocol Matrix (Table 1, 3.1.1, p. 5): map governance objectives (data privacy, algorithmic transparency, model drift) to interview items, verifying "that there are no gaps in interview questions that could leave any part of the research question unanswered" (3.1.1, p. 5).
- Close-Reading Checklist (15-item, Section 3.1.3, p. 7) — use internal compliance/IT/business stakeholders as "expert reviewers" before fielding.
- Jargon avoidance example (3.1.2, p. 5): "readiness for mobile learning" → plain-language question. BI analogy: avoid "algorithmic bias" → ask "What are your views on how we should check if AI-generated reports are accurate?"

**Implementation & Change Management**
- Introductory question: "to build rapport and to understand the interviewees' background, their work, and their workspace" (3.1.2, p. 6). Adapted: "Can you describe your role and the main data tools you currently use for decision-making?"
- Transition question: "to move the interview toward the main questions and maintain the flow" (3.1.2, p. 6). Adapted: "How has the introduction of GenAI tools affected the way you and your team access or analyze company data?"
- Guided script example (3.1.2, p. 6): "In the next few questions I would like to ask about English speaking areas you need, learning activities you prefer..." — adaptable signposting script for AI-policy interviews.

**Organizational Adaptation Dynamics**
- Clarifying/elaborating probes needed because open questions yielded "limited" answers (3.1.4, p. 9): "What kind of tasks at work require you to use English speaking skills?" / "What do these tasks involve?" → adapt to "What kind of tasks at work require you to use GenAI tools?" / "What does using GenAI for these data-analysis tasks involve?"
- Vernacular adaptation (3.1.4, p. 9): common conversational phrases from pilot transcripts folded into the final protocol — analogous to capturing employees' informal names for shadow-AI tools.
