## 1. WHY
GenAI is rapidly reshaping global healthcare and driving digital transformation, but there is a lack of evidence tracking how senior hospital leaders — who hold final decision-making power over technology adoption — evolve their perceptions over time (p. 2). Prior research focuses on technical/operational aspects, leaving a leadership-attitude gap; understanding shifting viewpoints is crucial for policy, training, and sustainable digital transformation (p. 2 [Introduction]; p. 3 [Introduction, motivation]).

## 2. HOW
Multicenter, longitudinal, descriptive **qualitative phenomenological study** (p. 2). Two-wave semistructured telephone interviews (p. 2). Interview protocol underwent a 3-stage development process (systematic-review-derived questions, pilot testing, role-played interviews) (p. 3). Colaizzi's method for manual data analysis (p. 2). Purposive sample: **32 senior hospital leaders** (8 hospital directors/deputies, 24 department heads/deputies) from 3 tertiary hospitals in Hebei, Guangdong, Sichuan provinces (p. 2). All 32 completed both waves → **64 interviews** total (p. 4). Baseline data collected **August 2024**, follow-up **February 2025** (6-month interval) (p. 2, 4).

## 3. WHAT
- **Knowledge gain**: correct understanding of GenAI rose from 17/32 to 29/32 over 6 months, driven by media, peer discussion, conventions, direct project exposure (Theme 1, p. 4).
- **Personal-to-institutional attitude shift**: initial preference for free personal trials (budget concerns) shifted to formal institutional planning/budgeting for hospital-level integration by wave 2 (Theme 2, pp. 4–5).
- **Evolving barriers/catalysts**: early obstacles (technical illiteracy, career/investment risk concerns) diminished; peer bandwagon effects and DeepSeek's launch emerged as key catalysts for budget requests (Theme 3, p. 5; Discussion, p. 6).
- **Diffusion of Innovation Theory** applied: longitudinal exposure/experiential learning moves leaders from skepticism to informed optimism (Discussion, p. 6).
- **Institutional Isomorphism**: coercive pressures (national policy) and normative pressures (peer institutions sharing successes) drove strategic convergence, legitimizing investment (Discussion, p. 6).

## 4. DEFINITION
**Perception**: "we define perception as the combination of awareness of GenAI's existence and accurate knowledge of its core functions." (Introduction, p. 2). Governance/policy are treated as institutional requirements/external pressures rather than formally defined standalone constructs — e.g., "provided that institutions address governance, workforce training, and ethical oversight" (p. 2); "robust policy frameworks are needed to facilitate GenAI integration" (pp. 1, 7).

## 5. CITABLE
> "Improved knowledge through diverse information channels has enhanced their comprehension and shifted their stance from cautious personal experimentation toward formal institutional adoption." (Abstract - Conclusions, p. 1; reiterated p. 6)

> "Under coercive pressures, national policy endorsements and regulatory expectations compelled leaders to align with government-backed technology agendas. Simultaneously, normative pressures emerged as peer institutions shared GenAI successes at professional forums, creating industry norms that legitimized investment... The dual forces reduced uncertainty, reinforced trust in GenAI's value, and accelerated formal adoption across hospitals..." (Discussion - Principal Findings, p. 6)

> "The dynamic interplay between diminishing obstacles and emerging catalysts, notably the influence of peer practices and technological breakthroughs such as DeepSeek, underscores the potential for transformative change in health care management. Targeted educational initiatives, pilot projects, and robust policy frameworks are needed to facilitate GenAI integration." (Conclusions, pp. 6–7)

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE

**Policy Framework Development**
- Recruitment/inquiry domain: "professional involvement in GenAI adoption, including current or past engagement with artificial intelligence (AI)–related projects, training programs, or policy development" (Methods, p. 3).
- Baseline awareness prompt: "Have you heard of GenAI?" (p. 3); conceptual-literacy prompt: "Can you describe how GenAI works in your own words?" (p. 3).
- Administrative-hesitation probe (adapted from Quote P3-HW1): "At the director's level, I support staff members to experiment with AI. However, I don't see how it can be adopted at the [organizational] level." (Theme 2 Subtheme 2, p. 5)

**Implementation & Change Management**
- Longitudinal change probe: asking participants in wave 2 about "the changes and current status of their GenAI adoption" (Theme 2 Subtheme 2, p. 5).
- Risk-tolerance probe (adapted from Quote P22-HW1): "We have to consider the benefits versus risks when deciding on an investment. Not that I don't support (formal adoption), the timing is not mature." (Theme 3 Subtheme 1, pp. 5–6)

**Organizational Adaptation Dynamics**
- Inquiry domain: "factors influencing institutional adoption choices such as resource allocation priorities, staff readiness assessments, and perceived utility" (Methods, p. 3).
- Mimetic/normative-pressure probe (adapted from Quote P14-HW2): "Other [organizations] are using AI already... We will fall behind if we don't do anything." (Theme 3 Subtheme 2, p. 5)
- Technical-disruption probe (adapted from Quote P29-HW2): "At least you have to have some specific projects to apply for the budgets... Much easier now, especially with [DeepSeek]. Everybody is promoting it." (Theme 3 Subtheme 2, p. 5)

## 9. LIMITATIONS
- Sampling diversity: drawn exclusively from three tertiary hospitals, restricting diversity and excluding other institution types/tiers in China (Discussion, p. 6).
- Data collection depth: remote telephone interviews "could limit the depth of information compared to face-to-face interactions." (p. 6)
- No direct macro-driver evidence: did not formally isolate/assess how DeepSeek's release or national policy endorsements drove adoption, despite identifying them as likely major drivers (p. 6).
