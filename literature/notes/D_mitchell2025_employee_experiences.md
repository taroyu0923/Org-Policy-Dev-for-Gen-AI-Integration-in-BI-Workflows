## 1. WHY
Rapid AI integration into operational, managerial, and customer-facing processes raises significant questions about job security, work identity, ethical implications, and human agency. Employees' lived experiences and subjective attitudes toward these changes remain underexplored in the literature; the qualitative study aims to fill this gap with nuanced, context-specific insight rather than purely quantitative metrics.
*Location: Section 1 (Introduction), p.2 & p.4.*

## Albert's Follow-Up Question (Turn 4) — Theory Base & Interview Design

**Albert's question:** *"Since this ref provide interview detail especially for internal contributor and mid-level manager, can you provide more structure about its theory base and interview design?"*

### 1. Theoretical Foundation (5 pillars)
1. **Socio-Technical Systems Theory** — AI adoption reconfigures organizational "relational contours," work roles, and the social contract between employees and the firm, not just technical installation. *(Section 1, p.4; Section 2, p.5)*
2. **Adapted Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)** — traditional TAM (usefulness/ease-of-use) extended to incorporate emotional, social, and ethical dimensions of employee–AI interaction. *(Section 1, p.4)*
3. **Responsible AI Governance Framework** — employee trust and perceived legitimacy stem from whether organizational policy delivers on fairness, explainability, privacy, and human oversight. *(Section 1, p.3; Section 2, p.6)*
4. **Competency-Based & Role-Renegotiation Theory** — employees dynamically combine technical, analytical, and social-cognitive skills to renegotiate professional authority and role definition in knowledge-intensive environments. *(Section 1, p.3; Section 2, pp.5–6)*
5. **Organizational Culture, Psychological Safety & Affective Trust** — a culture tolerant of failure and encouraging experimentation promotes positive adaptation; rigid hierarchy and low psychological safety amplify threat/alienation. *(Section 1, p.4)*

### 2. Semi-Structured Interview Design

Using Mitchell's stated protocol parameters (Section 3, p.8), mapped into a dual-track design across three organizational phases:

```
                  [ Mitchell (2025) Interview Design Map ]
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
         ▼                           ▼                           ▼
[ Phase 1: Policy Framework ] [ Phase 2: Change Management ] [ Phase 3: Adaptation Dynamics ]
  - Responsible AI Governance   - Training & Support          - Role Redefinition
  - Ethical Considerations      - Change Communication        - Workflow Transformation
  - Algorithmic Trust           - Resistance Moderation       - Collaborative Dynamics
```

#### Target A: Internal Contributors (Technical & Administrative Staff)
*Focus: job-security fears, daily workflow disruption, skill gaps, hands-on tool trust.*

- **Phase 1 (Operational Trust & Governance)** — Constructs: "Trust and Reliability Concerns" & "Ethical Considerations."
  *Core Q:* "How do you verify or validate the correctness of AI-generated insights before using them, and does your company have clear rules for when you are allowed to question the system's reasoning?"
  *Sub-probe:* "Do you feel personally responsible if an unverified AI output leads to an error in your routine tasks?"
- **Phase 2 (Training & Coping)** — Constructs: "Anxiety and Uncertainty" & "Skill Development and Learning Needs."
  *Core Q:* "What were your initial worries when AI was first integrated into your workflow, and what specific training or mentoring was offered to help you build the new technical and analytical skills required?"
  *Sub-probe:* "Did you feel you had a voice, or was the technology imposed from the top-down?"
- **Phase 3 (Workflow & Value)** — Constructs: "Workflow Transformation" & "Perceived Efficiency Gains."
  *Core Q:* "How has AI changed the split between your manual tasks and your analytical tasks, and has this made your daily work feel more strategic or more repetitive?"

#### Target B: Mid-Level Managers
*Focus: team restructuring, subordinate resistance, top-down policy implementation, strategic decision support.*

- **Phase 1 (System Governance & Accountability)** — Constructs: "Responsible AI Governance" & "Decision-Making Support."
  *Core Q:* "How do you balance corporate directives regarding ethical governance and human oversight with the practical pressure to make quick, data-driven decisions using predictive models?"
  *Sub-probe:* "How does your department handle accountability when an AI-supported decision results in an unintended consequence?"
- **Phase 2 (Leadership & Resistance)** — Constructs: "Communication and Change Management" & "Resistance and Adaptation."
  *Core Q:* "What change management strategies did you use to communicate the goals of AI adoption to your team, and how did you address subtle or explicit resistance from team members who feared redundancy?"
  *Sub-probe:* "How did the quality of organizational training programs affect your team's adaptation rate?"
- **Phase 3 (Role & Team Redefinition)** — Constructs: "Role Redefinition" & "Collaborative Work Dynamics."
  *Core Q:* "How has your role as a manager shifted from supervising manual processes to guiding critical thinking and human oversight in human-AI collaborative teams?"
