## 1. WHY
LLM assistants turn natural-language requests into analysis code, but can misread intent or produce plausible wrong outputs; existing assistants lack verification support and even experts "can be susceptible to automation bias and overreliance". *Location: Abstract and §1, pp.1–2.*

## 2. HOW
Qualitative user study with a purpose-built design probe (natural-language explanation, code, comments, interactive data tables, summary visualisations). 22 professional analysts (P01–P22), recruited "from a large data-driven software company"; the acknowledgments name Microsoft. One tutorial task plus 10 main tasks, 2 of them error-free, over 9 unique datasets (Kaggle datasets uploaded after February 2022; ARCADE benchmark; OpenAI Code Interpreter/GPT-4 outputs). Session about 60 minutes: tutorial about 10 min, activity about 40 min (think-aloud), semi-structured interview about 10 min, post-task survey. *Location: §4 pp.3–6; §5 pp.8–9.*

## 3. WHAT
- **Verification typology:** procedure-oriented behaviours ("what did the AI do?") and data-oriented behaviours ("does the resulting data make sense?"); analysts typically start procedure-oriented and shift to data-oriented once they notice an issue. *(p.3; §6.1, p.9)*
- **Interleaved artifacts:** data artifacts support procedure checks and procedure artifacts support data checks. *(§6.2, p.10)*
- **Background effects:** prior coding, data and domain experience shape verification style. *(§6.3, pp.13–14)*
- **Design implications:** connect data and procedure artifacts, communicate operation semantics, surface AI assumptions, embed test cases/checklists. *(§7.2, p.14)*

## 4. DEFINITION
"Each workflow starts when participants began verifying the AI-generated analysis (after understanding the task and data) and ends when they confirmed or rejected the existence of an error." *(§5, p.9)*
Typology definition: "Within a verification workflow, participants with procedure-oriented behaviors were primarily focused on validating the AI's procedure, possibly drawing upon data artifacts to aid their understanding. In contrast, participants exhibiting data-oriented behaviors gave precedence to scrutinizing the data involved in the analysis, relegating the AI's procedure to a supplementary role." *(§6.1, p.9)*

## 5. CITABLE
The paper does not investigate organisational policy, governance or rules; it studies analysts' verification behaviour.
- "In this new paradigm, analysts' focus now shifts from writing and validating their own analyses to understanding and evaluating the analyses generated by AI assistants." *(§1, p.1)*
- "We build on the perspective of recent works in Human-Centered Explainable AI… in that users' needs to understand AI-generated outputs are dependent on their backgrounds, social, and organizational contexts." *(§1, p.2)*
- "AI-based assistants, while powerful, can misinterpret user intentions… or make seemingly correct erroneous outputs… because conclusions from data analyses often inform high-impact decisions in science, business, and government it is important for users of AI-based analysis assistants to critically reflect on the assistant's outputs." *(§1, p.1; drop citation numbers when quoting)*

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
Instruments in the paper (both verbatim, §5, p.9): a post-task survey "that asked for a confirmation as to whether the AI's generation was erroneous and their reasoning", and a semi-structured interview in which the authors "asked open-ended questions to understand participants' backgrounds and prior experiences, overall verification workflows, and rationales behind their behaviors". The study also uses an "intern" scenario probe (p.8, Imagine having an intern who is provided with a dataset…). Usable for the incident-type (ii) probe on verifying AI-generated analyses.
- **Policy Encounter & Interpretation:** the paper supplies no policy content. Construct usable: the need to "critically reflect on the assistant's outputs" (p.1); how participants know whether an artifact is deterministic tool output or LLM-generated (§7.1.3, p.14).
- **Implementation & Change Management:** analysts move from writing code to auditing AI-generated logic (p.1); some translate Python steps into SQL or spreadsheet operations they know (§6.3.2, p.13; §7.2, p.14).
- **Organizational Adaptation Dynamics:** "some participants expressed a preference to reserve AI assistance for peripheral tasks such as error-checking or report generation rather than the central analysis" *(p.14; a participant preference, not an organisational norm)*; quick data sanity checks as a data-literacy practice (§7.1.1, p.14).
- **Not in the paper (NotebookLM added these; do not cite):** any statement that governance policies mandate critical reflection or that "policy enforcement" requires artifact provenance; "institutional pressure" behind vigilant verification.

## 9. LIMITATIONS
Verbatim, from "Limitations and Future Work" (§7.3):
1. "First, the analysis queries involved in the study tasks primarily involved sequences of data transformations. Other aspects of data analysis (e.g., data visualization, statistical modeling, machine learning, etc.) were not explored." *(p.15)*
2. "Second, since our study contained a small number of verification tasks while using real responses generated by state-of-the-art nondeterministic models, components of the AI's explanation (e.g., listing its assumptions of the data and columns) and specific data operations involved in the data analysis task (i.e., filtering for data or merging data) were not controlled for across tasks." *(p.16)*
3. "Third, as discussed in our study design considerations (Sec. 4.1) participants were not the ones gathering the data and coming up with the analysis goal. As a result, prior to them verifying the AI-generated analyses, they first spent time understanding the data and analysis goal, a process that may not be as pertinent in conducting their own data analysis with AI assistants." *(p.16; the PDF prints "desisgn" for "design")*
Design trade-off, stated in §4.1 rather than as a limitation: "In our study, we chose to zoom in on one turn of the human-AI interaction and disallow follow-up prompts… opting against follow-up prompts mitigates the impact of prompt expertise but at the cost of confining analysts' actions." *(p.5)*
Sampling note, not labelled a limitation: all participants came from one company.

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (v2.4-T; focus: how analysts verify AI-generated analyses, incident type ii)
- **Definitions:** see §4.
- **Main claims:** analysts' role shifts from authoring to evaluating (p.1); verification starts procedure-oriented and moves to data-oriented when an anomaly appears (p.3, §6.1 p.9); data and procedure artifacts are interleaved (p.10); "participants sometimes formed incorrect conclusions due to their reliance on the AI's procedure" *(p.11)*.
- **Mechanisms and conditions:** prior coding experience inclines analysts to code review, data and domain experience to data scrutiny; "we were maniacal about not drawing the wrong conclusion" (P22, p.13); lack of code comprehension and low trust in data artifacts whose creation is unclear lead to weaker verification (§6.3, p.13).
- **Evidence base:** empirical, qualitative; n = 22; single company; see §2.
- **Stated limitations:** see §9.
