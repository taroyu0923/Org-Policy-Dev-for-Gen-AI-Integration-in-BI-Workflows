## 1. WHY
More than 70% of BI projects fail to deliver expected returns ("Above the 70% of BI projects fail to yield the expected returns"); adoption, utilization and success (AUS) studies existed separately, but no review had synthesised all three. *Location: §1, pp.1–2.*

## 2. HOW
Systematic literature review following Kitchenham's guidelines. 612 articles identified as potentially relevant, 111 peer-reviewed studies retained after inclusion/exclusion and quality scoring (97 high quality, 14 medium, 12 low excluded, as printed), published 2000–2019. Ten leading databases "located through Google Scholar" (the list under Table 2 names eleven services: Emerald Insight, ScienceDirect, EBSCOhost, ProQuest, Wiley, IEEE Xplore, JSTOR, Taylor & Francis, Sage, SpringerLink, Web of Science). *Location: §2, pp.2–4.*

## 3. WHAT
- **Three research categories** by share of studies: Success 41%, Utilization 38%, Adoption 28% (Fig. 5; shares sum above 100% as printed, so studies count in more than one category). *(§3.3.1, pp.5–6)*
- **28 theories/frameworks/models;** most frequent DeLone & McLean (16), TAM (15), DOI (10). *(§3.3.2, pp.6–7)*
- **Three-perspective factor typology** (Table 12): organizational, IS and user perspectives. *(§3.3.3, pp.7–8)*
- **Methods:** 56% quantitative (62 survey studies), 19% qualitative, 11% mixed, 16% conceptual (Fig. 3; sums to 102% as printed). *(§3.2.2, p.4)* Coverage of at least 39 countries. *(§3.2.3, p.4)*
- **Gap:** "the user perspective was less frequently considered." *(§3.3.5, p.8)*

## 4. DEFINITION
"A business intelligence (BI) system is commonly known as a suite of technological solutions that facilitates organizations to amass, integrate and analyse vast stocks of data in order to understand their opportunities, strengths and weaknesses." *(§1, p.1)* Also: "BI is an information system that supports decision processes by i) facilitating more aggregation, systematic integration and management of unstructured and structured data, ii) dealing with a huge amount of data (e.g. 'Big Data'), iii) providing end-users with increased processing capabilities to discover new knowledge, and iv) offering analysis solutions, ad hoc queries, reporting and forecasting." *(§1, p.1)*
Note: the PDF's own in-text citation numbers appear in these sentences; drop them when quoting. The paper does not define "governance".

## 5. CITABLE
Speaks from the management and IS-design side; its main contribution to this thesis is the finding that users are the neglected perspective.
- "The focus of the majority of studies has remained limited to either organizational or IS-related factors as the most important influencers to AUS." *(§3.3.3, p.7)*
- "From this review's findings, it is notable that the organizational and IS perspectives were considered frequently while investigating the adoption, utilization or success of BI systems while the user perspective was less frequently considered." *(§3.3.5, p.8)*
- Popovič's finding, reported by the authors: "the fear of losing power over information, a change in job skills (e.g. the requirement of new skills to perform routine tasks) and a change in the decision-making approach (e.g. integrating BIS into organizational processes) are the main reasons for users' resistance to a system." *(§3.3.4, p.8)*
- "On the flip side, users' resistance or underutilization of BI systems may result in workflow problems, that ultimately produce strategy blindness and negative business performance." *(§3.3.4, p.10)*
Not for use: a claim that the paper says anything about rules, policy or governance enactment. The word "governance" occurs once, as a construct from Bischoff et al.; the words "policy" and "rules" do not describe any finding.

## 9. LIMITATIONS

1. "Firstly, the literature search was focused on three main categories of BI investigation, specifically the adoption, utilization and success of BI systems." *(p.10)*
2. "Secondly, the focus of the review was limited to BI systems and a limited set of their components, such as data warehouses, OLAP and dashboards." *(p.10)*

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (v2.4-T; focus: what BI systems and BI work are, who uses them, how users are treated in the BI literature)
- **Focal concept and definitions:** see §4 and §10.
- **Main claims:** over 70% of BI projects fail to yield expected returns (§1, p.2); management support, training, organizational culture, well-defined vision and goals, BI and business strategy alignment, data and system quality and IT infrastructure are important to BI AUS (§5, p.11); organisations "need to pay more attention to user-centred issues" (§5, p.11).
- **Mechanisms/antecedents named:** organisational (management support, champion, strategy alignment, culture, training), IS (data and system quality, infrastructure, perceived usefulness and ease of use) and user (IT competencies, analytical skills, self-efficacy, trust, resistance) factors (Table 12, pp.7–8; §3.3.4, p.8; §3.3.5, pp.8–9).
- **User roles:** BI end-users range from managers and executives to BI professionals and analysts (§1 and §3.2.2); reporting users produce standardised outputs and analytical users do deeper analysis (§1; §3.3.5).
- **Evidence base:** SLR of 111 studies, not primary data.
- **Stated limitations:** see §9.
- **Caution:** NotebookLM's §13(b) paraphrase linking "governance and rules/policy impact" to user resistance is an over-reading; the paper does not discuss rules or policy.
