## 1. WHY — Research Problem & Motivation
AI's opacity, autonomy, adaptability, and scalability disrupt traditional corporate governance's ability to assign clear accountability when algorithms output biased or harmful decisions; boards lack structures/digital literacy to respond. The paper aims to outline how governance must evolve into dynamic, proactive ethical guardrails.
*Location: Abstract, p.1141; Introduction, pp.1141–1142.*

## 2. HOW — Method/Design
Qualitative conceptual review proposing a "dynamic governance model." Corpus = major global AI ethics/governance standards (OECD AI Principles, EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 22989:2022, OECD Corporate Governance Principles, national codes) plus secondary survey data (corporate ethical concerns, regulatory readiness, board AI-competency readiness). No primary empirical sample. Reviewed literature spans 2017–2022; received Dec 18 2024, accepted Feb 28 2025.
*Location: Abstract p.1141; Section 2 p.1142; Section 5 p.1144; Section 8 p.1146.*

## 3. WHAT — Key Findings/Frameworks
- Traditional vs. AI-enhanced governance model (manual/retrospective → real-time automated audits, AI ethics boards, shared responsibility). *Table 1, p.1142.*
- Shared Accountability Model mapping legal/ethics, developers, managers, auditors across the AI lifecycle. *Diagram 1, p.1143.*
- Ethical Oversight Framework: AI Ethics Committee, Impact Assessments, Transparency Reports, employee training. *Table 2, pp.1143–1144.*
- Stakeholder Engagement Ecosystem (feedback sessions, town halls, advisory councils). *Diagram 2 & Table 4, pp.1144–1145.*
- AI Risk Lifecycle Management Framework (technical/ethical/strategic risk categories). *Diagram 3 & Table 5, pp.1145–1146.*
- Board AI Governance Competency Typology: AI Literacy, Ethical Reasoning, Risk Oversight, Regulatory Awareness. *Table 6, pp.1146–1147.*

## 4. DEFINITION — Core Governance Construct
Traditional: "...the mechanisms, processes, and relations by which corporations are controlled and directed..." (Introduction, p.1141)
AI-era: "Corporate governance in the AI era is no longer confined to financial integrity or regulatory compliance; it now encompasses algorithmic transparency, ethical responsibility, and proactive risk mitigation." (Conclusion, p.1147)

## 5. CITABLE
- "With increasing reliance on AI, governance structures must accommodate rapid innovation cycles, large-scale data flows, and opaque decision-making systems... organizations are beginning to implement AI ethics boards, create risk protocols specific to AI, and adopt real-time auditing systems." (Section 1, p.1142)
- "...ethical oversight cannot remain reactive or symbolic. It must be structured, continuous, and integrated into organizational governance... Establishing AI Ethics Committees, codifying ethical design practices, and conducting regular audits are no longer optional—they are governance imperatives." (Conclusion, p.1147)
- "...corporate governance in the age of AI is an interdisciplinary endeavor that blends ethics, law, technology, and strategy. It is not merely about controlling risk but about enabling responsible innovation." (Conclusion, p.1147)
