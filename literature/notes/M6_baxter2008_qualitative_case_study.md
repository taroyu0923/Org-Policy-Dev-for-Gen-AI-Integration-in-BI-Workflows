## 1. WHY
The authors offer a guide for novice researchers on designing and implementing qualitative case studies: "The purpose of this paper is to guide the novice researcher in identifying the key elements for designing and implementing qualitative case study research projects." *(Abstract, p.544)* The abstract adds an overview of design types, advice on writing questions and propositions, and clear examples. NotebookLM's framing that novices "misperceive" case study as clinical-only or a teaching tool was not checked against the PDF and is not carried.

## 3. WHAT
- **Case study defined and located** in the constructivist paradigm; contrasts Yin and Stake *(Philosophical underpinnings, p.545)*.
- **When to use it** (Yin 2003, quoted on p.545): "(a) the focus of the study is to answer 'how' and 'why' questions; (b) you cannot manipulate the behaviour of those involved in the study; (c) you want to cover contextual conditions because you believe they are relevant to the phenomenon under study; or (d) the boundaries are not clear between the phenomenon and context."
- **The case / unit of analysis** *(p.545)*; **binding the case** by time and place, time and activity, or definition and context *(pp.546–547)*.
- **Type of case study:** Yin's explanatory, exploratory and descriptive and Stake's intrinsic, instrumental and collective *(Determining the Type of Case Study, pp.547–549)*.
- **Single or multiple designs:** single holistic, single with embedded units, and multiple cases *(pp.549–550)*.
- **Propositions and issues** (pp.551–552); **conceptual framework** (p.553); **data sources and convergence** (p.554); **analysis** (p.555); **reporting** (p.555); **trustworthiness strategies** (pp.555–556).

## 4. DEFINITION
- **Qualitative case study methodology:** "Qualitative case study methodology provides tools for researchers to study complex phenomena within their contexts." *(Abstract, p.544)*
- "This qualitative case study is an approach to research that facilitates exploration of a phenomenon within its context using a variety of data sources." *(Background, p.544; NotebookLM said "p. 25")*
- **The case:** "The case is defined by Miles and Huberman (1994) as, 'a phenomenon of some sort occurring in a bounded context. The case is, "in effect, your unit of analysis"' (p. 25)." *(Determining the Case/Unit of Analysis, p.545; the "p. 25" is the Miles & Huberman page inside the quotation.)*

## 5. CITABLE
This is a case-study methods paper (health sciences and nursing examples); it does not address organisations, AI policy or BI.
- "The ability to look at sub-units that are situated within a larger case is powerful when you consider that data can be analyzed within the subunits separately (within case analysis), between the different subunits (between case analysis), or across all of the subunits (cross-case analysis)." *(Single Case with Embedded Units, p.550)*
- "A multiple or collective case study will allow the researcher to analyze within each setting and across settings. […] Overall, the evidence created from this type of study is considered robust and reliable, but it can also be extremely time consuming and expensive to conduct." *(Multiple-Case Studies, p.550; the omitted middle sentences contrast holistic and multiple designs)*
- "A hallmark of case study research is the use of multiple data sources, a strategy which also enhances data credibility… In case study, data from these multiple sources are then converged in the analysis process rather than handled individually." *(Data Sources, p.554)*
- Yin (2003) on replication, quoted on p.550: multiple case studies can be used to either "(a) predicts similar results (a literal replication) or (b) predicts contrasting results but for predictable reasons (a theoretical replication)" *(Yin, 2003, p.47, quoted on p.550)*.

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (role: Chapter 3 method anchor; focus: single versus multiple, holistic versus embedded designs; binding the case; units of analysis; how it reports Yin's typology)
- **(a) Definitions:** see §4.
- **(b) Main claims:**
  - Design types: single holistic, single with embedded units, multiple (§3, pp.549–550). On multiple cases: "the context is different for each of the cases" *(p.550)*, with literal and theoretical replication (§5).
  - Bounding: "The boundaries indicate what will and will not be studied in the scope of the research project." *(Binding the Case, p.547)* Boundaries by time and place, time and activity, or definition and context (p.546). The paper warns against study "explosion" without boundaries (p.546).
  - The case, not the sub-unit, is the anchor: "The pitfall that novice researchers fall into is that they analyze at the individual subunit level and fail to return to the global issue that they initially set out to address" *(p.550)*.
- **(c) Conditions named:** propositions and a conceptual framework to limit scope (pp.551–553); converging data sources rather than reporting them separately (pp.554–555); the trustworthiness strategies (pp.555–556; the section names credibility, transferability, dependability and confirmability).
- **(d) Evidence base:** conceptual guide with illustrative examples; see §2.
- **(e) Limitations:** see §9.
- **(f) What a careful examiner would expect** — verbatim:
  - "novice researchers have a responsibility to ensure that: (a) the case study research question is clearly written, propositions (if appropriate to the case study type) are provided, and the question is substantiated; (b) case study design is appropriate for the research question; (c) purposeful sampling strategies appropriate for case study have been applied; (d) data are collected and managed systematically; and (e) the data are analyzed correctly" *(p.556)*
  - "The boundaries indicate what will and will not be studied in the scope of the research project." *(p.547)*
  - "One danger associated with the analysis phase is that each data source would be treated independently and the findings reported separately. This is not the purpose of a case study. Rather, the researcher must ensure that the data are converged in an attempt to understand the overall case…" *(Analysis, p.555)*
  - "The goal of the report is to describe the study in such a comprehensive manner as to enable the reader to feel as if they had been an active participant in the research and can determine whether or not the study findings could be applied to their own situation. It is important that the researcher describes the context within which the phenomenon is occurring as well as the phenomenon itself." *(Reporting a Case Study, p.555; NotebookLM said "p. 5")*
  - "In order to fully understand the findings they are compared and contrasted to what can be found in published literature in order to situate the new data into preexisting data." *(p.555)*
  - "The pitfall in the report writing that many novice researchers fall into is being distracted by the mounds of interesting data that are superfluous to the research question." *(p.555)*
  - The embedded-unit pitfall quote in (b) also belongs here (p.550).
- **Ties to the thesis's design:** the paper's expectations bear on the depth (Depth A, Depth B) and breadth cases and their embedded participants in `sampling_frame.md`: name the case, its bounds and its sub-units, and analyse across them rather than only within.
