## 1. WHY
Prior HCI research on artists and generative AI relies on cross-sectional surveys or short-term lab experiments, offering only "static snapshots" of a fast-changing phenomenon, unable to capture how attitudes, collaborative practices, and ethical/value frameworks evolve over time (Introduction, p. 2; Related Work, pp. 3–4). This study addresses the gap via a five-year (2021–2025) longitudinal mixed-methods study tracing digital painters' relationships with generative AI (p. 2).

## 2. HOW
Five-year longitudinal mixed-methods design (2021–2025) (Methodology, pp. 5–7). Annual quantitative surveys (11-point Likert, 0–10) plus 45–60 min semi-structured online interviews. Baseline sample: 17 Chinese digital painters (11 professional, 6 hobbyist); attrition to 15 (2022), 14 (2023), 13 (2024 and 2025). Recruitment via WeChat, Xiaohongshu, Bilibili, Baidu Tieba, Weibo (2021).

## 3. WHAT
- **Three-phase attitude trajectory**: "strong resistance" (2021–2022) → "instrumental/pragmatic adoption" (2022–2024) → "reflective reconsideration and identity reconstruction" (2025) (pp. 13–18).
- **"Identity and Value Negotiation" theoretical lens**: technology adoption as ongoing negotiation of identity/labor/values rather than binary acceptance/rejection (p. 2; p. 21).
- **Social-Emotional Resonance Framework**: adoption co-constructed by peer/community normative pressure and a curiosity→frustration→excitement→fatigue emotional arc (p. 21).
- Professional painters adopted faster/more pragmatically (survival/competition) than hobbyists (p. 12; p. 21).
- Copyright/authorship concerns (Q10) rose from 7.82/10 (2021) to a stable 9.57/10 plateau (2024–2025) (p. 11).
- "Controlled Failure" design concept: future creative-support systems should preserve AI errors as sparks for human exploration (p. 22).

## 4. DEFINITION
No organizational governance construct. Core construct: **identity and value negotiation** — "an ongoing negotiation of identity and values rather than a simple binary of acceptance or rejection" (Introduction, p. 2); "AI technology adoption is not merely about utility, but about the ongoing reconstruction of identity, value, and social relationships" (Discussion, p. 21). Closest governance-adjacent concept: copyright, framed under a "3C" (consent, credit, compensation) lens (Table 2, p. 7).

## 5. CITABLE

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
Contains a **Table 2 survey instrument and Appendix 1 interview guide** with directly reusable items.

**Policy Framework Development**
- Survey Q10 (Table 2, p. 7): "The copyright and authorship of AI-generated works are important issues that need clarification."
- Survey Q7: "Using AI may lead to homogenization of artworks and reduce individual style." (useful for human-in-the-loop policy)
- Interview C8 (Appendix 1, p. 28): "Is it unethical to train models on data without authorization?"
- C9: "Should artists have the right to decide whether their works are used for training?"
- C10: "Should copyright of AI-generated works belong to the user?"
- D3: "What do you see as the most urgent ethical and copyright issues, and what solutions would you propose?"

**Implementation & Change Management**
- Survey Q6: "I am willing to spend time learning how to use generative AI effectively."
- Interview B4 (Appendix 1, p. 28): "At which stages of creation have you used AI? (e.g., ideation, early references, sketching, composition, rendering, post-processing, direct image generation)" — adaptable to "At which stages of data analytics have you used AI — data preparation, querying, visualization, or reporting?"
- B5: "How have you integrated AI content into your work? (inspiration only, redraw based on reference, modify base image, partial use, minor adjustments, direct adoption)"
- C18: "Do you feel pressured to learn new technologies because of AI?"

**Organizational Adaptation Dynamics**
- Survey Q8/Q9 (Table 2, p. 7): AI competitiveness and market-demand effects.
- C12: "Will generative AI have disruptive impacts on the industry?"
- C13: "Is AI mainly a threat to lower-tier commercial art?" — adaptable to "Is AI mainly a threat to entry-level data-processing roles?"
- D2: "What are the greatest challenges AI poses (or is expected to pose) for artists?"
- D5: "What would you like to say to artists who reject AI and to those who fully rely on it?" — probes long-term organizational polarization.

## 9. LIMITATIONS
- Modest sample and attrition: baseline 17, declining to 13 by 2024–2025 (Section 6.4, pp. 23–24).
- Geocultural constraints: confined to Chinese digital-art landscape; industrialization/copyright/policy context limits generalizability (p. 24).
- Single creative domain: digital visual art only; unclear transfer to music, literature, fashion (p. 24).
- Assumed conceptual stability ("semantic drift"): terms like "originality" may have shifted meaning 2021–2025 (p. 24).
- Retrospective/affective bias in later-year recall of past emotions (p. 24).
- Cannot cleanly isolate socio-cultural attitude shifts from concurrent model-capability maturation (p. 24).
