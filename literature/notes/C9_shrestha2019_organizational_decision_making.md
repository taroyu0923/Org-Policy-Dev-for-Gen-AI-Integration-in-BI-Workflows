## 1. WHY
Rise of AI decision algorithms introduces new organizational-design challenges beyond well-understood human-actor decision structures; managers remain responsible despite hidden bias/opacity risks. *Location: Summary p.66; Intro pp.66-67.*

## 2. HOW
Conceptual synthesis / theoretical framework paper; no empirical dataset, drawing on organization theory, microeconomics, cognitive science, ML literature. *Location: Summary p.66; Sec. "How Do Human and AI Compare?" pp.67-70.*

## 3. WHAT
- 5 decision-making contingency conditions comparing human vs. AI (search-space specificity, interpretability, alternative-set size, speed, replicability). *(pp.67-70, Table 1)*
- Three structural categories: Full Human-to-AI Delegation, Hybrid Sequential, Aggregated Human-AI. *(pp.70-76, Table 2)*

## 4. DEFINITION
"how to structure organizational decision making—that is, designing where, when, and how to make and integrate decisions involving groups of individuals" *(Intro, p.66)*

## 5. CITABLE
Executive/organizational-design perspective, not frontline empirical.
- "managers can delegate authority to AI, but not responsibility." *(Sec. Full Delegation, p.73)*
- "In introducing AI to organizational decision making, managers must build internal capabilities to decide on the inputs to the algorithm, the algorithms themselves, and the interpretation of predictions." *(Conclusion, p.78)*
- "How does the loss of decision-making authority to AI influence the motivation and performance of human decision makers?" *(Conclusion, p.78)*
