## 1. WHY
Generic adoption models (TAM, TOE, DOI) treat organizational size as one continuous dimension and miss GenAI-specific, scale-dependent dynamics (p. 1, 3). Midsize firms lack specialized AI talent/infrastructure; large enterprises face bureaucracy and cross-unit coordination problems (p. 1–2). Needs a dedicated, scale-aware GenAI framework (Abstract, p. 1; Section 1, p. 1–2; Section 2.1, p. 3).

## 2. HOW
Conceptual/perspective study synthesizing TAM, TOE, DOI, Kotter's 8-Step, McKinsey 7S, Resource-Based View, Absorptive Capacity (p. 1, Section 3, p. 5). No empirical dataset. Proposes future mixed-methods validation: documentary evidence, semi-structured stakeholder interviews, longitudinal case studies, and a three-round modified Delphi expert panel (Section 5.1/5.3, p. 16, 18; Section 6.4, p. 22).

## 3. WHAT
- **FAIGMOE 4-phase model**: Strategic Assessment → Planning & Use Case Development → Implementation & Integration → Operationalization & Optimization, with continuous feedback loops (p. 1, Section 3.3, p. 7–8).
- 4 integration dimensions: Strategic, Operational, Technical, Cultural — scaled dynamically by maturity (Section 4.1, p. 10–11).
- Midsize vs. enterprise typology: midsize → cloud-native/vendor partnerships; enterprise → formal program governance/legacy integration (Section 2.2, p. 3; Section 6.2, p. 20–21).
- GenAI-specific catalog: prompt engineering, model orchestration, RAG, hallucination management (Section 6.1, p. 18–19).

## 9. LIMITATIONS (self-stated, Section 6.4, p. 22)
- Requires comprehensive empirical validation across diverse organizations/industries; longitudinal studies needed.
- Highly regulated sectors need additional compliance protocols beyond current scope.
- Assumes baseline digital maturity (cloud connectivity, data management, cybersecurity) — orgs lacking this need prior digital transformation.
- Assumes baseline leadership commitment and change-management capability.
- Faces ongoing maintenance challenges from rapid GenAI evolution (agentic AI, advanced reasoning models).
- Scale boundaries: very small orgs (<50 employees) may find it overly complex; very large multinationals may need additional coordination mechanisms.
