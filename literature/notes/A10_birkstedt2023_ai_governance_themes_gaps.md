## 1. WHY
"Principles-to-practices gap" in AI ethics; AIG literature is fragmented, conceptual, and lacks organizational-level synthesis. *Location: Sec.1, p.134; Abstract p.133.*

## 2. HOW
SLR (PRISMA + Kitchenham). Databases: Scopus, WoS, ACM DL. 2010–2021. 1,071 initial hits → 68 final articles (52 journal, 16 conference; 61 conceptual, 4 reviews, 3 empirical). *Location: Sec.2, pp.134-135; Sec.2.1, p.135; Sec.2.2-2.3, pp.135-137.*

## 3. WHAT
- Four themes: Technology, Stakeholders & Context, Regulation, Processes. *(Sec.3.2, p.142, Fig.6)*
- Four knowledge gaps. *(Sec.4, pp.149-153)*
- Four future agendas. *(Sec.5.1, pp.153-157)*
- AI Oversight Unit (AOU) proposal, analogous to GDPR DPO. *(Sec.5.1.4, p.156)*
- Collaborative/polycentric governance. *(Sec.5.1.2, p.156)*

## 4. DEFINITION
"AI governance is a system of rules, practices, processes and technological tools that are employed to ensure an organization's use of AI technologies aligns with the organization's strategies, objectives, and values; fulfills legal requirements; and meets principles of ethical AI followed by the organization." *(Sec.5.1 p.154, citing Mäntymäki et al. 2022a)*

## 5. CITABLE
61/68 papers conceptual — explicit empirical void on individual enactment noted.
- "AI development practitioners play a mediating role by implementing ethics within AI solutions, while the parameters are set by legislation, organizational norms and clients." *(Sec.3.2.2, p.144)*
- "A power imbalance also exists between the actors who set specifications for AI systems...and the practitioners who implement them." *(Sec.3.2.2, p.145)*
- "...empirical research is required to understand how organizations deploy AIG processes...and how individuals enact AIG practices." *(Sec.5.1.4, p.157)*
- "such examinations will also help organizations understand the effects of their AIG initiatives over a specific period, thereby creating a feedback loop." *(Sec.5.1.4, p.157)*
