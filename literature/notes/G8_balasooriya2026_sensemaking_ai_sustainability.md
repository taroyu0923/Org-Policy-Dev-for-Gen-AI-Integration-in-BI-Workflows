## 1. WHY
AI integration into sustainability efforts is limited and ad hoc, and prior work centres on technology rather than on how organisations make sense of and embed AI. *Location: abstract p.7916; introduction p.7917; research question p.7917: "What are the salient strategies that organizations could employ in integrating AI for sustainability initiatives".*

## 2. HOW
Qualitative, interpretive multiple-case study (two Australian manufacturers, X apparel and Y fast-moving consumer goods), with sensemaking theory as a sensitizing concept (p.7916 and the Theory section). **10 semi-structured interviews, five per organisation**: "the CEO of the organization, two employees from the top management, and two employees from the middle management" (p.7920); "The interviews ranged in duration from 45 min to over 1 h and were conducted virtually in English" (p.7920). Data collected January to March 2025 (p.7919). Table 1 (p.7920) lists participants: per organisation a CEO, directors and managers (for example Chief executive officer, Director of operations, Director of supply chain, Production manager, Quality assurance manager at X). Coding in NVivo with an intercoder agreement of 82% (p.7920).

## 3. WHAT
- Four AI-driven strategies, each tied to a sensemaking mechanism: Strategy 1 "Seeing the Unseen With AI for Optimization" (p.7923, extraction of environmental cues); Strategy 2 "Increasing Consumption Consciousness With AI, Reducing Waste" (p.7924, plausibility-driven action); Strategy 3 "Practicing Responsive Stewardship With AI Leading to Higher Productivity" (p.7925, retrospective reinterpretation); Strategy 4 "Weaving Ethical Interdependence to Improve Compliance" (p.7925, enactment of ethical environments).
- Nine axial codes feed the four themes (p.7920).

## 4. DEFINITION
"According to Maitlis (2005), organizational sensemaking is the process by which individuals interpret their environment by engaging with others, slowing for coordinated action and shared understanding" ("slowing" as printed) *(p.7923)*.
"According to Seidel et al. (2018), sensemaking is an organizational, process-oriented approach grounded in social constructivism, involving both retrospective and prospective possibilities" *(p.7923)*.

## 5. CITABLE
The paper is not about generative AI, policy, or analytical staff; participants are executives and managers.
- Abstract: "The findings reveal that integrating AI for sustainability practices is a dynamic sensemaking process that reconfigures how organizations interpret value, responsibility, and interdependencies across the broader ecosystem" *(p.7916)*
- "Organizations often integrate AI technologies reactively, driven by industry trends, competitive pressures, and internal aspirations to be perceived as innovative" *(p.7917)*
- Interview quotes: "It's not AI deciding alone, we look at the suggestions and then take the final call" *(p.7930)*; "Our AI systems do not replace our teams, they guide them. We are giving them tools that improve their ability to make responsible and solid decisions" *(p.7921)*; "When the AI flags something and we give feedback, it learns from us too. That back- and- forth really reduces mistakes on both sides" *(p.7930; hyphenation as printed)*.
