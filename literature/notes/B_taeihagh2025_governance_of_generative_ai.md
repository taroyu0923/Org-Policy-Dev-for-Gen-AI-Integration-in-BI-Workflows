## 1. WHY
AI governance literature lags rapid real-world advancement and diffusion of generative AI (p. 2). Since ChatGPT's late-2022 release, GenAI's unique risks (hallucination, data bias, opacity, Big Tech power imbalance) exceed what reactive, technocratic regulation can handle (p. 2–3). Need to shift from narrow "governance fixes" to proactive, adaptive, participatory governance (p. 6).

## 2. HOW
Conceptual introductory review/synthesis for a special issue (p. 1). Synthesizes foundational AI-risk literature + introduces 7 peer-reviewed special-issue papers (p. 1–2, p. 11). Builds on 2021 special issue on narrow-AI governance through early-2025 developments (p. 1, 7).

## 3. WHAT
- **Typology of 7 GenAI risks** (Table 1): hallucination/inaccuracies, jailbreaking, data training/validation risks, sensitive information risks, opacity risks, control risks, design/implementation risks (p. 2–4).
- **Comprehensive governance framework** (Table 2): 8 operational areas — regulatory sandboxes, mandatory training-data disclosure, independent audits, AI literacy, international standard-setting (p. 6–10).
- Synthesis of paradigms across special-issue papers: traditional IT governance vs. CAS-based governance (Janssen 2025); Big Tech as "super policy entrepreneurs" (Khanal et al. 2025); technocratic "governance fix" vs. democratic/participatory models (Ulnicane 2025) (p. 11–16).

## 4. DEFINITION
> Generative AI: "a category of AI systems that create new content (text, images, audio, or video) based on inputs, leveraging ML, particularly GANs, VAEs, LLMs, and diffusion models." (p. 2)
> Adaptive governance requires "an approach that facilitates iteratively responding to new risks and challenges swiftly and ensuring that regulations do not become obsolete due to the pace of advancements." (p. 6)
> "Governance fix" (Ulnicane 2025) = "the application of a technocratic and expert-driven governance approach to manage risks with limited deliberation and democratic engagement." (p. 13)
