## 1. WHY
Top management's time and attention are scarce resources; earlier strategy research concentrated on decision execution rather than on how issues reach the executive agenda. The paper examines how middle-level managers direct top management's attention through upward influence, interpretation and framing at the early, identification stage of decision-making. *Location: Abstract p.397; Introduction pp.397–399.*

## 2. HOW
Conceptual paper. No empirical sample, participants, setting or data collection. It integrates three literatures, social problem theory, impression management and upward influence, into a framework and a set of numbered propositions. *Location: Abstract p.397; Introduction p.399; pp.402–404.*

## 3. WHAT
- **Significance matrix:** individual versus organisational levels and instrumental versus symbolic logics (Fig. 1, p.400).
- **Issue-selling framework (Fig. 2, p.405):** choices about initiation, packaging (framing, presentation, appeals, bundling) and the selling process (involvement of others, channels, formality), leading to top management's attention and to change in the seller's credibility.
- **Propositions 1–17** (with sub-parts 14a/b, 15a/b, 16a/b, 17a/b), pp.409–420. (NotebookLM said 16; the PDF numbers 1–17.)
- The link from attention to substantive action is deliberately not modelled (body text, p.404).

## 4. DEFINITION
"Issue selling refers to individuals' behaviors that are directed toward affecting others' attention to and understanding of issues." *(Introduction, p.398)*
"We see issue selling as a critical activity in the early stages of organizational decision-making processes." *(Abstract, p.397)* And: "We envision issue selling as a critical activity in the early stages of an organization's decision-making process. It is a process by which middle-level managers attempt to influence the identification phase of organizational decision making." *(p.399)*

## 5. CITABLE
The paper is about strategic issues in general, not AI policy.
- "the process by which issues are sold in organizations is an important arena for studying the more general processes of interpretation and the construction of meaning in organizations" *(p.398)*
- "an issue-selling perspective underscores the roles that middle-level employees play in the meaning-creation process. Although individuals vary in how skillfully they can frame issues, these skills can be taught to lower level managers by organizations interested in empowering their lower ranks in order to create a more adaptive organization." *(p.422)*
- "Our model of issue selling suggests that when top managers devote attention to an issue, they may activate other organizational members to try to initiate subsequent issue-selling episodes that shape both the way top management understands an issue and the likely course of action that they take with respect to it." *(p.422)*

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
No interview instrument (conceptual). Constructs usable for interview design, as NotebookLM's mapping onto the three phases; none is content of the paper as a question.
- **Policy Encounter & Interpretation:** perceived supportiveness and openness of top management (Proposition 2, p.409); how an issue is packaged, for example as threat or opportunity or as top management's responsibility (Propositions 7 and 8, p.414).
- **Implementation & Change Management:** attaching a solution to an issue before raising it (Proposition 3, p.410); public versus private channels and formal versus informal tactics (Propositions 16a/b, 17a/b, pp.419–420).
- **Organizational Adaptation Dynamics:** signs that top management has attended: "(a) the naming of an issue, (b) collection of issue-relevant information, (c) conversations about the issue, or (d) creation of either roles or task forces devoted to the issue" (p.404); credibility loss as a risk for the seller (Proposition 8, p.414).

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (v2.4-T; focus: definition of issue selling, when employees sell issues upward, the moves and packaging, conditions that favour or block it)
- **Definitions:** §4.
- **Main claims (verbatim propositions):**
  - Proposition 2: "The initiation of issue selling is more frequent when top management is perceived as more supportive and open." *(p.409)*
  - Proposition 3: "The initiation of issue selling is more frequent when an issue seller can identify a solution to attach to the issue." *(p.410)*
  - Proposition 7: "The more an issue seller attempts to get an issue framed through time in a manner that implies top management's responsibility, the greater the level of top management's attention invested in an issue." *(p.414)*
  - Proposition 8: "The more an issue seller frames an issue in a manner that implies top management's responsibility, the more likely it is that the seller will lose credibility for future selling attempts." *(p.414)*
  - Proposition 15a: "The more an issue seller brings others on board to help sell his or her issue, the greater the level of top management's attention invested in the issue." *(p.418)*
  - Proposition 16a: "The more an issue seller uses public channels to sell his or her issue, the greater the level of top management's attention invested in an issue." *(p.419)*
  - Proposition 1 (p.409) is a seven-part statement about seller values, general-manager status, functional match, credibility, network centrality and perceived impression-management and performance risk.
- **Mechanisms:** perceived openness of top management, seller position and credibility, solution availability, perceived personal and performance risk, and packaging and process choices (pp.409–420).
- **Evidence base:** conceptual; no empirical data.
- **Stated limitations:** §9.
- **Caution:** NotebookLM labelled the openness proposition "Proposition 1" and gave p.406; both were wrong (see `_raw/G3.md`).
