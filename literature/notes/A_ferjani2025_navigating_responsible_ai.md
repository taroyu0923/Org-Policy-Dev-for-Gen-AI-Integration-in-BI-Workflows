## 1. WHY
Traditional governance frameworks for passive tools cannot handle dynamic, autonomous AI systems; regulatory cycles lag technical progress (Section 1, p. 4726–4727). Persistent implementation gap between abstract ethics and operational deployment, worsened by exclusion of underrepresented stakeholders (indigenous communities, SMEs, non-Western regions) from Western-dominated frameworks (Abstract, p. 4726; Section 1, p. 4727; Section 8, p. 4734).

## 2. HOW
SLR + scenario planning (Section 2, p. 4728). 75 peer-reviewed studies (Abstract, p. 4726; Section 2.2, p. 4728). Databases: Web of Science, Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, arXiv (Section 2.1, p. 4728). Time span 2010–2024. PRISMA (Cohen's kappa 0.84), MMAT quality tool, 65% high quality (Section 2.1–2.2, p. 4728).

## 3. WHAT
- **"Governance Galaxy" framework**: human-AI co-governance core + 4 thematic clusters (ethics, regulation, technology, global coordination) + stakeholder "planets" + scenario "orbital pathways" + solution "moons" (Section 1, p. 4727; Section 7.1, p. 4732).
- Thematic split: Ethics 53%, Regulation 43%, Technical 32%, Global Coordination 27% (Section 5, Table 2, p. 4730).
- Only 33% (13/40) of ethics-focused studies give implementation tools (Section 5, p. 4730–4731).
- Three 2035 scenarios: Utopian / Dystopian / Fragmented (Section 5.2, p. 4731).
- Staged roadmap: Foundation (2025–29), Transition (2029–32), Mature Co-Governance (2032–35) (Section 7.2–7.3, p. 4733–4734).

## 4. DEFINITION
> Core construct — co-governance: "envisioning AI as a potential collaborative partner in governance by 2035" (Section 1, p. 4727), operationalized as "formalized mechanisms for AI systems to contribute analytical perspectives in governance structures while maintaining human authority over fundamental values and governance parameters." (Section 7.3, p. 4733)
