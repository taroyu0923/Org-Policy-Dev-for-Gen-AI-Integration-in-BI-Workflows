## 1. WHY
Employees increasingly build, introduce or use IT without approval to get their jobs done; CIOs lose control, security and compliance risks grow, and data analytics quality suffers. Neighbouring concepts (BYO, workarounds, IT consumerisation) overlap with shadow IT but do not capture it. *Location: §1 p.469; §2 pp.469–470.*

## 2. HOW
Conceptual state-of-the-art overview under the BISE "Catchword" rubric. Not an empirical study or systematic review; no data, database or corpus. It compares shadow IT with neighbouring constructs, sets out a multidimensional framework (Fig. 2) and a research agenda. *Location: header and §1 p.469; §§2–4 pp.469–472.*

## 3. WHAT
- **Delineation of shadow IT** against target IT, personal IT (BYO), workarounds and IT consumerisation, by provision, approval and obstacle circumvention (Fig. 1, p.470).
- **Four-dimension framework (Fig. 2, p.471):** causes (person, situation), usage (overt, covert), consequences (favourable, unfavourable; individual, group, organisation, society) and shadow IT governance.
- **Overt versus covert use** (p.472): managers may ignore overt use when enforcement costs exceed benefits; users may conceal use where enforcement is consistent.
- **Vicious circle of restriction** (p.472): see §5.

## 4. DEFINITION
"shadow IT which is hardware, software, or services built, introduced, and/or used for the job without explicit approval or even knowledge of the organization." *(§1, p.469)*
"employees can use shadow IT, that is, they themselves either bring unapproved IT and/or change approved IT in unapproved ways (e.g., Györy et al. 2012), for example, by creating MS Excel macros without approval to automate repetitive work tasks." *(§2, p.470; the word "either" is in the PDF)*
IT policies: "formal and/or informal IT policies including rules, guidelines, standards, and procedures of how they expect users to use target IT…" *(§2, p.470)*

## 5. CITABLE
The source frames governance from the management side (restrict, allow within boundaries, encourage), but it says how employees meet rules.
- "If employed users perceive the target IT, personal IT, and/or the IT policies as obstacles to task performance, they can create a workaround to circumvent the perceived obstacle and perform the work task by other means (Ferneley and Sobreperez 2006; Alter 2014)." *(§2, p.470; the PDF says "employed users", not "employees")*
- "technologically blocking or organizationally restricting shadow IT usage is certainly possible, but is not a reliable and sustainable solution. Rather, blocking or restricting shadow IT can lead to a circular problem in form of an impasse: Employees who already use shadow IT because they perceive the target IT and/or the organization's IT policies as obstacles to successfully performing their tasks may face an even larger obstacle if the organization blocks this shadow IT solution and/or tightens the IT policies concerning shadow IT. As a consequence, such restrictions may not limit shadow IT, but rather reinforce it by pushing shadow IT usage into secrecy and thus the users and their behaviors, figuratively, into the shadows." *(§4, p.472)*
- "By contrast, if IT managers collectively and consistently enforce target IT usage, users may conceal their shadow IT usage to avoid punishment for policy-breaking (Martin et al. 2013)." *(§4, p.472)* Separately: "However, when users still deploy these same IT solutions as shadow IT in secrecy, these shadow IT solutions could later result in outcomes that the society acknowledges as useful and a breakthrough." *(§4, p.472; a later passage, do not join the two with an ellipsis)*

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
No interview questions or instruments (conceptual paper). The paper is from 2017 and does not use "AI" or "generative AI"; mapping to the three phases is NotebookLM's proposal.
- **Policy Encounter & Interpretation:** whether people experience policy as enabling or as an obstacle to task performance (§2, p.470); whether people can tell approved from unapproved tools (definition, p.469).
- **Implementation & Change Management:** the three workaround means, non-IT workarounds, repurposing approved software, deploying unapproved tools (§2, p.470); social pressure from colleagues as a driver (p.470); governance postures restrict, allow within boundaries, encourage (§3, p.471).
- **Organizational Adaptation Dynamics:** the vicious circle of tightening policy (p.472); overt versus covert use (p.472); bimodal or separate digital units as a way to reduce obstacle perceptions (p.472).

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (v2.4-T; focus: definition of shadow IT, why employees use it, consequences, how organisations respond)
- **Definitions:** §4.
- **Main claims:** shadow IT boosts job and business performance ("used to boost job and business performance") while creating "greater and greater risks for information systems (IS) security" (§1, p.469); it is distinct from BYO, consumerisation and workarounds (§2, pp.469–470); restrictive governance can reinforce it (§4, p.472).
- **Antecedents:** person-related (technical skills, creativity, wish to perform, fear of punishment driving secrecy) and situation-related (target IT or policy perceived as an obstacle, peer pressure, cheap on-demand cloud services, slow official IT request processes) (§§1–4, pp.469–472).
- **Evidence base:** conceptual; secondary evidence cited, for example Segal (2016) "80% of end users and 90% of functional managers" and a lab experiment by Haag et al. (2015) (p.469, reference list p.472).
- **Stated limitations:** none stated.
- **Relevance to K5/K11 (reading of the text, not a quotation):** the source's use of shadow IT presupposes an existing policy or approval requirement; it says nothing about use before any rule existed.
