## 1. WHY
Intended strategies produce unintended outcomes; the paper looks at how change recipients' sensemaking, especially among middle managers, mediates implementation. *Location: introduction, pp.1573–1574.*

## 2. HOW
"qualitative embedded single case study design" (p.1576), real-time and interpretive. Setting: a privatised UK utility, "Anonco" (pseudonym), restructured into three divisions (pp.1576, 1578). **26 middle managers were diarists** from a group of about 90 (p.1577). Implementation began in March 1993 with three divisional directors (p.1576) and tracking continued until July 1994 (p.1577), about 16 months. (NotebookLM's Q2 said 12 months; that is wrong.) Figure 4 gives time period T1 as April–September 1993 (p.1590).

## 3. WHAT
- Middle managers' interpretations arise "primarily from lateral, informal social processes of interaction between themselves" *(p.1574)*.
- Second-order model linking social processes of interaction, developing schemata and change consequences, congruent or counteracting (p.1587).
- Design flaws and "hard levers" as triggers (hard levers p.1578).
- Illustration of interlinked counteracting and congruent consequences (p.1581).

## 4. DEFINITION
"Sensemaking is primarily a conversational and narrative process … involving a variety of communication genre … both spoken and written, and formal and informal" *(p.1576, with its citations omitted here)*.
Schemata: "the mental models held by individuals that affect the events individuals respond to and how" *(p.1575)*.
"Social processes of interaction are the conversational and social practices identified from the first-order analysis…"; "Developing schemata are the interpretations that change recipients arrive at, through their social processes of interaction, of what change is about" *(p.1587)*.

## 5. CITABLE
The paper is about corporate restructuring, not AI.
- "The senior managers provide a blueprint for change, but the way the blueprint actually operates is determined by the new behavioural routines created by the change recipients through their interpretation of and response to senior management change plans" *(p.1596)*
- "the greatest amount of middle manager sensemaking activity occurs through these lateral and largely informal middle manager processes in the absence of more senior managers" *(p.1595)*
- "'managing' change is less about directing and controlling and more about facilitating recipient sensemaking processes to achieve an alignment of interpretation" *(p.1596)*; senior management "striving to deliver clarity of purpose, expected outcomes and boundary conditions" *(p.1596; "simple rules" and "boundary conditions" also p.1596)*
