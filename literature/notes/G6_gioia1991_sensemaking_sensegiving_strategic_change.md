## 1. WHY
Strategic change had been studied mainly as formulation and implementation; the paper asks how a CEO instigates it, using a grounded, interpretive approach. *Location: introduction, pp.433–434.*

## 2. HOW
Ethnographic study of the initiation of a strategic change effort in a large public university (p.433), with an "insider" and an "outsider" researcher (p.436). Informants "engage in a stream of consciousness" in open interviews (p.437). The on-site research ended 2.5 years after the new President arrived (p.436); the four phases below "covered about 12 months" (p.438). *(NotebookLM's "hold-out informant" was not found in the PDF and is not used.)*

## 3. WHAT
- **Four phases of strategic change initiation:** "The initial stage of the change effort progressed from an 'Envisioning' to a 'Signaling' to a 'Re-Visioning' and eventually to an 'Energizing' phase" *(p.438)*. Envisioning p.438, Signaling p.439, Re-Visioning p.439, Energizing p.441.
- **Second-order concepts:** sensemaking and sensegiving (p.442) as a cycle between leader and stakeholders (pp.442–444).
- **Ambiguity-by-design:** "existing schemes that are entrenched by tradition can be eased toward fluidity by intentionally creating, but carefully managing, the ambiguity" *(p.446; first described p.439)*.
- **Symbolic vision:** the "Top 10" public-university image (pp.436, 439, 442, 445).

## 4. DEFINITION
"strategic change involves an attempt to change current modes of cognition and action to enable the organization to take advantage of important opportunities or to cope with consequential environmental threats" *(p.433)*.
"'sensemaking' has to do with meaning construction and reconstruction by the involved parties as they attempted to develop a meaningful framework for understanding the nature of the intended strategic change" *(p.442)*.
"'Sensegiving' is concerned with the process of attempting to influence the sensemaking and meaning construction of others toward a preferred redefinition of organizational reality" *(p.442)*.

## 5. CITABLE
The paper is about university strategic change, not technology policy.
- "the targets of the President's sensegiving attempts would make their own interpretations or sense of these proposals and actions, and attempt to communicate their revised understanding of, or preferences for, the new organizational reality back to the President and the top management team" *(p.443)*
- "The feedback loop indicates that the sensegiving activities of the various stakeholders also leads to some modifications to the espoused vision" *(p.443)*
- "Ultimately, strategic change is a negotation process" [sic, as printed]; "In this negotiation process each group tries to sell its vision of the future to the others (sensegiving), even as it is engaged in the process of trying to figure out what the others want and to ascribe meaning to it (sensemaking)" *(p.446)*

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (v2.4-T; focus: sensegiving and sensemaking in change initiation, the stakeholder response, the feedback to leaders)
- **Definitions:** §4.
- **Main claims:** the four-phase model (p.438); the sensemaking/sensegiving cycle (pp.442–444); change as a negotiated process (p.446): "The CEO (and ultimately the top management team) can be seen as architects, assimilators, and facilitators of strategic change" *(p.446)*.
- **Mechanisms and conditions:** ambiguity-by-design (p.439, p.446); symbolic vision (p.445); widening consultation through hypothetical scenarios (p.441); non-crisis context (p.445).
- **Evidence base:** empirical, single-site ethnography (§2).
- **Stated limitations:** §9.
- **Caution:** this thesis's terms (working rule, carriers/recipients, receiving end) are not Gioia & Chittipeddi's. The study tracks executive and dean-level informants, not frontline staff.
