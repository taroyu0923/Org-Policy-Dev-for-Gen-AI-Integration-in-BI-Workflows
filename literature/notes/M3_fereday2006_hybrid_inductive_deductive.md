## 1. WHY
The study addressed performance feedback as an input to nurses' self-assessment of competence in South Australia (guidance from the Nurses Board of South Australia, 2000): "a review of the literature highlighted limited research to support the utility of feedback in this context." *(Introduction, p.80; NotebookLM's "pp.1–2" are PDF pages.)* The methodological aim, as the paper states it, is to detail its "step-by-step process of analysis … to demonstrate rigor using a hybrid approach to thematic analysis" *(p.81)*, giving "a trail of evidence" *(pp.81, 84)* from raw data to themes.

## 2. HOW
Qualitative interpretive study grounded in Schutz's social phenomenology. Data: "10 focus groups and a document analysis of 16 policies or procedures", managed in QSR NVivo. The code template "was developed a priori, based on the research question and the theoretical framework." *(p.83)* No databases searched. The paper's interview questions (p.86) are its own nursing feedback questions.

## 3. WHAT
Six stages, printed pages *(Stages of data coding, pp.84–91)*:
1. Developing the code manual (p.84)
2. Testing the reliability of the code (p.85)
3. Summarizing data and identifying initial themes (p.86)
4. Applying template of codes and additional coding (p.87)
5. Connecting the codes and identifying themes (p.89)
6. Corroborating and legitimating coded themes (p.90)
The method combines Boyatzis's data-driven inductive approach with Crabtree & Miller's deductive a priori template (definition, §4). Each code has three parts: "the code label or name", "the definition of what the theme concerns", and "a description of how to know when the theme occurs" *(pp.84–85; NotebookLM said pp.4–5)*. *(The nursing-specific findings on feedback credibility and "Familiarity" were not checked and are not used.)*

## 4. DEFINITION
- **Hybrid approach (the paper's own words):** "The method of analysis chosen for this study was a hybrid approach of qualitative methods of thematic analysis, and it incorporated both the data-driven inductive approach of Boyatzis (1998) and the deductive a priori template of codes approach outlined by Crabtree and Miller (1999). This approach complemented the research questions by allowing the tenets of social phenomenology to be integral to the process of deductive thematic analysis while allowing for themes to emerge direct from the data using inductive coding." *(runs from p.82 to p.83; a page header falls inside the sentence, and the PDF prints "data -driven" with a stray space)*
- **Introduction wording:** "a combined technique of inductive and deductive thematic analysis" *(p.81)*.
- **Thematic analysis:** "Thematic analysis is a search for themes that emerge as being important to the description of the phenomenon (Daly, Kellehear, & Gliksman, 1997)." *(p.82)* Note the word "emerge": Braun & Clarke (2021, M2, p.343) object to this framing for reflexive TA.
- **Code:** "The coding process involved recognizing (seeing) an important moment and encoding it (seeing it as something) prior to a process of interpretation (Boyatzis, 1998)." *(p.83)*
- **Theme:** "Boyatzis defined a theme as 'a pattern in the information that at minimum describes and organises the possible observations and at maximum interprets aspects of the phenomenon' (p. 161)." *(p.83)*
- **Template:** "This involved a template in the form of codes from a codebook to be applied as a means of organizing text for subsequent interpretation." *(p.83)*

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (role: Chapter 3 method anchor; focus: how a priori and data-driven codes are combined; the codebook's role; the stages)
- **(a) Definitions:** see §4.
- **(b) Main claims:**
  - Deductive and inductive coding can be combined: the a priori template carries the theory, and "themes … emerge direct from the data" through inductive coding (pp.82–83, quoted in §4).
  - The codebook is "a data management tool" and gives "a clear trail of evidence for the credibility of the study" (p.84, quoted in §5).
  - A step-by-step procedure with explicit stages makes the analysis auditable (six stages, §3). *(paraphrase)*
- **(c) Conditions named:** each code given a label, definition and description (pp.84–85); the code tested on sample text before full coding (Stage 2, p.85); analysis grounded in raw data and checked iteratively (Stage 6, p.90, where the paper warns against "fabricating evidence" by seeing expected data).
- **(d) Evidence base:** one empirical exemplar: nursing clinicians and clinical managers in South Australian health care, 10 focus groups and 16 policy or procedure documents (p.83).
- **(e) Limitations:** see §9.
- **(f) What a careful examiner would expect** — verbatim requirements from the paper (postulates: printed p.81; the paper attributes them to Schutz):
  - "The researcher must establish the highest degree of clarity of the conceptual framework and method applied, and these must follow the principles of formal logic." *(p.81, postulate of logical consistency)*
  - "The model must be grounded in the subjective meaning the action had for the 'actor.'" *(p.81, postulate of subjective interpretation)*
  - "There must be consistency between the researcher's constructs and typifications and those found in common-sense experience. The model must be recognizable and understood by the 'actors' within everyday life." *(p.81, postulate of adequacy)*
  - "interpretive research still requires a trail of evidence throughout the research process to demonstrate credibility or trustworthiness" *(p.81)*
  - "Interpretive rigor requires the researcher to demonstrate clearly how interpretations of the data have been achieved and to illustrate findings with quotations from, or access to, the raw data" *(p.82)*
  - "An essential step in the development of a useful framework for analysis is to determine the applicability of the code to the raw information" *(Stage 2, p.85)*
  - The multi-coder suggestion and the replicable-reporting sentence: see §9 item 3 and §5.
- **Direct relevance to protocol v0.98 §10 (solo adaptation):** two verbatim passages.
  1. Stage 2: "Following the coding process of the documents using the predefined codes, I invited my supervisor to code the documents as well. The results were compared, and no modifications to the predetermined code template were required." *(p.85; two appraisal documents were the test pieces)*
  2. The single-coder sentence in §9 item 2 (p.91).
  **The paper does not report re-coding after an interval.** It reports a supervisor test-coding of two documents and a supervisor discussion of the themes. It is a precedent for a solo doctoral analysis with supervisor input, not for the interval re-coding step in §10.
