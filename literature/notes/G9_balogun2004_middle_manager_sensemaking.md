## 1. WHY
Middle managers implement restructuring they did not design; the paper traces how their schemata change through lateral sensemaking. *Location: abstract p.523; introduction pp.523–524.*

## 2. HOW
Longitudinal qualitative case in a restructured UK utility; 26 middle managers from about 90 acted as diarists (p.526); diary tracking started in August 1993 (p.526); diary questions include "What rumors and stories are circulating?" and "What is going well and why?" (p.526); interview prompts were drawn from the diaries (p.527). Divisions: a Core Division plus Engineering and Services (p.526). *(The page counts NotebookLM gave for the data corpus were not checked and are not used.)*

## 3. WHAT
A "replacement" pattern of schema development: middle managers moved from shared through clustered sensemaking to shared-but-differentiated sensemaking (abstract, p.523). Senior managers were "ghosts" in the middle managers' sensemaking (p.524).

## 4. DEFINITION
"Sensemaking is a conversational and narrative process through which people create and maintain an intersubjective world" *(p.524)*.
Schemata "constitute the cognitive structures or frameworks by which generic concepts derived from past events and experiences are stored in memory" *(p.525)*. "Organizational schemata or interpretive schemes … are frames of reference shared among members of an organization or its subgroups" *(p.525)*.

## 5. CITABLE
The paper is about restructuring, not AI.
- "Change recipients create change. They determine its outcomes through their social processes of interaction and the meanings they develop as a result—and therefore, the term 'change recipient,' with its implication of passive acceptance, may be inappropriate" *(p.546)*
- "Senior managers acted more as agents of 'indirect infection' (Ford, 1999b) influencing organizational meanings" and "it is the actions, behaviors, gestures, and language of peers, and their shared personal experiences, that have a more direct impact on middle manager schema development and change outcomes" *(p.524)*
- "The nature of the relationships that develop, and the way the new structure operates, are thus determined by these middle manager processes of interaction" *(p.545)*; "Middle manager sensemaking occurs primarily outside of senior management control" *(p.545)*

## 9. LIMITATIONS
Verbatim, all p.546: (1) "Like much of the organizational change and sensemaking literature we draw on in this article, this research examined patterns of change in only one organization and in one form of restructuring"; (2) "But, as with any single case study research, issues arise about the generalizability of our findings to other instances of structural change involving shifts to more decentralized forms. We acknowledge that the generalizability of our model of schema change needs to be established through further research"; (3) "However, we looked at a top-down, directive change involving a substantial restructuring and a significant break with the past in terms of working practices. As a result, we have only been able to make limited suggestions as to how patterns of schema change vary with context"; (4) "We also only looked at middle managers. It is likely that the process of senior management schema development during restructuring is very different, with different triggers and negotiation processes".
