## 1. WHY
Membership status has been discussed mostly for observational and field research; the authors argue it matters in all qualitative work and that the insider/outsider split is too crude. The abstract's own statement of the aim: "Rather than consider this issue from a dichotomous perspective, the authors explore the notion of the space between that allows researchers to occupy the position of both insider and outsider rather than insider or outsider." *(Abstract, p.54)* Introduction: "Whether the researcher is an insider, sharing the characteristic, role, or experience under study with the participants, or an outsider to the commonality shared by participants, the personhood of the researcher, including her or his membership status in relation to those participating in the research, is an essential and ever-present aspect of the investigation." *(p.55)*

## 2. HOW
Conceptual reflection built on the two authors' own studies: Corbin Dwyer as an insider (a parent of children adopted from Asia, studying such parents, hermeneutic phenomenology) and Buckle as an outsider (not a bereaved parent, studying bereaved mothers and fathers, grounded theory) *(Abstract, p.54; pp.55–57)*. **The paper states no literature corpus, search method or time span.** (NotebookLM's "1984–2007 corpus" was only the range of the reference list and was dropped.)

## 3. WHAT
- **"The space between"** as a third position for researchers *(p.60 ff.)*.
- **Insider and outsider positions** each with positive and negative aspects *(Insider or outsider: To be or not to be, pp.57–59)*: "From this discussion, it is clear that there are both positive and negative aspects of insider and outsider status." *(p.59)*
- **Fay (1996) on knowing:** being a member is "neither necessary nor sufficient" to know a group's experience, and sometimes not being a member helps, for four reasons the authors list *(p.59; paraphrase)*.
- **Complete membership** brings "a certain amount of legitimacy and/or stigma (Adler & Adler, 1987)" *(p.58)*.

## 4. DEFINITION
- **Insider research:** "Insider research refers to when researchers conduct research with populations of which they are also members (Kanuha, 2000) so that the researcher shares an identity, language, and experiential base with the study participants (Asselin, 2003)." *(p.58)*
- **Outsider:** the researcher is "an outsider to the commonality shared by participants" *(p.55, in the sentence quoted in §1)*. The paper gives no separate one-sentence definition of "outsider".
- **The space between (via Aoki, 1996):** "Drawing on Aoki's (1996) work, insider and outsider are understood as a binary of two separate preexisting entities, which can be bridged or brought together to conjoin with a hyphen. This hyphen can be viewed not as a path but as a dwelling place for people. This hyphen acts as a third space, a space between, a space of paradox, ambiguity, and ambivalence, as well as conjunction and disjunction." *(p.60)*

## 5. CITABLE
This is a qualitative methods paper; it does not address organisations, AI policy or BI.
- "To present these concepts in a dualistic manner is overly simplistic. It is restrictive to lock into a notion that emphasizes either/or, one or the other, you are in or you are out. Rather, a dialectical approach allows the preservation of the complexity of similarities and differences." *(p.60)*
- "Instead, we posit that the core ingredient is not insider or outsider status but an ability to be open, authentic, honest, deeply interested in the experience of one's research participants, and committed to accurately and adequately representing their experience." *(p.59)*
- "Perhaps, as researchers we can only ever occupy the space between. We may be closer to the insider position or closer to the outsider position, but because our perspective is shaped by our position as a researcher (which includes having read much literature on the research topic), we cannot fully occupy one or the other of those positions." *(p.61)*
- "The intimacy of qualitative research no longer allows us to remain true outsiders to the experience under study and, because of our role as researchers, it does not qualify us as complete insiders. We now occupy the space between, with the costs and benefits this status affords." *(p.61)*
- On insider access: "This insider role status frequently allows researchers more rapid and more complete acceptance by their participants. Therefore, participants are typically more open with researchers so that there may be a greater depth to the data gathered." *(p.58)*

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (role: Chapter 3 method anchor; focus: insider versus outsider; the "space between"; what the researcher should disclose)
- **(a) Definitions:** see §4.
- **(b) Main claims:** the dichotomy critique (§5, p.60); the inevitability of the middle position (§5, p.61); the core ingredient is authenticity rather than status (§5, p.59); insider and outsider roles both have positive and negative aspects (§3, p.59).
- **(c) Conditions named:** the researcher's reading of the literature and role as researcher move an insider away from pure insidership (p.61, quoted in §5); participants are not uniform, so an insider shares experience with some and not others *(Being an Insider, p.56; paraphrase from NotebookLM, not re-read line by line)*; insider status can bring more open participants (p.58).
- **(d) Evidence base:** conceptual, from two authors' own studies (§2).
- **(e) Limitations:** see §9.
- **(f) What a careful examiner would expect** — verbatim:
  - "By extension, researchers are increasingly making known their membership identity in the communities they study." *(p.55)*
  - "points to the need for bracketing assumptions" *(p.55; the surrounding sentence was not checked)*
  - "Disciplined bracketing and detailed reflection on the subjective research process, with a close awareness of one's own personal biases and perspectives, might well reduce the potential concerns associated with insider membership." *(p.59)*
  - "A prudent researcher must be aware of these aspects in relation to his or her particular status to the group under study." *(p.59)*
  - "As qualitative researchers we must be fully authentic in our interactions with our participants and 'honour the consequences of acting with genuineness' (p. 105), highlighting the necessity of remaining reflexive." *(p.60)*
  - "Surely the time has come to abandon these constructed dichotomies and embrace and explore the complexity and richness of the space between entrenched perspectives." *(p.62)*
- **Ties to the thesis's design (mine, not the paper's):** Chapter 3 should say plainly how the researcher relates to participants' roles, employers and tools (shared membership, or none), what bracketing or reflexive notes were kept, and how that position may have shaped interviews and coding. The paper supports treating that position as disclosed and managed, not as something to be removed.
