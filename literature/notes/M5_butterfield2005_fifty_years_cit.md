## 1. WHY
The CIT began as a task-analysis tool built on direct observation and became a qualitative interview method built on retrospective self-report. The authors set four aims: to consolidate 50 years of the technique, to place it in the qualitative tradition, to examine the CIT research done at the University of British Columbia, and to offer recommendations *(Introduction, p.477)*. They frame the credibility problem as: "Given the evolution of the CIT away from direct observation to retrospective self-report, and from task analysis to examining psychological concepts, how might a researcher establish the credibility of results arising from this qualitative method in a way that is consistent with Flanagan's exhortation to report them as the final step in a CIT study?" *(p.484)*

## 2. HOW
Literature review and methodological synthesis. "the authors reviewed over 125 articles, theses, dissertations, and book chapters about the CIT, ranging in dates from 1949 to 2003. This included 74 articles, nine books, 44 dissertations and theses, three paper presentations and one report" *(p.477; the last item is cut off in the text layer and is taken from NotebookLM; NotebookLM wrote "74 journal articles", the PDF says "74 articles")*. Plus "a total of 19 master's theses and doctoral dissertations that used the CIT between the years 1991 and 2003" at UBC *(p.485)*. No databases or search terms stated in the passages checked.

## 3. WHAT
- **Four departures from Flanagan's original design** *(Evolution of the CIT, pp.480–484)*: psychological constructs rather than overt behaviour; retrospective self-report rather than direct observation; varied data-analysis approaches; varied credibility checks. *(paraphrase from NotebookLM; the section span was not checked line by line.)*
- **CIT across paradigms** *(The CIT and other qualitative methods, pp.482–485)*.
- **Nine credibility checks**, recommended for routine use in future CIT studies *(quoted in full in §13(f), pp.490–491)*.

## 4. DEFINITION
- **Flanagan's flexibility statement (quoted by the authors):** the CIT "does not consist of a single rigid set of rules governing such data collection. Rather it should be thought of as a flexible set of principles that must be modified and adapted to meet the specific situation at hand" (Flanagan, 1954: 335) *(p.476)*.
- **The authors' summary of the CIT as a qualitative method:** "(a) Focus is on critical events, incidents, or factors that help promote or detract from the effective performance of some activity or the experience of a specific situation or event; (b) Discipline origin is from industrial and organizational psychology; (c) Data collection is primarily through interviews, either in person (individually or in groups) or via telephone; (d) Data analysis is conducted by determining the frame of reference, forming categories that emerge from the data, and determining the specificity or generality of the categories; and (e) Narrative form is that of categories with operational definitions and self-descriptive titles" *(p.483; NotebookLM said p.485)*. This is the closest the paper comes to a definition.
- **Qualitative research (McLeod, 1994: 77, quoted):** "any kind of research that produces findings not arrived at by means of statistical procedures or other means of qualification" *(p.482; the PDF prints "qualification", which reads like a slip for "quantification" in the source; quote it with [sic])*.

## 5. CITABLE
This is a research-methods paper (counselling psychology); it does not address organisations, AI policy or BI.

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (role: Chapter 3 method anchor; focus: how CIT moved from task analysis to qualitative interviewing; credibility checks proposed for CIT)
- **(a) Definitions:** see §4.
- **(b) Main claims:**
  - Over 50 years the CIT shifted to retrospective self-report (see §1, and "virtually all of them followed Nagay's… and Herzberg et al.'s… leads by using retrospective self-report", p.481).
  - The credibility question is stated on p.484 (§1); the answer is the nine-check protocol (§13(f)).
- **(c) Conditions named:** full, clear and detailed incident accounts (p.481); the three-part inclusion criteria (p.488); the 25 percent participation criterion attributed to Borgen and Amundson (1984) (pp.487, 490).
- **(d) Evidence base:** conceptual review plus a review of 19 UBC theses and dissertations (pp.477, 485).
- **(e) Limitations:** see §9.
- **(f) What a careful examiner would expect** — verbatim:
  - "The research worker is responsible for pointing out not only the limitations but also the degree of credibility and the value of the final results obtained" (Flanagan, 1954: 355, quoted) *(p.479; NotebookLM said p.480)*
  - "Therefore it is critically important that the researcher examines his/her own assumptions (and predilections), considers very carefully the nature of the research problem to be investigated, and thinks through how the technique may most appropriately be applied in the particular researchable case." (Chell, 1998: 51, quoted) *(p.483)*
  - "carefully following an established and robust qualitative research method is one part of establishing the credibility of a study's results (Creswell, 1998). Hence, it strikes us as important that researchers embrace and apply the steps of the CIT research method as set out by Flanagan (1954) and the practices discussed here in order to maintain and enhance both its research tradition and its credibility." *(p.490)*
  - "we believe the CIT method would be strengthened by standardizing the term 'critical incident technique' for all studies using this method. It becomes confusing when a plethora of terms is used to refer to the same research method." *(p.490)*
  - "To determine the soundness of the results arising from a CIT study, our recommendation is to standardize use of the credibility and trustworthiness checks discussed earlier in this article. This would consist of routinely incorporating the following nine data-analysis checks into future CIT studies: (1) extracting the critical incidents using independent coders; (2) cross-checking by participants; (3) having independent judges place incidents into categories; (4) tracking the point at which exhaustiveness is reached; (5) eliciting expert opinions; (6) calculating participation rates against the 25 percent criteria established by Borgen and Amundson (1984); (7) checking theoretical agreement by stating the study's underlying assumptions and by comparing the emerging categories to the relevant scholarly literature; (8) audio- or video-taping interviews to ensure participants' stories are accurately captured; and (9) checking interview fidelity by getting an expert in the CIT method to listen to a sample of interview tapes." *(runs across a page break, pp.490–491; NotebookLM said pp.492–493)*
  - The inclusion criteria in §5 (p.488).
- **Ties to the thesis's design:** several of the nine checks need people the thesis does not have (independent coders, independent judges, participant cross-checks in a second interview, an expert auditing recordings). A solo study should say which checks it did, which it did not, and why, rather than claim the protocol.
- **Not addressed:** interview length or sample size for CIT interviews; CIT in AI or organisational-policy settings.
