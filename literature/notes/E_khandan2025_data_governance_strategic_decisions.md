## 1. WHY
AI integration into corporate environments has transformed strategic decision-making, shifting from human intuition to algorithmic logic. This creates challenges maintaining data integrity, transparency, and accountability — poorly governed or biased AI systems risk flawed strategic conclusions, legal violations, and reputational damage. Organizations face an urgent need to translate abstract ethical principles into actionable corporate governance mechanisms.
*Location: Abstract, p.518; Introduction, pp.518–519.*

## 3. WHAT
- Data governance as strategic enabler: formalized maturity (ownership, lineage, stewards) increases AI reliability, correlates with resource efficiency/predictive accuracy. *(p.520)*
- Ethical AI as determinant of trust: bias detection, fairness audits, explainable AI under "AI ethics boards" convert moral compliance into reputational asset. *(pp.520–521)*
- Regulatory compliance & alignment: EU AI Act risk classification, OECD AI Principles, ISO/IEC 38505-1 reduce compliance friction. *(p.521)*
- Culture and ethical leadership: top-down commitment as "connective tissue" turning written policy into operational reality via executive incentives. *(pp.521, 523)*
- Improved strategic decision-making quality: data safeguards protect against litigation risk, build adaptive strategic foresight. *(p.521)*
- Economic/competitive advantages: ethical data practices reduce transaction costs, streamline data silos, differentiate brand. *(pp.521–522)*
- The Triad of Responsible Decision-Making: data governance (structural foundation) + ethics (moral direction) + AI guidelines (actionable policy) — shifts orgs from reactive compliance to proactive governance. *(p.522)*

## 4. DEFINITION
"the framework of policies, standards, and controls that dictate how data is collected, stored, processed, and utilized within an organization." *(Introduction, p.518)*
