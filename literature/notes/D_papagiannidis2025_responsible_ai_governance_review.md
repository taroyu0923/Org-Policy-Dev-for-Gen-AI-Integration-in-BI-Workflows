## 1. WHY
Organizations have ethical guidelines but face a severe gap operationalizing high-level principles across the technology lifecycle: "adhering to responsible AI principles is generally deprioritized or considered an ancillary task during the actual implementation and management of AI projects." Firms struggle translating abstract guidelines into concrete practice.
*Location: Introduction, pp.1–2.*

## 2. HOW
Systematic literature review, scoping-review approach. 1,080 documents initially identified, screened to 48 peer-reviewed articles. Databases: Scopus, Business Source Complete, Emerald, Taylor & Francis, Springer, Web of Knowledge, ABI/Inform Complete, IEEE Xplore, AIS libraries, ScienceDirect, JSTOR, DBLP, Google Scholar. English-language articles from 2017 onward; data collection Nov 2022, refined Dec 2023.
*Location: Research methodology (Protocol development p.2; Inclusion/exclusion criteria p.3; Data sources p.3; Fig.1 p.2).*

## 3. WHAT
- Seven Pillars of Responsible AI: accountability, diversity/fairness, human agency, privacy, technical robustness, transparency, societal/environmental well-being. *(pp.7–9)*
- Structural/Procedural/Relational Practices typology. *(pp.10–13)*
- Antecedents–Practices–Effects Framework (Fig.2). *(pp.10–14)*
- Structured research agenda (Table 5) on governance scaling, competitive-strategy alignment, social evaluation. *(pp.11–13, 15)*

## 4. DEFINITION
"A set of practices for developing, deploying, and monitoring AI applications in a safe, trustworthy, and ethical manner that ensures appropriate functionality of AI over the entire lifecycle." *(p.6)*

## 5. CITABLE
- "...responsible AI principles are in a continuous state of flux, whereby every new type of emergent AI technology comes into a new set of conditions that must be considered. An indicative example is the recent release of Open AI's ChatGPT..." *(p.7)*
- "Organizations often struggle to harmonize their competitive strategies with the aims and ambitions of responsibly managing AI applications... it is crucial to understand the processes at the strategic level that facilitate the responsible deployment of AI technologies to be utilized as a competitive strategy, and vice versa." *(p.12)*
- "...evolving regulations and directives such as the AI Act influence which aspects of governance are prioritized... organizations must develop appropriate mechanisms to identify external stimuli quickly and accurately and interpret how they affect their AI applications." *(p.10)*

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
No practitioner-ready interview guide, but Table 5's research questions convert directly to interview prompts:
- **Policy Framework Development:** "How should organizations develop vertical and horizontal structures to attain responsible AI outcomes?" (Table 5, p.11); "How can responsible AI practices incorporate elements of competitive strategies?" / "How are tensions... between being responsible with AI and attaining a competitive edge... resolved?" (Table 5, p.11)
- **Implementation & Change Management:** "How do ethical AI guidelines translate into specific processes that can be implemented at different organizational levels?" / "How should organizations design processes to ensure data quality and minimize bias?" ## 9. LIMITATIONS
Four self-acknowledged limitations (Conclusions, p.15):
- Geographical bias: source papers/principles predominantly from Europe/USA.
- Lack of empirical testing of the proposed arguments/relationships.
- Non-exhaustive research questions ("neither exhaustive nor complete").
- Regulatory uncertainty: "How such regulations affect organizations' responsibility for AI governance implementation and how society and end users perceive their effect remain unclear."

## 10. BI LINK
"Business intelligence" does not appear verbatim, but the paper connects governance constructs to analytics/data management explicitly:
- "Although AI can produce accurate analytical findings, implementing accountability procedures is typically challenging because of its 'black-box' nature..." (p.7)
- "In data management, these practices ensure the organization, security, and accessibility of data through classification and encryption protocols in a manner that minimizes bias and checks for errors." (p.11)
- Cites Conboy et al. (2020) "'Big time': ...temporal complexity and business value in analytics" (p.16); Ji-fan Ren et al. (2017) on big data analytics environment (p.16); Rana et al. (2022) on "dark side" of AI-integrated business analytics (p.17).
