## 1. WHY
Chinese students face severe access barriers to leading international GenAI tools (ChatGPT) due to national internet censorship/firewall regulations (Abstract, p. 1; Introduction, p. 3; Results, p. 12). Mainstream global models remain English-centric and Western-culturally embedded, limiting effectiveness for non-Western users/disciplines (Introduction, p. 3; Results, p. 19). Research objective: "How do Chinese undergraduate students engage with and navigate global and domestic generative AI in their learning process?" (Introduction, p. 4)

## 2. HOW
Qualitative study, semi-structured interviews (Methodology, pp. 10–11). Sample: 15 Chinese-speaking undergraduates (8 female, 7 male; 6 STEM, 9 social sciences/humanities) at one prestigious first-tier-city Chinese university (p. 11). Conducted July–August 2024 via Zoom (~60 min each), auto-transcribed and manually verified. NVivo 14 Mac; Saldaña's (2025) two-cycle coding (in vivo/descriptive, then pattern/focused/axial) (pp. 11–12). Literature review includes a technical benchmarking radar comparing 3 global models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, Gemini-Exp) and 3 Chinese models (Deepseek-v3, Doubao-1.5-Pro, Qwen2.5) across 5 dimensions (pp. 5–7).

## 3. WHAT
- **UTAUT2 framework** applied: Performance Expectancy, Effort Expectancy, Social Influence, Facilitating Conditions (pp. 8–10).
- Censorship drives unstable, risky workarounds (VPNs, shared accounts, social-media intermediaries), raising privacy risks; domestic tools offer seamless access (pp. 12–14).
- Payment friction (foreign bank card mandates) deters paid global tool adoption; no students pay for domestic AI (free suffices) (pp. 14–16).
- Bilingual strategic usage: 10/15 participants use global tools for English/STEM/coding, domestic tools for Chinese/humanities (pp. 16–19).
- Cultural/ideological gap: global models struggle with Chinese philosophy and ideology courses, forcing reliance on domestic (censored) models (pp. 19–21).
- Anthropomorphism: students say "please"/"thank you" to GenAI (pp. 22–23).
- Disciplinary anxiety paradox: STEM and humanities students each fear the *other* group faces more job displacement (p. 24).
- **Strategic Navigation Framework**: access limitations, linguistic capabilities, cultural familiarity as three pillars (Conclusion, p. 27).

## 4. DEFINITION
- **Facilitating Conditions**: "relates to users' perceptions of the resources and support available to help them effectively use the technology (Venkatesh et al., 2003)." (Theoretical Framework, pp. 9–10)
- Socio-technical extension: "Facilitating Conditions are not merely technical (e.g., internet access), but also social, economic, and relational." (Results, p. 13)
- Practical governance definition: "Access to generative AI is further complicated by regulatory differences across countries. In nations such as China, Russia, Iran, and North Korea, varying degrees of restrictions on tools like ChatGPT limit students' access to internationally developed generative AI platforms." (Introduction, p. 3)

## 5. CITABLE
> "Leading universities worldwide have begun developing institutional policies and faculty training programs aimed at fostering the ethical use of generative AI in higher education... However, national governments and universities exhibit varying levels of responsiveness and regulatory emphasis, resulting in significant disparities in students' access to policy frameworks and technical support..." (Introduction, p. 3)

> "Students often employ workarounds (e.g., VPNs) to access global generative AIs, raising ethical and privacy concerns." (Abstract, p. 2) / "participants expressed concerns about data privacy and service authenticity when relying on intermediaries." (Results, p. 13)

> "They underscore the urgency of developing human-centered, context-sensitive AI ecosystems that balance functionality with inclusivity." (Conclusion, p. 28)

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
No verbatim interview appendix, but the paper documents its protocol structure and explicit key questions.

**Policy Framework Development**
- "Shadow IT" driven by rigid bans: interviews with IT security/BI leads should investigate "How do current organizational restrictions on advanced global AI models impact daily BI deliverables? What unofficial, personal, or shadow IT workarounds are currently being deployed by analysts, and what security/compliance risks do these introduce?" (derived from Results, pp. 12–13)
- Facilitating Conditions probe: "What structural, social, and technical support frameworks (e.g., enterprise-wide licensing, compliance sandboxes, peer mentorship) are necessary to facilitate safe and authorized AI use in data workflows?" (derived from Theoretical Framework, pp. 9–10; Results, p. 13)

**Implementation & Change Management**
- Price-value/procurement friction probe: "Does our internal software procurement and licensing workflow introduce administrative friction? How does this deter analysts from adopting enterprise-approved, paid GenAI tools?" (derived from Results, pp. 14–16)
- Task-to-model alignment probe: "How do BI analysts strategically toggle between different GenAI platforms depending on the specific task (e.g., SQL generation vs. local-language report drafting)?" (derived from Results, pp. 16–18)

**Organizational Adaptation Dynamics**
- Human-AI interaction paradigm: "Do BI analysts perceive GenAI as a passive mechanical utility, an active digital assistant, or a collaborative partner? How does this alter prompt-engineering habits, error checking, and trust?" (derived from Results, pp. 21–23)
- Job-security anxiety probe: "How does the integration of GenAI in automated data processing alter analysts' long-term job security perceptions within their department versus neighboring business units?" (derived from Results, pp. 24–25)
- Documented interview protocol structure: "The interview protocol includes both open- and closed-ended questions across five sections: academic background, generative AI usage, institutional guidance and support, attitudes toward generative AI, and reflections." (Methodology, p. 11)

## 9. LIMITATIONS
- Single-site scope: "future research should employ comparative mixed methods across institutions to strengthen generalizability." (Conclusion, p. 28)
- Sampling/network bias: participants recruited via WeChat/personal network, all from one prestigious first-tier-city university (Methodology, p. 11).
- Excluded multimodal evaluation: "due to the unstandardized evaluation for image generation, the technical comparisons... will primarily focus on text-to-text models." (Literature Review, p. 6)
