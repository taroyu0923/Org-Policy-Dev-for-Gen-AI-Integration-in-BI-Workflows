## 1. WHY
Existing GenAI-adoption literature focuses on well-resourced Western newsrooms, leaving GenAI's role in news production in developing, non-Western contexts critically understudied (p. 2; "Why Bangladesh's Case Matters," p. 6). The study examines Bangladesh — a Global South nation with sharp socio-economic/press-freedom disparities from the West — to understand how contextual constraints shape technology-adoption trajectories (p. 2; pp. 5–6).

## 2. HOW
Semi-structured qualitative interview study ("Method"/"Data Collection," p. 6), IRB-approved 17-question protocol in Bangla. Purposive sample of 23 working journalists (3–20 yrs experience): 8 reporters, 6 copy editors, 6 news gatekeepers/editors, 3 digital content creators, across legacy print, online, and TV (p. 6; Table 1, pp. 6–7). Data collected May–September 2025. Transcribed, open/axial/selectively coded in NVivo 14; data triangulation used ("Data Analysis," p. 7).

## 3. WHAT
- Organic, individual-level GenAI adoption (ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek) despite near-total absence of formal institutional policy/training — except *Prothom Alo* (p. 8; pp. 12–13).
- Five usage domains: information gathering/research, scriptwriting/content generation, idea generation, editing/translation, multimedia editing (pp. 8–9).
- Benefits ("time saver") vs. concerns (hallucinations, "lazy brain," loss of "human touch," redundancies, deepfakes, plagiarism) (pp. 9–12).
- Standard UTAUT fails: "facilitating conditions" do not predict adoption — high adoption occurred organically without them (p. 12; p. 13).
- Horizontal social influence and a "voluntary-compulsion" spectrum replace top-down directives (Abstract, p. 1; p. 13).
- Proposed 6 UTAUT modifications for non-Western, resource-constrained, institutionally vacant settings (pp. 13–14).

## 4. DEFINITION
**Facilitating conditions** (Venkatesh et al., 2003): "organizational and technological infrastructure support system to make use of technology easy and smooth" (p. 12). Paper notes this is a critical missing link in Bangladesh: "None of the participants reports formal institutional AI policies, training programs (except Prothom Alo), and organizational guidelines for managing AI risks..." (p. 12).

## 5. CITABLE
> "The absence of organizational AI policies in news media left Bangladeshi journalist to set their own policies when it comes to the question of bias, inaccuracy, and plagiarism concerns. This process of navigation is highly informed by their own ethical principles and rule of thumb – verification and cross-checking." (p. 13)

> "Furthermore, institutional support does not drive adoption (adoption was already widespread) but rather manages adoption, potentially mitigating risks and improving practice. This suggests a temporal distinction: facilitating conditions may matter less for adoption initiation but more for adoption quality and ethical implementation in later stages." (p. 12)

> "To move forward as ethic and rule-bound media organizations, Bangladesh and similar non-Western developing countries, should consider developing institutional policy and framework on GenAI..." (p. 14)

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
Contains an actual Appendix A interview protocol — highly transferable.

**Policy Framework Development**
- Q16: "What is your organization's institutional stance on adopting AI?..." (Appendix A, p. 19)
- Q17: "Does your organization have an AI policy, whether published or unpublished? If yes, could you please provide an overview? If not, why has an AI policy not been formulated so far?" (Appendix A, p. 19)

**Implementation & Change Management**
- Q4: "How can artificial intelligence tools be used in your professional purposes (e.g., reporting/copy-editing/photography/editorial decision-making/research/knowledge improvement)?" — adaptable to BI data analysis/reporting/dashboards (Appendix A, p. 19).
- Q7: "How easy or difficult have the artificial intelligence tools been so far for you in terms of using them for your professional or personal purposes?" (Appendix A, p. 19)

**Organizational Adaptation Dynamics**
- Q6: "Have you heard about any of your friends or colleagues using them for their professional and personal purposes? If yes, how and why do they use them?" (Appendix A, p. 19) — probes horizontal/peer diffusion.
- Q13: "Do you see any negative consequences AI might bring for journalism [or BI] in general?" (Appendix A, p. 19)
- Q14: "How might it create disadvantages for news reporters/copy editors/news editors/editors [or BI analysts, data engineers, managers]? And how?" (Appendix A, p. 19)

## 8. SNOWBALL
1. Venkatesh, V., Morris, M. G., Davis, G. B., & Davis, F. D. (2003). "User Acceptance of Information Technology..." *MIS Quarterly*, 27(3), 425–478 (References, p. 17) — defines "facilitating conditions"/"social influence" foundational to UTAUT.
2. Venkatesh, V., Thong, J., & Xu, X. (2012). "Consumer acceptance and use of information technology..." *MIS Quarterly*, 36(1), 157 (p. 17) — UTAUT2, explains shadow-IT adoption via habit/hedonic motivation.
3. Beckett, C. (2019). *New powers, new responsibilities: A global survey of Journalism and Artificial Intelligence.* LSE (p. 14) — maps structural/cultural barriers (financial, knowledge, redundancy fear).
4. Helberger, N., & Diakopoulos, N. (2023). "ChatGPT and the AI Act." *Internet Policy Review*, 12(4), 1–26 (p. 15) — bridges macro-regulation (EU AI Act) with micro corporate compliance.
5. Simon, F. M. (2024). *Artificial intelligence in the news...* Tow Center, Columbia University (p. 17) — systemic rationalization/reshaping of professional autonomy under AI.

## 9. LIMITATIONS
- Generalizability: "Qualitative research with low sample as a research approach itself lacks generalizability." (Limitations and Future Research, p. 14)
- Theoretical verification: "the proposed suggestions to the UTAUT may lack statistical significance if quantitative method applied." (p. 14)
- Translation risk: "such 'piecemeal' translation (Birbili, 2000) has limitations of losing of misrepresentations..." — interviews conducted in Bangla, translated for the manuscript (Method > Data Analysis, p. 7).
