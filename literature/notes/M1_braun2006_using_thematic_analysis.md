## 1. WHY
Thematic analysis is described as "a poorly demarcated and rarely acknowledged, yet widely used qualitative analytic method" *(Introduction, p.77)*. The authors' aim is a guide to the theory, application and evaluation of TA as a method in its own right. Support for the claim that researchers do not say how they analysed: "Qualitative psychologists need to be clear about what they are doing and why, and to include the often-omitted 'how' they did their analysis in their reports" *(p.79)*.

## 2. HOW
Theoretical paper and step-by-step guide; it collects no dataset of its own. Illustrations come from the authors' and others' published studies, for example a study of 75 men's feelings about their bodies and clothes (Frith & Gleeson, 2004; the "75" appears on pp.77–80) and 26 talk-show episodes (Clarke & Kitzinger, 2004; p.82). ## 3. WHAT
- **Six phases** *(Table 1, p.87; Phase headings pp.87–93)*: (1) familiarising yourself with your data; (2) generating initial codes; (3) searching for themes; (4) reviewing themes; (5) defining and naming themes; (6) producing the report. NotebookLM cited Table 1 as p.88; it is **p.87**.
- **Epistemological flexibility** across essentialist/realist, contextualist and constructionist positions *(pp.81, 85)*.

- **A 15-point checklist of criteria for good TA** *(Table 2, p.96)*.

## 4. DEFINITION
- **Thematic analysis:** "Thematic analysis is a method for identifying, analysing and reporting patterns (themes) within data." *(p.79)* The next sentence, "It minimally organizes and describes your data set in (rich) detail", is on the same page; the PDF spells it "organizes", not "organises".
- **Theme:** "A theme captures something important about the data in relation to the research question, and represents some level of patterned response or meaning within the data set." *(p.82)* Also, describing the end point of analysis: "themes are abstract (and often fuzzy) constructs the investigators identify [sic] before, during, and after analysis" (Ryan & Bernard, 2000: 780, quoted by Braun & Clarke) *(p.86)*. **NotebookLM's version ended "after data collection"; the PDF says "after analysis".**
- **Inductive:** "An inductive approach means the themes identified are strongly linked to the data themselves (Patton, 1990)… Inductive analysis is therefore a process of coding the data without trying to fit it into a preexisting coding frame, or the researcher's analytic preconceptions." *(p.83)*
- **Theoretical:** "In contrast, a 'theoretical' thematic analysis would tend to be driven by the researcher's theoretical or analytic interest in the area, and is thus more explicitly analyst-driven." *(p.84)*
- **Semantic:** "With a semantic approach, the themes are identified within the explicit or surface meanings of the data, and the analyst is not looking for anything beyond what a participant has said or what has been written." *(p.84)*
- **Latent:** "In contrast, a thematic analysis at the latent level goes beyond the semantic content of the data, and starts to identify or examine the underlying ideas, assumptions, and conceptualizations – and ideologies – that are theorized as shaping or informing the semantic content of the data." *(p.84)*

## 9. LIMITATIONS
Verbatim, from "So what does thematic analysis offer psychologists?":
1. "Many of the disadvantages depend more on poorly conducted analyses or inappropriate research questions than on the method itself." *(p.96)*
2. "Further, the flexibility of the method – which allows for a wide range of analytic options – means that the potential range of things that can be said about your data is broad. While this is an advantage, it can also be a disadvantage in that it makes developing specific guidelines for higher-phase analysis difficult, and can be potentially paralysing to the researcher trying to decide what aspects of their data to focus on." *(pp.96–97)*
3. "Another issue to consider is that a thematic analysis has limited interpretative power beyond mere description if it is not used within an existing theoretical framework that anchors the analytic claims that are made." *(p.97)*
4. "For instance, unlike narrative or other biographical approaches, you are unable to retain a sense of continuity and contradiction through any one individual account, and these contradictions and consistencies across individual accounts may be revealing." *(p.97)*
5. "In contrast to methods similar to DA and CA, a simple thematic analysis does not allow the researcher to make claims about language use, or the fine-grained functionality of talk." *(p.97; NotebookLM omitted the words "a simple")*
6. "Finally, it is worth noting that thematic analysis currently has no particular kudos as an analytic method – this, we argue, stems from the very fact that it is poorly demarcated and claimed, yet widely used." *(p.97)*
These are disadvantages of the method as the authors see them (pp.96–97), not a limitations section about a study.

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (role: Chapter 3 method anchor; focus: the six phases; what a theme is; inductive versus theoretical TA; semantic versus latent)
- **(a) Definitions:** see §4.
- **(b) Main claims:**
  - TA is a method in its own right, not tied to one theory: "thematic analysis is not wedded to any pre-existing theoretical framework, and therefore it can be used within different theoretical frameworks (although not all), and can be used to do different things within them." *(p.81; NotebookLM's excerpt began "In contrast to IPA or grounded theory…", which is elided here.)*
  - The researcher is active: see the "emerging" quote in §5 (p.80).
  - Analysis is recursive rather than linear (Phases, pp.86–87). *(paraphrase)*
  - Inductive analysis gives a rich description of the whole dataset; theoretical analysis gives a more detailed account of one aspect (pp.83–84). *(paraphrase)*
- **(c) Conditions named:** epistemological position chosen and made explicit (pp.81, 85); every data item coded with equal attention (Table 2, item 2, p.96); themes checked for internal homogeneity and external heterogeneity (Phase 4, p.91); extracts embedded in an analytic narrative (Phase 6, p.93).
- **(d) Evidence base:** conceptual guide with illustrative studies; no empirical sample of its own.
- **(e) Limitations:** see §9.
- **(f) What a careful examiner would expect** — verbatim:
  - "Qualitative psychologists need to be clear about what they are doing and why, and to include the often-omitted 'how' they did their analysis in their reports" *(p.79)*
  - "What is important is that the theoretical framework and methods match what the researcher wants to know, and that they acknowledge these decisions, and recognize them as decisions." *(p.80)*
  - "Any theoretical framework carries with it a number of assumptions about the nature of the data, what they represent in terms of the 'the world', 'reality', and so forth. A good thematic analysis will make this transparent." *(p.81)*
  - "Extracts need to be embedded within an analytic narrative that compellingly illustrates the story you are telling about your data, and your analytic narrative needs to go beyond description of the data, and make an argument in relation to your research question." *(p.93)*
  - "Thematic analysis is not just a collection of extracts strung together with little or no analytic narrative. Nor is it a selection of extracts with analytic comment that simply or primarily paraphrases their content." *(p.94; NotebookLM said p.95)*
  - **Table 2, "A 15-point checklist of criteria for good thematic analysis" (p.96):** "1. The data have been transcribed to an appropriate level of detail, and the transcripts have been checked against the tapes for 'accuracy'." "2. Each data item has been given equal attention in the coding process." "3. Themes have not been generated from a few vivid examples (an anecdotal approach), but instead the coding process has been thorough, inclusive and comprehensive." "4. All relevant extracts for each theme have been collated." *[the PDF prints "All relevant extracts for all each theme have been collated"; the original has a typo, which NotebookLM silently corrected. Quote the PDF wording or use [sic].]* "5. Themes have been checked against each other and back to the original data set." "6. Themes are internally coherent, consistent, and distinctive." "7. Data have been analysed – interpreted, made sense of – rather than just paraphrased or described." "8. Analysis and data match each other – the extracts illustrate the analytic claims." "9. Analysis tells a convincing and well-organized story about the data and topic." "10. A good balance between analytic narrative and illustrative extracts is provided." "11. Enough time has been allocated to complete all phases of the analysis adequately, without rushing a phase or giving it a once-over-lightly." "12. The assumptions about, and specific approach to, thematic analysis are clearly explicated." "13. There is a good fit between what you claim you do, and what you show you have done – ie, described method and reported analysis are consistent." "14. The language and concepts used in the report are consistent with the epistemological position of the analysis." "15. The researcher is positioned as active in the research process; themes do not just 'emerge'."
  - Items 12–14 speak to the label decision: the thesis must say what approach it took (M2's answer: codebook TA) and describe what it actually did.
