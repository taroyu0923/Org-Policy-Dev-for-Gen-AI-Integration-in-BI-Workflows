## 4. DEFINITION
"Data governance specifies a cross-functional framework for managing data as a strategic enterprise asset. In doing so, data governance specifies decision rights and accountabilities for an organization's decision-making about its data. Furthermore, data governance formalizes data policies, standards, and procedures and monitors compliance." *(Sec.3, p.426)*

## 5. CITABLE
Top-down policy-design side, but includes feedback/enactment claims.
- "an escalation process helps to address issues to higher levels of authority...This enables stakeholders to give feedback, e.g. concerning policy changes to meet new business requirements." *(Sec.4.1.2, p.430)*
- "If organizations use too bureaucratic, complex, and restrictive data governance mechanisms, this 'over-governance' could lead to a performance decrease by limiting data-led innovations and motivating users to bypass policies and take unnecessary risks with their data." *(Sec.5.4, p.435)*
- "continuous training helps them act according to data policies, processes, and procedures..." *(Sec.4.1.3, p.431)*

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
Moderate-High — structured literature review, no original interviews, but a strong construct vocabulary:
- **Policy Encounter & Interpretation:** structural/procedural/relational mechanisms as the concrete forms through which policy reaches staff (Sec.4, Framework).
- **Implementation & Change Mgmt:** "contingency factors" (org size, industry, data culture maturity) moderating actual implementation (Sec.4.3); explicit research-agenda call for studies on "the actual behavior of individuals" in data-governance implementation (Sec.5, Research Agenda) — direct scholarly support for Albert's own study.
- **Organizational Adaptation Dynamics:** relational mechanisms framed as the primary lever for adapting governance over time (Sec.4.2); review notes an "under-researched" gap in longitudinal/processual studies of governance evolution (Sec.5).

## 9. LIMITATIONS
1. Structured literature review methodology — synthesizes existing studies rather than generating new empirical/interview data. *(Sec.3, Methodology)*
2. Authors explicitly note the reviewed literature is "conceptual and normative" with a scarcity of empirical studies on how governance mechanisms play out among individual employees. *(Sec.5, Research Agenda)*
3. Scope covers data governance broadly, not GenAI-specific, and not BI-specific in most reviewed sources.
