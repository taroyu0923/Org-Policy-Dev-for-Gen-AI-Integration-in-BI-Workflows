## 1. WHY
Existing qualitative-methods literature does not "come together to offer a systematic framework for developing and refining interview protocols" (Introduction, p. 811). Standardizing protocol development is important because "by enhancing the reliability of interview protocols, researchers can increase the quality of data they obtain from research interviews," and it gives the field "a shared language for indicating the rigorous steps taken to develop interview protocols" (Introduction, pp. 811–812).

## 2. HOW
Methodological "How To" article (not an empirical study) (Title Page, p. 811). The author develops the framework by integrating existing qualitative-methods literature with her own experience teaching and conducting qualitative research (p. 827). She illustrates it with a worked example from her own study of first-generation college students, interviewed three times over one academic semester (Phase 2, pp. 813–814). References span 1994–2015 (pp. 828–829). No database search or empirical sample of its own beyond the illustrative case.

## 3. WHAT
The **Interview Protocol Refinement (IPR) Framework** — four phases (Abstract, p. 811; p. 812):
- **Phase 1 — Ensuring Alignment**: build an "interview protocol matrix" mapping interview questions against research questions to find gaps (p. 812; Table 5, p. 827).
- **Phase 2 — Constructing an Inquiry-Based Conversation**: balance inquiry and conversation using everyday language, ordinary social rules, a script, and prompts (p. 813; Table 5, p. 827).
- **Phase 3 — Receiving Feedback**: close reading and think-aloud activities with a peer and volunteers to check comprehension/reliability (p. 824; Table 5, p. 827).
- **Phase 4 — Piloting the Protocol**: simulate real interview conditions (rapport, timing, recording) with a small sample resembling the target population (p. 827; Table 5, p. 827).
- **Typology of question types**: Introductory, Transition, Key, and Closing Questions (p. 822; Table 3, p. 823).

## 4. DEFINITION
No organizational/governance construct (this is a pure qualitative-methods paper). Core methodological constructs defined:
- **Inquiry-based conversation**: "I refer to this balance between inquiry and conversation as an inquiry-based conversation" (Phase 2, p. 813).
- **Congruency**: "Congruency means the researchers' interviews are anchored in the purpose of the study and the research questions" (p. 812).
- **Reliability of an interview protocol**: "The purpose of obtaining feedback on the interview protocol is to enhance its reliability—its trustworthiness—as a research instrument" (Phase 3, p. 824).

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
This is the **foundational instrument-design source** for the thesis methodology. Direct reusable instruments/questions, mapped to the thesis's three phases:

**Policy Framework Development**
- *Instrument:* Interview Protocol Matrix (Phase 1, p. 812; Table 2, p. 821) — map every policy/governance research question to specific interview items to catch gaps: "interview protocol matrix to map the interview questions against the research questions."
- *Construct:* Translate theoretical/technical language into everyday interviewee language (Phase 2, p. 813, citing Brinkmann & Kvale, 2015).
- *Adapted intro question* (from Table 3, p. 823 original: "Based on the information that you provided in the questionnaire, you went to high school at ___. Did you grow up in ___?"): adaptable to "Based on your survey responses, your department operates primarily on [BI Platform X]. Is that correct?"

**Implementation & Change Management**
- *Construct:* Avoid "why" framing (defensive); use Rubin & Rubin's (2012) alternatives — "what influenced, what caused, what contributed to, what shaped" (Phase 2, p. 822). Adapted: "What shaped the decision to use unapproved consumer GenAI tools rather than the approved BI environment?"
- *Actual question (Table 3, p. 823)*: "What makes you identify with that community?" → adaptable to probing alignment with an AI steering committee.

**Organizational Adaptation Dynamics**
- *Actual closing question (Table 3, p. 823)*: "Before we conclude this interview, is there something about your experience... that you think influences how you engage... that we have not yet had a chance to discuss?"
- *Actual advice question (Phase 2, p. 824)*: "If you could give advice to another first-generation college student to help them with their transition to college, what would that be?" → adaptable to BI directors' advice for peers planning a GenAI policy transition.

## 9. LIMITATIONS
- Scope: "IPR framework is most suitable for refining structured or semi-structured interviews... from a grounded theory perspective, piloting interview protocols/guides are unnecessary" (p. 811).
- Resource constraints: "Some researchers may not have the time, money, or access to participants to engage in a piloting phase" (Phase 4, p. 827).
- Pretesting is not a guarantee: "completing a pretest successfully is not a guarantee of the success of the formal data collection for the study" (quoting Hurst et al., 2015; Phase 3, p. 827).
- Protocol vs. researcher intuition: "The interview protocol is a research instrument, but in qualitative research, the most useful instrument is the researcher... [who] can listen carefully and adjust... in a way that his/her/zer protocol will never be able to do" (p. 828).
