## 3. WHAT

- **Ten problems**, Problem one (p.333) to Problem ten (p.343), including: citing without reading (Problem two, p.336); mash-ups of incompatible approaches (Problem three, pp.336–337); assuming TA is atheoretical (Problem four, pp.337–338); confusing codes and themes (Problem seven, p.340); confusing themes and topics (Problem eight, p.341); themes "emerging" (Problem nine, pp.342–343).
- **Evaluation tool:** 20 questions for reviewers and editors, introduced on p.344; Table 1 runs pp.345–346.

## 4. DEFINITION
Three cluster definitions, all *(Problem one, pp.333–334)*. Bullets are quoted in full from the PDF.
- **Coding reliability:** "'Coding reliability' TA captures neopositivist approaches that have at their core concerns about 'objective' and 'unbiased' coding" — see PDF p.333 for the full bullet (deductive orientation; inter-rater reliability as the key quality measure).
- **Codebook:** "'Codebook' TA captures a cluster of methods that broadly sit within a qualitative paradigm (albeit with some pragmatic compromises). They use some kind of structured coding framework for developing and documenting the analysis, but consensus between coders and inter-rater reliability are not usually measures of quality. Themes are typically initially developed early on, as they are with coding reliability, but in some methods can be refined or new themes can be developed through inductive data engagement and the analytic process." *(p.333)*
- **Reflexive:** "'Reflexive' TA captures approaches that fully embrace qualitative research values and the subjective skills the researcher brings to the process – a research team is not required or even desirable for quality. Analysis, which can be more inductive or more theoretical/deductive, is a situated interpretative reflexive process. Coding is open and organic, with no use of any coding framework. Themes should be the final 'outcome' of data coding and iterative theme development." *(bullet starts p.333, continues p.334)*
- **Theme (reflexive TA):** "Our conceptualisation of themes as patterns of shared meaning, cohering around a central concept – the central idea or meaning the theme captures – is not universal, for instance." *(p.331)* And: "Themes in reflexive TA are patterns of shared meaning, united by a central concept or idea (Braun and Clarke 2013; Braun et al. 2014)." *(p.341)*

## 5. CITABLE
This is a methods paper; it does not address generative-AI policy or organisations.
- "Demonstrating coding reliability and the avoidance of 'bias' is illogical, incoherent and ultimately meaningless in a qualitative paradigm and in reflexive TA, because meaning and knowledge are understood as situated and contextual, and researcher subjectivity is conceptualised as a resource for knowledge production, which inevitably sculpts the knowledge produced, rather than a must-be-contained threat to credibility." *(pp.334–335)*
- "For themes to be patterns of shared meaning underpinned by a central concept, they must be analytic outputs, not inputs." *(p.342)*
- "Methodological mash-ups should be warranted, justified and theoretically coherent, and based in a full understanding of what (reflexive) TA can – and cannot – offer." *(Problem three take-away, p.337)*
- "We encourage TA researchers to clearly demarcate which TA approach they are using. Furthermore, if they cite authors from different orientations to TA, to clearly specify what they are 'taking' from each and justify (well) any use of divergent criteria and practice." *(Problem one take-away, p.335)*
- **Do not cite** "An account of themes 'emerging' or being 'discovered'…" as Braun & Clarke (2021)'s own words. It is their 2006 sentence, quoted in M2 with "(p. 80)"; the page in M2 is **p.343**. Cite it as Braun & Clarke (2006, p.80), quoted in Braun & Clarke (2021, p.343), or take it from M1.

## 13. THESIS ANCHOR (role: Chapter 3 method anchor; focus: how Braun & Clarke distinguish coding-reliability, codebook and reflexive TA; what they say researchers get wrong when claiming reflexive TA; codebooks, consensus/inter-rater reliability, saturation, subjectivity)
- **(a) Definitions:** see §4.
- **(b) Main claims:**
  - TA is not one approach; the three families differ in philosophy and procedure (pp.333–335).
  - Reflexive TA treats subjectivity as a resource; reliability and "bias" demands do not fit it (pp.334–335, quoted in §5).
  - "The application of such 'coding reliability' criteria to reflexive TA also, to us, suggests that the researcher does not fully 'get' the fundamentals of reflexive TA, does not understand what the qualitative values underpinning the framework expect or delimit." *(p.335)*
  - Codebook approaches "share early theme development (of some or all themes) and the use of a structured codebook or coding frame (the framework, template or matrix) with coding reliability approaches, and the qualitative philosophy and values of reflexive TA, such as recognising researcher subjectivity and that knowledge is contextual." *(p.335)*
  - Deduction is allowed inside reflexive TA: "It can be used for a more deductive or more inductive analytic process (recognising this can be a continuum, rather than dichotomy)." *(p.331)* "Using reflexive TA deductively means existing research and theory provide the lens through which we analyse and interpret data." *(p.331)* But: "coding is not – in general – a process for finding evidence for pre-conceptualised themes." *(p.332)*
  - "Indeed, if themes are developed prior to any analysis and coding, it is unlikely they can be much more than topic summaries." *(p.342)*
  - Themes do not "emerge"; that is Braun & Clarke (2006, p.80) quoted on p.343.
- **What researchers get wrong (verbatim):**
  - "Numerous authors claim to have 'followed the procedures outlined by Braun and Clarke (2006),' then describe procedures with little or no resemblance to those we outlined." *(Problem two, p.336)* "Reading such papers, we have discovered that we promote the use of codebooks and coding frames, consensus coding, the measurement of coding reliability, developing themes before data coding, data or theme saturation, the measurement and reporting of theme frequency, constant comparative analysis, and more . . . Reader, we do not!" *(p.336)* Take-away: "do not cite us (or indeed any other TA author) as the method used for TA without: a) reading the methodological guidelines provided; b) confirming that what you did is what is advocated; and c) being clear on why any 'deviations' from the broad approach were adopted." *(p.336)*
  - "For example, the combination of reflexive TA with the use of codebooks and coding reliability measures is common, but the tensions between the organic and subjective coding processes of reflexive TA and concerns for coding accuracy and reliability seem to be far less commonly recognised – certainly they are not often discussed." *(Problem three, p.336)*
  - Confusing themes and topics: "This is probably one of the most common problems in published TA research." *(p.341)* On a paper that used TA "according to Braun & Clarke" together with codebooks and consensus coding: "This type of analysis seems better suited to a codebook or coding reliability TA, in which themes can be predetermined prior to analysis and themes may consist of summaries of data domains." *(p.341)*
  - "Confused q": "research that seems to unknowingly, unreflexively and incoherently combine elements of qualitative positivism with the values and assumptions of a qualitative paradigm." *(p.329)*
- **(c) Mechanisms/conditions named:** see the passages above; the paper frames poor practice as following from assuming one TA (p.346) and from uncritical citing (p.336).
- **(d) Evidence base:** conceptual; see §2.
- **(e) Limitations:** see §9.
- **(f) What a careful examiner would expect** — verbatim from Table 1 (all eight on **p.345**; Table 1 runs pp.345–346; the tool is introduced p.344). Items 9 and 12 are abridged with "…" as NotebookLM returned them; the full wording is on the page.
  1. "Do the authors clearly specify and justify which type of TA they are using?" (Item 2)
  2. "Is there a good 'fit' between the theoretical and conceptual underpinnings of the research and the specific type of TA (i.e. is there conceptual coherence)?" (Item 4)
  3. "Combining philosophically and procedurally incompatible approaches to TA without any acknowledgement or explanation." (Item 7)
  4. "Confusing summaries of data topics with thematic patterns of shared meaning, underpinned by a core concept." (Item 7)
  5. "Are the theoretical underpinnings of the use of TA clearly specified (e.g. ontological, epistemological assumptions, guiding theoretical framework(s)), even when using TA inductively…?" (Item 9)
  6. "Are the analytic procedures used clearly outlined, and described in terms of what the authors actually did, rather than generic procedures?" (Item 11)
  7. "Is there evidence of conceptual and procedural confusion? For example, reflexive TA (e.g. Braun and Clarke 2006) is the claimed approach but different procedures are outlined such as the use of a codebook or coding frame, multiple independent coders and consensus coding, inter-rater reliability measures…" (Item 12)
  8. "Are the reported themes topic summaries, rather than 'fully realised themes' – patterns of shared meaning underpinned by a central organising concept?" (Item 15) The fourth bullet of Item 15 adds: "Or, if the authors are claiming to use reflexive TA, would the manuscript benefit from claiming to use a different type of TA (e.g. coding reliability or codebook)?" *(p.345)*

## Analysis-label decision (S4) — applies to Chapter 3

**Judged against the definitions in §4** and the thesis's procedure in `research-design/interview_protocol_v0.98.md` §10 and `analysis/templates/codebook_v0.md`:

| Feature of the thesis procedure | What M2's definitions say | Points to |
|---|---|---|
| Structured codebook (`codebook_v0`), with definitions and include/exclude rules | Codebook TA "use[s] some kind of structured coding framework for developing and documenting the analysis" (p.333); reflexive TA has "no use of any coding framework" (p.334) | **Codebook** |
| A priori spine (STR/PRO/REL) plus seed codes, set before coding | Codebook approaches "share early theme development (of some or all themes) and the use of a structured codebook" (p.335) | **Codebook** |
| Inductive codes added as they emerge | "…in some methods can be refined or new themes can be developed through inductive data engagement" (p.333) | **Codebook** (fits) |
| One coder | "a research team is not required or even desirable" (p.333) applies to reflexive TA; a team is not required for codebook TA either | Neutral |
| Re-coding after an interval and supervisor spot-check | M2 does not address either. It says consensus and inter-rater reliability "are not usually measures of quality" in codebook TA (p.333) and that reliability criteria do not fit reflexive TA (pp.334–335) | Neutral, but the check must not be described as a reliability measure |
| Rule that no a priori code may be the answer to the RQ | Compatible with codebook TA | Codebook |

**Label for Chapter 3:** codebook thematic analysis.
**Suggested sentence:** "Interview data were analysed with codebook thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2021): a structured codebook combining an a priori structural–procedural–relational spine with codes added inductively, applied by one researcher; consistency was checked by re-coding after an interval and a supervisor's review of a sample, not by inter-rater reliability measures."
**To claim reflexive TA instead**, the thesis would have to: drop the fixed codebook (keeping the spine as a sensitising lens only); treat themes as outputs of coding and never as inputs; never say themes "emerged"; drop the reliability framing of the re-coding and spot-check; and state its ontological and epistemological assumptions and the researcher's position (Table 1, Items 9–12, p.345).
Whichever label is used, Item 2, Item 11 and Item 12 (p.345) require it to be specified, justified, and described as what was actually done. Note that `codebook_v0.md` already carries a flag saying the label must be decided at S4 and reported honestly.
