## 1. WHY
GenAI tools (e.g., ChatGPT) are rapidly transforming higher-ed workplaces; universities need to understand faculty/student adoption "in order to assess how it can be harnessed effectively in support of teaching, learning, and research," and to determine "what kinds of support and policies should be put in place moving forward" (Pre-Interview Introduction, p. 1).

## 2. HOW
This is a **data-collection instrument itself** (a semi-structured interview guide), not a completed study — a multi-institutional qualitative research effort between East Carolina University and Ithaka S+R (Pre-Interview Introduction, p. 1). Interviews recorded, anonymized transcripts shared with Ithaka S+R to compile national findings/recommendations; each interview ~"just under an hour" (p. 1; Conclusion, p. 2). No sample size, database, or time span specified in the guide itself.

## 3. WHAT
No empirical findings (it's an instrument, not a report). It is structured to probe: familiarity with GenAI, teaching/learning integration, research workflows, ethical implications, training/support needs, and university responses (throughout, pp. 1–2).

## 4. DEFINITION
No organizational governance construct defined. Core technology definition:
> "Generative AI refers to technologies that can create original content such as text, code, and images based on patterns identified in training datasets." (Pre-Interview Introduction, p. 1)

## 7. INTERVIEW VALUE
This is the **richest source in the cluster for directly reusable, verbatim interview questions** — it is itself an actual instrument, not a report about one.

**Policy Framework Development**
- Q1: "How would you describe your level of familiarity and expertise with AI in general and with generative AI tools specifically?" (Introduction, p. 1) — establishes baseline literacy before drafting policy.
- Q3 (Research section): "How is your field navigating the ethical implications of the technology? Are there any resources that you have found to be especially helpful within your discipline to navigate this issue?" (p. 2) — maps to BI data-privacy/bias governance.
- Conclusion Q1: "What has the university done (that you are aware of) in response to the rise of generative AI technologies? » Are you satisfied with that response? What do you think the university could do to better support instructors and researchers moving forward?" (p. 2) — directly gauges perceived helpfulness of corporate guardrails.

**Implementation & Change Management**
- Teaching & Learning Q2: "Have you tried to incorporate generative AI tools into your instructional practices? ... If yes, can you give me specific examples of how you've done so? Do you think your attempts were successful or not? Why?" (p. 1) — adapt to BI workflow integration (automated SQL, report summarizing).
- Teaching & Learning Q4: "What is the biggest challenge you've experienced when trying to integrate generative AI into your teaching?" (p. 1) — pinpoints friction (data siloing, hallucinations, trust).

**Organizational Adaptation Dynamics**
- Introduction Q2: "In general, how have researchers in your field reacted to the advent of generative AI?" (p. 1) — captures peer resistance/enthusiasm or shadow-AI emergence.
- Support Needs Q2: "Looking toward the future and considering evolving trends in your field, what types of training or support will be most beneficial to researchers and/or teachers in your field?" (p. 2) — informs L&D strategy for upskilling analysts.
