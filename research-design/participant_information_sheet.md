## What is this study about?

I am studying how organizations develop and change their internal rules for using generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude, or built-in AI features of analytics platforms) in reporting, analytics and business intelligence work.

Most existing research looks at published frameworks and government policy. Very little looks at how such rules actually come about inside a company, how they reach the people doing the work, and how they change over time. That is what I would like to ask you about.

## Why have I been invited?

Because you work with, decide about, or set rules for generative AI tools in a reporting or analytics context. I am speaking with people at different levels — from those who set organizational policy to those who use the tools day to day — because their views often differ, and that difference is part of what I am studying.

## What would taking part involve?

One interview, about 60-75 minutes, held remotely at a time that suits you. It is a conversation, not a questionnaire. I will ask about your role, about one or two concrete situations you have experienced, and about how the rules in your organization came about and changed.

With your permission the interview will be audio-recorded so that I can transcribe it accurately. If you prefer not to be recorded, I will take written notes instead.

## Do I have to take part?

No. Participation is entirely voluntary. You may:

- decline to answer any question, for any reason or none;
- ask me to pause or stop the recording at any point;
- ask that something you said be excluded, during the interview or afterwards;
- withdraw entirely up to [DATE — suggest 4 weeks after your interview], after which your data will have been anonymised and merged into the analysis and can no longer be separated out.

There is no consequence of any kind to declining or withdrawing, and I will not report to your employer who did or did not take part.

## In what capacity am I taking part?

In a personal capacity — as yourself, not as a representative of your employer. I am not asking you to speak for your organization, and I will not present anything you say as an organizational position.

Please do not share anything confidential or covered by an agreement with your employer. If a question touches on something you cannot discuss, simply say so or skip it; I will not ask why, and it will not affect the interview.

## Will what I say be confidential?

Yes, with the following specifics:

- **You will not be named.** You will be referred to by role type and tier only (for example, "an analytics team lead at a mid-size services firm").
- **Your organization will not be named.** It will be described by sector and approximate size only.
- **Where colleagues from the same organization take part**, findings are reported by organizational level without a persistent pseudonym, so that separate statements cannot be assembled into a profile or linked to a specific reporting relationship. This matters most where several people from one team are interviewed.
- **I will check for indirect identification.** In a small market, a detailed description can identify an organization even without its name. Before anything is quoted, I will generalise details that could identify you or your employer.
- **Recordings and transcripts are stored** on [ENCRYPTED AALTO STORAGE — SPECIFY], accessible only to me. My supervisor may see anonymised excerpts.
- **Recordings are deleted** after transcription and checking, at the latest by [DATE]. Anonymised transcripts are retained until [DATE] for thesis examination.

I cannot promise absolute anonymity if you tell a story that only one person could have told. If you are about to say something you would not want traceable, say so and I will either not record it or exclude it.

## What about my organization's documents?

In some interviews I may ask whether internal policy documents can be looked at. This is optional and separate from taking part in the interview, and the consent form asks about it separately. There are two different levels, and you may grant one, both, or neither:

1. **Access for reference** — I may read or view the document during our conversation to help us both be accurate about dates and wording. Nothing from it is copied, quoted, or reproduced in the thesis. The thesis would only note that documents were consulted.
2. **Permission to quote** — I may quote short passages in the thesis, anonymised.

Most participants grant (1) and not (2), and that is entirely sufficient for my purposes.

## Are there any risks?

The main risk is that something you say about your employer's practices could be traced back to you. The protections above are designed to prevent this. I do not ask about anything sensitive under data-protection law — no health, political, religious, ethnic, or similar information — and I am not investigating compliance or misconduct.

## What are the benefits?

There is no payment. Participants who wish it will receive a short summary of the findings, and, if you would like, I will send you a summary of what I understood from our conversation so you can correct anything I got wrong.
