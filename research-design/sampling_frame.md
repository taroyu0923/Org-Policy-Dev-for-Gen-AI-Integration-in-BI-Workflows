# Sampling Frame and Case Structure

**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** Sep 2, 2026 | Confirmed from Albert's participant list.

## 1. Design

Embedded multiple-case. Two strata, analysed separately and never pooled.

- **Depth stratum** — multiple informants across tiers within one organization → within-case triangulation, mechanism.
- **Breadth stratum** — one informant per organization → cross-case variation.

**n = 14 participants across 9 organizations in 6 jurisdictions.**

## 3. What the frame supports — and what it does not

**No strategic tier.** The most senior informants (Lead OPS BI & Payment; Engineering Manager) are senior tactical. **No participant authored a GenAI policy.**

Decision (Sep 2): **reframe rather than re-recruit.** The study becomes an account of how organizational GenAI policy is *encountered, interpreted and adapted at the analytical working tier*, and whether adaptation feeds back upward. This is defensible because the entire A+B corpus theorises the tier hierarchy from the top down out of published documents — Batool's five levels (`batool2024aigov` §4.2 p.14), Joshi's strategic/tactical/operational (`joshi2025resai` p.2), Lee's L1/L2/L3 (`lee2024questionbank` §4.1 p.7–8), NTT DATA's three tiers (`ismail2025frameworks` §4.3 p.129) — and none observes the bottom of it empirically. This sample *is* the bottom, in depth, across nine organizations.

Consequence: Phase 3 upward-feedback questions (E2, E3) move from a section to a central finding. Bidirectional governance is asserted by `joshi2025resai` p.6–7, `taeihagh2025govgenai` p.6 and `weinberg2025faigmoe` §3.3 p.8; whether the upward channel exists at all is testable here.

**Opportunistic upside:** protocol question G2 asked of participant #4 (Lead, OPS BI & Payment) is the realistic route to one policy owner. A single policy author at Shopee would complete Depth A's vertical and is worth more than three additional breadth interviews.

**Second, unplanned contribution — jurisdictional variation.** Smartly, Nordea, Twipe fall under the EU AI Act; JPMorgan and Nordea add regulated-finance exposure; Taiwan has no comparable binding instrument. `luna2024paradigms` compares regimes across six regions at the *document* level (p.6) and `ismail2025frameworks` compares EU AI Act against ASEAN's voluntary approach (§4.1 p.127–128). Whether that divergence is visible in daily analytical practice is unstudied and answerable here.

**Sector composition — state honestly as convenience with purposive stratification.** Digital platforms dominate (Shopee, Delivery Hero, Roku, Amazon, Smartly, Twipe); manufacturing (Toyota) and regulated finance (JPMorgan, Nordea) provide the contrast. This is a professional-network sample, not a purposive sample drawn from a frame. Say so in Chapter 3 and defend it on variation in regulatory exposure and organizational type, not on representativeness.

## 4. Inclusion criterion (must be stated in Chapter 3)

> A participant is eligible if they **produce or maintain analytical outputs that other people use to make decisions**, or manage those who do.

This admits data engineers (they maintain the pipelines the outputs depend on), planning and business-operations roles (they produce analysis consumed by decision-makers), and excludes general software or product roles with no analytical output. Without a stated criterion an examiner will ask why a Data Engineer counts in a BI study.

⚠ **Confirm participant #13 (Amazon, Business Operations)** against this criterion and assign a tier before scheduling.

## 5. Sequencing (capacity-driven)

14 interviews ≈ 17 hours of audio plus transcription, with 6 requiring translation, in Sep–Oct alongside Chapter 2 drafting. Priority order if capacity bites:

1. **Pilot:** #5 or #6 (Toyota / Delivery Hero senior analyst) — a Chinese-speaking breadth participant. Piloting here locks the Chinese wording *before* the depth case and does not burn a Depth A informant.
2. **Depth A (Shopee, #1–4)** — the analytic core. Run early while the schedule is intact.
3. **Depth B (Smartly, #7–9).**
4. **Breadth (#10–14)** as capacity allows.

Dropping two or three breadth singletons costs some variation. Dropping a depth case costs the design. Never trade in the other direction.

## 6. Confidentiality — the risk is inside Shopee, not across the sample

Four Shopee informants work together and know each other. Standard pseudonymisation is insufficient: statements attributed to a consistent pseudonym across sections can be assembled into an identifiable profile, and colleagues can identify each other from role plus content.

**Rule for Depth A reporting:** report by tier without a persistent per-person pseudonym; do not place a tier-adjacent pair of quotes in a way that reconstructs a specific reporting relationship; generalise team names, product names and dates. State this rule in the information sheet — these four participants will care about it.

## 7. Employer permission — do NOT approach gatekeepers for most of this sample

These are personal contacts speaking as individuals, several under NDA (JPMorgan, Nordea, Amazon among the likeliest). Approaching employers would jeopardise both the interview and the participant.

- **Recruitment template B (gatekeeper) is not the default route** for this sample. Use template A (individual) throughout, and template B only where a participant themselves suggests organizational sign-off is needed.
- The consent form must carry an explicit **personal-capacity clause**: the participant speaks for themselves, not their employer; is asked not to disclose confidential or contractually protected information; and should skip any question covered by an agreement.
- **Document access is realistically limited to Shopee**, and possibly only to participants #3 and #4. Do not plan the analysis on the assumption of more.

## 8. Analytic axes available in this frame

| Axis | Contrast | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| Tier | Operational vs. tactical within Depth A and B | Batool / Joshi / Lee / NTT DATA convergence |
| Jurisdiction | EU AI Act (FI, BE) vs. US vs. TW vs. JP | `luna2024paradigms` p.6; `ismail2025frameworks` §4.1 |
| Regulatory sector | Finance (JPM, Nordea) vs. platform vs. manufacturing | `ozman2025platforms` §4.3 p.82 (sector bureaucratic rigidity) |
| Tenure | 1–2 yr (arrived into the policy) vs. >10 yr (remember before) | recall resource; see below |
| Evidence tier | Document-anchored (Shopee) vs. self-report | recall-validation strategy |

**Tenure note:** participants #7, #12, #14 (1–2 yr) joined after GenAI adoption and may have no memory of policy evolution. Do not press Section E with them; use them instead for the *sedimented state* — what they found already in place on arrival, and how they learned it. That is its own finding about how governance is transmitted to newcomers.
