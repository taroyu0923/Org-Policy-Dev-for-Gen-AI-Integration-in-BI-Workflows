# Fixed-Wording Card — EN / 繁體中文（台灣）

**Version:** 1.0 | Sep 2, 2026
**Purpose:** the protocol is delivered in English and interpreted live for the six Chinese-speaking participants (#1–6). These sentences are the exception: they are **read as written**, in the language of the interview, and must not be improvised. They carry the core section's comparability.

**Scope:** study purpose statement, the incident menu, the four process-trace questions, the confidence probes. Everything else may be rendered live.

**The Chinese column is a draft — rewrite it.** Albert is a native Taiwanese Mandarin speaker and will deliver these questions himself; natural phrasing matters more than fidelity to the English. If a sentence sounds stiff, or is not how an analyst at these companies would actually talk, change it. Pay particular attention to item (ii): an over-literal rendering turns a neutral description of ordinary work into an accusation.

**Verification (chosen route, Sep 2): pilot-based, not back-translation.** Independent back-translation is a convention from cross-cultural *survey* research, where an instrument is administered by people other than its author. Here there is one bilingual researcher and semi-structured interviews, so the live risk is not mistranslation but **wording drift between the six Chinese interviews** — which fixing the wording at all resolves, regardless of who wrote it. Verification therefore runs as:

1. **Pilot in Chinese** with participant #5 or #6 (a breadth participant, so no Depth A informant is spent). Record any question that had to be rephrased on the spot, and amend this card. This is empirical evidence of comprehensibility, and stronger than a notional equivalence check.
2. **Optional — one bilingual reader** (a colleague or classmate, never a participant) skims for naturalness. ~20 minutes.
3. Full independent back-translation only if the supervisor requests it.

Log what was actually done, and by whom, for Chapter 3.

## 1. Purpose statement (opening)

**EN** — "I'm studying how organizations actually develop and change their rules for using generative AI in reporting and analytics work — not what the policy says, but how it got there and how it has changed."

**ZH** — 「我在研究組織實際上是怎麼制定、以及後來怎麼調整生成式 AI 在報表與數據分析工作上的使用規範。重點不是規範寫了什麼，而是它是怎麼來的、後來又是怎麼變的。」

## 2. Neutrality framing (opening, before recording)

**EN** — "There are no right answers, and I'm not evaluating your organization's compliance. I'm interested in how things actually work."

**ZH** — 「這沒有標準答案，我也不是要評估你們公司有沒有合規。我想了解的是實際上事情是怎麼運作的。」

## 3. Personal-capacity reminder (opening, on record)

**EN** — "You're speaking for yourself, not for your employer. Please don't share anything confidential or covered by an agreement — just skip anything like that, and I won't ask why."

**ZH** — 「這次訪談你是以個人身分分享，不代表公司立場。如果有任何涉及機密或保密協議的內容，請直接跳過就好，我不會追問。」

---

## 4. Incident menu — introduction

**EN** — "I'd like to spend most of our time on one concrete situation rather than on the policy in the abstract. Three kinds of situations tend to come up — could you pick whichever you have a real example of?"

**ZH** — 「接下來我想把大部分的時間放在一個具體的情境上，而不是抽象地談政策。以下這三種情況比較常見，你可以挑一個你真的遇過的例子跟我說。」

## 5. Incident menu — the three types

**(i) A refused or restricted use**

**EN** — "Someone wanted to use a generative AI tool for something in a report or analysis, and it wasn't allowed, or it had to be done differently."

**ZH** — 「有人想把生成式 AI 用在某份報表或某個分析上，但不被允許，或是必須換一種做法。」

**(ii) An output that couldn't be used as it was**

**EN** — "A figure, a summary, a query, or a piece of commentary that turned out to be wrong, unverifiable, or needed rework before it could go into a deliverable."

**ZH** — 「某個產出——一個數字、一段摘要、一段 query，或一段說明文字——沒辦法直接使用，需要重做或再確認過，才能放進要交出去的成果裡。」

**(iii) A use that got approved**

**EN** — "A tool or a way of working that went from 'someone was trying it' to 'this is how we do it.'"

**ZH** — 「某個工具或某種做法，從『有人在試試看』變成『我們現在都這樣做』。」

> ⚠ **Wording discipline — both languages.** Never say hallucination / violation / breach / mistake / error. 中文請避免：幻覺、違規、出錯、犯錯、失誤。Item (ii) asks the participant to describe ordinary work, not to confess. If a participant uses those words themselves, you may follow their language.

---

## 6. The four process-trace questions

Asked of whichever incident type the participant chose, in this order.

**B1 — What happened?**

**EN** — "Can you walk me through it from the beginning?"
*Probes:* which report, dashboard or dataset; who was involved; what the deadline pressure was like.

**ZH** — 「可以從頭跟我說一次當時發生什麼事嗎？」
*追問：* 是哪一份報表／dashboard／哪一份資料？當時有誰參與？當時的時程壓力大概是什麼樣子？

**B2 — What rule existed at the time?**

**EN** — "What rule existed at the time? Was there anything written down? Did people know about it? How did you find out what was allowed?"

**ZH** — 「當時有什麼規定嗎？有沒有寫下來的東西？大家知道嗎？你當時是怎麼知道什麼可以做、什麼不行的？」

**B3 — What changed afterwards?**

**EN** — "Did anything change afterwards? Did the way you work change? Was anything written down, announced, or built into a tool? Or did it just settle?"

**ZH** — 「後來有什麼改變嗎？你的工作方式有跟著變嗎？有沒有寫成文件、公告出來，或是直接改在工具裡？還是就這樣慢慢定下來了？」

**B4 — Who decided?**

**EN** — "Who made the call? Who else had to agree? Did it go anywhere above or below your level?"

**ZH** — 「這個決定是誰做的？還有誰需要同意？有沒有往上或往下傳到其他層級？」

---

## 7. Confidence probes

Asked lightly after B3 and B4. **The answer is data** — record it as `high / approximate / cannot say`, and do not press for precision.

**EN** — "Roughly when was that — a month, a quarter, or is it hard to pin down?"

**ZH** — 「那大概是什麼時候——哪個月、哪一季，還是其實不太好說？」

**EN** — "Is that written down somewhere, or is it more that people just know it?"

**ZH** — 「這件事有寫在哪裡嗎？還是比較像大家心裡都有數？」

---

## 8. Closing — the referral ask (asked of #3 and #4 especially)

**EN** — "Is there anyone here whose view on this would be different from yours?"

**ZH** — 「在你們公司裡，有沒有誰對這件事的看法會跟你不太一樣？」

**EN** — "Would you be willing to look at a short summary of what I understood, to check I've got it right?"

**ZH** — 「之後我整理一份簡短的摘要，寫下我理解到的內容，可以請你幫我看一下有沒有誤解嗎？」
