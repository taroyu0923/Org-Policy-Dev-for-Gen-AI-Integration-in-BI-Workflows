### K5 split (Sep 29, Albert's expectation — hypothesis)

Most early use Albert knows of happened **before any rule existed**; a few cases **after** a rule was published. Two phenomena, two claims:

| | Phenomenon | Literature claim it tests |
|---|---|---|
| K5a | Use after a rule was published, around it | **K5** over-governance → bypass (Joshi, Tallon, Abraham) — never observed in the corpus |

Presc. If practice came first, the rule was *encountered by people already using the tool*, which bears directly on the main RQ |

## The pre-rule period — what finding a survey cannot produce (Sep 29)

Albert: "internal contributors' behaviour when using AI without any regulation" (tentative).
Claude's view — sharpen from a topic into findings that need a within-person sequence (before rule → rule arrives → after), which a cross-sectional survey like B9 cannot reconstruct:

| Candidate finding | What it would show | Claim it speaks to |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 Self-made rules before official ones** | Practitioners set their own limits before any policy (e.g. no customer data in prompts, re-check every figure); the later rule codified, ignored or contradicted them | K11 governance-first; K2 formal channels |
| **P2 Bypass as continued habit, not reaction** ★ | Use "around" a rule is the pre-rule practice carrying on, not a response to over-control | **Challenges the causal story in K5** (Tallon, Abraham, Joshi: over-governance → bypass). Path dependence instead of over-control |

## Contribution (2) restated — how policy reaches practitioners (Sep 29)

Albert (Shopee, expectation — hypothesis): a refused or modified request first teaches the analyst **only the decision on their own request**; separately, **all data-related workers receive the whole rule document**. Whether it governs what they do depends on whether they read it thoroughly or skim it.

Two channels in parallel: **the document** (distributed to everyone) and **the ruling** (case-specific, arrives through local BI). B9's awareness item (4.4/7) would score both the same; it cannot tell which one a person acts on.

Distinction to carry into Chapter 2 and the coding: **delivered → read → used in the decision.** K2 (formal channels carry policy) may be confirmed at "delivered" and fail at "used".
Candidate finding (restated contribution 2): *even where the full policy is distributed, practitioners' working rule is learned from rulings on their own requests.* Speaks to K2, K10 (sedimentation: rulings → "we just know now") and D4 Nahar (templates skimmed or skipped).
Coding attribute (no new interview question): **source of the working rule** in each incident — document / ruling / colleague / tool block / own judgement.

## Contributions (2) and (5) as one loop (Sep 29)

Albert: same loop seen from two ends. When a case is unclear, workers may check the rule or ask — or just go ahead, especially when workload / deadline is pressing.

```
unclear case ──► FORK ──► ask / check ──► ruling (local BI) ──► working rule ("we just know now") ──► goes up? (SQ2)
                   │
                   └────► act now (deadline) ──► own judgement ──► habit? (P2 / K5) ──► nothing travels up
```

- Fork variable (Albert's expectation): workload / deadline pressure. Corpus support: D4 Nahar (RAI treated as low-risk "compared to client deadlines", §3.2 p.8); D7 Rakova (defer to product/timeline pressure, §4.4); K6. Protocol already probes it: B1 (deadline pressure), D3 (gap under deadline).
- Joins SQ1 and SQ2: the ask branch is where rulings — and any upward travel — start; the act-now branch leaves no trace upward.
