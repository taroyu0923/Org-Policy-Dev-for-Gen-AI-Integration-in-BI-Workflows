## What 2.5 argues (outline v2)

> Feedback loops are prescribed widely and observed rarely; champions drive upward change where it happens; non-champions are unstudied. Voice and silence (efficacy, safety) and issue selling explain why concerns do or do not travel.

Claims: **K7** (governance is bidirectional; feedback loops exist or should — prescribed) and **K8** (upward change is driven by champions — D7 observed). SQ2 is the test: *do BI practitioners' requests or concerns travel upward to whoever decides the rules, and what comes back?* A null is a finding (Sep 11 log D3; claims map, Albert's pick on K7), so the section must make **both answers informative**.

## Argument rules

- **Synthesise by claim, not by paper.** Each paragraph opens with a claim about the literature.
- **Keep Presc./Obs. visible** in every paragraph: *prescribed* (B4, B5, B8, B1, E2), *observed at system or project level* (C10, B6), *observed absence* (D4, D5, B9), *observed upward change by champions* (D7), *conceptual* (G2, G3).
- **Heading as a question:** "Does anything go up?" (outline working heading; keep unless Albert changes it).
- **Null is a finding.** Frame SQ2 so that "nothing travels" and "something travels" both answer a question the literature left open. Do not suggest the data will show either.
- **No interview data, no hypotheses as findings.** The ask/act-now fork, "rulings not the document", and any expectation about who screens requests appear only as open questions, or not at all.
- **Theory is not evidence about AI governance.** G2 and G3 explain mechanisms in general organisations; say once that neither concerns AI rules, then use them as lenses.
- **No interview content ever enters this session** (privacy notice §7). If Albert pastes any, stop and remind him.
