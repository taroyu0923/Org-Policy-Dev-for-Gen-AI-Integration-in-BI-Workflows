## §11 re-read against the PDF (working-tier reception)

What the paper says about people below the rule-setting level, all of it prescriptive and drawn from literature (no fieldwork of its own):

- (a) **How policy reaches staff.** Employees are listed as stakeholders "expected to adhere" (p. 173:7). A code of ethics (p. 173:10–11) and ethics training (p. 173:12) carry the rules. The ethics committee gives feedback to project teams on proposals (p. 173:10).
- (b) **Gaps.** A code of ethics depends on individuals "with limited monitoring and enforcement" (p. 173:11). Training covers "only … a subset" of knowledge (p. 173:12). Both are prescriptive drawbacks, not observed gaps.
- (c) **Attitudes:** none reported.
- (d) **Upward channels:** none. Stakeholder engagement (§3.3.4, pp. 173:13–14) concerns stakeholders of an AI system (interviews, workshops, crowdsourcing), not staff influencing governance.
- (e) **Silence or edge cases:** none.

**Corrected verdict: documented absence.** The paper is prescriptive only and gives no account of how staff receive governance. The earlier "substantive but secondary" rested on the invented champion pattern and checkpoint content.
