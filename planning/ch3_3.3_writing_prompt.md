## 5. Paragraph plan (approved)

1. **Overview** (~80 words). One interview per participant, 45–60 min, Teams, English or Taiwanese Mandarin; the section order; documents only in a separate optional call. Markers: `[PENDING: interviews]`, `[FILL: interview period]`.
2. **Protocol development: the four IPR phases** (~170). Castillo-Montoya pp. 812, 824, 827. Phase 1 → a map from each research question to the interview sections that answer it. Phase 2 → plain, conversational wording that avoids judgemental terms (e.g. "violation", "mistake"). Phase 3 → my supervisor's reading of the design. Phase 4 → the October 2026 pilot (facts in §3). Then: v1.0 cut six questions and moved the document walkthrough; the pilot was not retained as data. Name the changed elements briefly, not by question code.
3. **Critical-incident anchor** (~150). Gremler pp. 66, 76; Butterfield pp. 478, 480–481. The participant chose one incident from a bounded menu of three types — a refused or restricted use; an output that could not be used as it was; a use that got approved — stated as the inclusion criteria. Each incident was traced with the same four fixed questions (what happened; what rule existed; what changed afterwards; who decided), a probe on whether a one-off decision had become a standing rule, and a probe on what came back when something was passed up. One incident by default. Accounts are retrospective, so probes asked for concrete detail, and the participant's confidence in dates was recorded as data rather than pressed.
4. **The later sections and conditional probes** (~120). C: how the rules first arrived; a probe on use before the rules and what stopped, changed or continued, asked only of those who used GenAI before the rules (others: what they found in place). D: technical controls; what happens to data before it goes into an AI tool, listening for whether the answer is rule-shaped or case-shaped (case attribute, 3.4). E: whether a route upward exists and has been used, and, where nothing went up, the participant's own reason; questions on how rules changed only for those there before the rules; tier-specific probes when time allows. Closing: anything missed; in depth cases, whether colleagues would see it differently; the summary offer (Section 3.5).
5. **Bilingual delivery** (~90). English master version; I conducted the Mandarin interviews myself. The purpose statement, the incident menu, the four trace questions, the escalation and confidence probes and three later probes were fixed in both languages and read as written; other probes were rendered live in the language of the interview. The Mandarin wording was checked through the pilot, not by back-translation or a second reader. Translation of quotations: Section 3.5.
6. **Documents, Depth A safeguards, after the interview** (~70). Document call: version numbers and dates only; nothing copied; quotation only with the separate permission (Section 3.6). Depth A safeguards (§3). Contact summary within 24 hours (Section 3.4). Close with one or two plain summary sentences.
