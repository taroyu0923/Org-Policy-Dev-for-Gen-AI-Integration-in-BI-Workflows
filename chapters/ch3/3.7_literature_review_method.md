The corpus was built in five passes, and each pass was recorded in a search log kept in the thesis repository. The first pass (S1, July 2026) was a web-search compilation rather than a systematic database search, and its record is partial [FILL: S1 databases and query strings, if reconstructed]. Second, a verification pass (S2) resolved every identifier against publisher records, excluded three sources and restricted two to supporting use. Third, a snowball pass (S3) ranked the works cited across the compiled notes by how many notes cited them, and 14 of 15 targets were kept. Finally, two targeted passes added the methods sources (S4) and six context and theory sources (S5). Only sources whose metadata had been checked against the PDF are cited, and preprints are cited only in a supporting role.

The count reported in Section 2.6 comes from one screening question put to every governance source. The question asked what the source says about how governance is experienced, interpreted, resisted, worked around or ignored by the people doing analytical and technical work, as distinct from how it is designed. Each source received one verdict: substantive, partial or documented absence. Only data, first- or second-hand, on how staff received AI governance counted. In contrast, naming the gap or citing another study did not count. Every verdict was checked against the PDF. Methods sources were kept out of the count, since they would have returned absences for reasons unrelated to governance. Finally, the count is a range because one study of information governance reports users' behaviour only through executives' accounts.

NotebookLM was used as a reading aid, answering structured queries on each PDF. Every quotation, page and metadata item was then checked against the PDF and corrected where wrong. For example, one source was excluded after its generated summary asserted metadata that the publisher record did not support. Claude (Anthropic) was used for planning, drafting support and verification. The use of AI tools on interview material is described separately, in Section 3.6.
