The corpus was built in five passes, each recorded in a search log kept in the thesis repository. The first (S1, July 2026) was a web-search compilation rather than a systematic database search, and its record is partial [FILL: S1 databases and query strings, if reconstructed]. A verification pass (S2) resolved every identifier against publisher records, excluded three sources and restricted two to supporting use. A snowball pass (S3) ranked the works cited across the compiled notes by how many notes cited them; 14 of 15 targets were kept. Two targeted passes added the methods sources (S4) and six context and theory sources (S5). Only sources whose metadata had been checked against the PDF are cited, and preprints only in a supporting role.

The count reported in Section 2.6 comes from one screening question put to every governance source: what it says about how governance is experienced, interpreted, resisted, worked around or ignored by the people doing analytical and technical work, as distinct from how it is designed. Each source received one verdict: substantive, partial or documented absence. Methods sources were kept out of the count, since they would have returned absences for reasons unrelated to governance. The count is a range because two conceptual audit papers could be coded either way.

NotebookLM was used as a reading aid, answering structured queries on each PDF. Every quotation, page and metadata item was then checked against the PDF and corrected where wrong; one source was excluded after its generated summary asserted metadata the publisher record did not support. Claude (Anthropic) was used for planning, drafting support and verification. No AI tool processed interview content [CHECK: Aalto / programme guidance on AI use in theses].
