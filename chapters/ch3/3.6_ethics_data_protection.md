This section sets out how the study met the requirements of research ethics and research integrity. It covers three points: whether ethical review was needed, how participants gave consent and how their data were protected, and how the integrity of the work was maintained, including the use of AI tools.

The study followed Aalto University's guidance on research ethics and research integrity, which rests on the Finnish code of conduct for research integrity drawn up by the Finnish National Board on Research Integrity (TENK) with the Finnish research community [@tenk2023ri]. The code states that its good research practices also apply to bachelor's and master's studies and theses [@tenk2023ri, p. 9]. At Aalto, the Research Ethics Committee reviews non-medical research with human participants on request. The study was assessed against Aalto's criteria for when such review is needed. Participation was voluntary, all participants were adults, no special categories of personal data were collected and there was no intervention of any kind. Therefore, none of the criteria applied. At a supervision meeting on 6 October 2026, my supervisor confirmed that this master's thesis required neither ethical review nor prior approval, and that interviews could begin once each participant had given informed consent.

Consent was obtained with my supervisor's consent template for interview studies, adapted to this study as one form that combined the information about the study with the consent itself [PENDING: form sent before each interview]. The form described the procedure, voluntary participation, confidentiality, the use of third-party and AI software, data retention, and risks and benefits. Participants consented either by signing the form or by replying to the email that sent it [PENDING: consent obtained]. As the student researcher, I was the controller of the personal data, and the legal basis for processing was consent. Furthermore, the form separated the conditions of taking part from six optional permissions, each answered yes or no: audio recording, anonymised quotation, viewing internal documents during the interview, quoting from those documents, checking a summary of the interview, and receiving a summary of the findings. Agreeing to a document being viewed did not imply agreeing to its quotation. In addition, a personal-capacity clause stated that participants spoke for themselves rather than for their employer and could skip any question covered by an agreement with it. Participants could withdraw up to four weeks after their interview. Employers were not approached.

Interviews were recorded and transcribed in Microsoft Teams under my Aalto account. Recordings, transcripts, consent forms and memos were stored on Aalto OneDrive, and the key linking participant numbers to names was kept apart from everything else [PENDING: fieldwork]. Only I had access to identifiable data, and my supervisor saw anonymised excerpts only. Recordings and identifiable transcripts were deleted when the thesis was completed [PENDING: deletion]. Anonymisation applied to both the person and the organisation. Organisations are described by sector and jurisdiction, and participants by tier. For example, Depth A, where several colleagues took part, is reported by tier without persistent pseudonyms, and quotations are not placed in ways that would reconstruct a reporting relationship. In addition, team names, product names and dates are generalised throughout.

The use of AI tools was disclosed to participants in the consent form, and my supervisor agreed to it on the condition that only de-identified material was processed. Recordings were transcribed in Teams under my Aalto account and were not passed to any other software. Once names and other identifying details, such as colleagues, the organisation, teams and products, had been removed from a transcript, the de-identified text could be processed with third-party software, including AI tools. These tools were used to check the transcription, to draft English translations of Mandarin passages and to support the analysis. The form also named the remaining risk that a third party could access data during processing. However, AI output was treated as a draft, not as a result. Every machine transcription or translation used in the thesis was checked against the recording, and every coding and interpretive decision was mine (Sections 3.4 and 3.5) [PENDING: AI use log kept during analysis].

Research integrity also covers the rest of the work. Every direct quotation and page number from the literature was checked against the source PDF, and AI tools used in the literature review are described in Section 3.7. Departures from the planned procedure, such as a smaller sample, are reported where they occur rather than smoothed over (Section 3.2). To sum up, the study needed no ethical review, rested on informed and documented consent, and used AI tools openly and under the researcher's control.
