The study was assessed against Aalto University's published criteria for ethical review. Participation was voluntary, all participants were adults, no special categories of personal data were collected and there was no intervention of any kind, so none of the criteria applied. My supervisor confirmed in writing that ethical review was not required [FILL: date of supervisor's written confirmation].

Each participant received an information sheet and a privacy notice before deciding whether to take part [PENDING: documents sent before each interview]. As the student researcher I was the controller of the personal data, and the legal basis for processing was consent [CHECK: privacy notice transferred to the Aalto template]. The consent form separated the conditions of taking part from four optional permissions: audio recording, anonymised quotation, viewing internal documents during the interview, and quoting from those documents. Agreeing to a document being viewed did not imply agreeing to its quotation. A personal-capacity clause stated that participants spoke for themselves rather than for their employer and could skip any question covered by an agreement with it. Participants could withdraw up to four weeks after their interview [PENDING: consent obtained]. Employers were not approached.

Interviews were recorded and transcribed in Microsoft Teams under my Aalto account. Recordings, transcripts, consent forms and memos were stored only on Aalto OneDrive, with the key linking participant numbers to names kept apart from everything else [PENDING: fieldwork]. Recordings were deleted once the transcripts had been checked [PENDING: deletion] [FILL: deletion and retention dates]. Apart from the transcription built into Teams, no AI tool processed interview content; AI tools were used only on anonymised material such as the codebook's definitions (Section 3.7 covers their use in the literature review).

Anonymisation applied to both the person and the organisation. Organisations are described by sector and jurisdiction, and participants by tier. Depth A, where four colleagues took part, is reported by tier without persistent pseudonyms, and quotations are not placed in ways that would reconstruct a reporting relationship. Team names, product names and dates are generalised throughout.
