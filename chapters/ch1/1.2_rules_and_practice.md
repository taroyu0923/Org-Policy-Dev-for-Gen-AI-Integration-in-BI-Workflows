Organisations have answered generative AI with rules, and the speed of its arrival explains much of the urgency. Open-source libraries, cloud computing and friendly user interfaces "have democratized access to generative AI" and sped its adoption across sectors [@taeihagh2025govgenai, p. 3]. A tool that anyone can open in a browser reaches the analyst before the organisation has decided anything about it. The risk at stake is largely a matter of data: users' familiarity with how a system works, or "their commitment to protecting their personal data and their organization's sensitive information, can affect the likelihood of them sharing sensitive information with the models" [@taeihagh2025govgenai, p. 4]. The organisational response takes the form of rules: a policy document, an approval step, a blocked website, a ruling on a single request. Section 2.1 sets out what this study means by a *rule*.

These rules are written for a moving target. @taeihagh2025govgenai [p. 2] judges the literature on governing AI to be "still significantly underdeveloped relative to the developments in the field". The review by @responsibleaigovreview [p. 7] adds that responsible AI principles "are in a continuous state of flux", because each new type of AI technology "comes into a new set of conditions that must be considered", and names ChatGPT as an example. Organisations are therefore writing rules while both the tool and the thinking about how to govern it are still changing.

A rule may also arrive after practice has begun. Use without approval is older than AI: @haag2017shadowit [p. 469] define shadow IT as "hardware, software, or services built, introduced, and/or used for the job without explicit approval or even knowledge of the organization". Its generative AI form appears in what executives report. Drawing on their interviews with executives, @silic2025shadowai [p. 11] write that "employees continue to use unauthorized AI tools despite formal restrictions". A rule, then, may land on use that is already under way. Whether such use began before any rule existed or continued around one is a distinction this study keeps open; Sections 2.2 and 2.3 take it up.
