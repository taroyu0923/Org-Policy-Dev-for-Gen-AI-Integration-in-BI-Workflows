## 1.4 Aim and research questions

The aim of this study is to understand, through practitioners' accounts of specific incidents, how organisations' rules for generative AI use shape analytical work, and whether anything practitioners raise travels back to those who set the rules.

The study asks one main question and two sub-questions:

- **Main question:** How do BI practitioners encounter, interpret and act on their organisation's rules for generative AI use?
- **SQ1:** What happens when they use, or try to use, GenAI in their analytical work, and how do the rules shape what they do next?
- **SQ2:** Do their requests or concerns travel upward to whoever decides the rules, and what comes back?

SQ1 follows the rule downward into the work, while SQ2 follows requests and concerns in the other direction. SQ2 does not assume that anything travels. In my view, a finding that nothing goes up, or that nothing comes back, answers it as fully as a finding that something does.

The study defines BI practitioners as people who produce, maintain or directly supervise analytical outputs that others use to make decisions, and who neither set their organisation's rules for generative AI use nor hold a formal responsible-AI or AI-governance role. The definition includes first-line managers of analytical work, because they receive the rules as their teams do. It excludes those who make the rules, since the study looks at governance from the receiving end. Section 3.2 explains each clause and how the definition was applied in recruitment. *Rule* is used in the broad sense set out in Section 2.1, which covers unwritten expectations as well as documents.

The questions do not presuppose that an organisation's rules changed while participants worked under them. Some participants may have joined after the rules were in place. Change over time is therefore examined only where the accounts allow it. In the depth case where change was expected, it is reported as a finding only if independent accounts meet a criterion fixed before the interviews (Section 3.4).
